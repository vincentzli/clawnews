# Etched Raises $700M for Transformer ASICs, Harvey Launches Tenet Legal LLM, and FDA Clears PrecivityAD2 Test

### **Etched Secures $700 Million Series B Led by Jane Street to Accelerate Sohu ASIC Production**
[Read More](news/The_21_Billion_Binary_Bet_Inside_Etched’s_Sohu,_Jane_Street’s_Low-Latency_Play,_and_the_ASIC_vs._GPU_Inference_War.md)
The hardware startup [Etched](https://etched.com) has raised $700 million in a Series B funding round led by Jane Street, valuation-doubling the company to $21 billion. This monumental round comes just weeks after a previous capital raise and brings the company's customer contracts to over $1 billion. Notably, Jane Street is serving as both the lead investor and a major production customer, already deploying Etched's server racks into their mathematical and trading workloads. The core debate surrounding Etched lies in the viability of Application-Specific Integrated Circuits (ASICs) compared to general-purpose GPUs like Nvidia's Blackwell series. Etched’s flagship chip, "Sohu," is hardwired exclusively to run Transformer models. By hardcoding the attention mechanism directly onto the silicon, Sohu delivers unprecedented throughput and latency reductions—reportedly up to 20 times faster than standard GPUs. Proponents argue that since Transformers dominate modern AI, specialized ASICs are the only way to scale inference cost-effectively. However, critics express concern over architecture lock-in: if the industry shifts toward alternative architectures like state-space models (SSMs) or liquid neural networks, hardcoded ASICs risk immediate obsolescence.

### **Harvey Unveils "Tenet" Legal Model and LAB Evaluation Suite Built on Kimi K3**
[Read More](news/The_Sovereign_Weights_of_Law_Harvey’s_Tenet_and_the_Post-API_Shift_in_Enterprise_GenAI.md)
Legal AI platform [Harvey AI](https://www.harvey.ai) has launched "Tenet," its first in-house proprietary large language model (LLM) designed specifically for complex legal tasks. Developed in partnership with Fireworks AI, Tenet is post-trained on the open-weight foundation model Kimi K3 from Chinese startup Moonshot AI. The model was trained using asynchronous reinforcement learning on a comprehensive corpus of public and expert legal datasets, with no customer data utilized in the training pipeline. Strategically, Harvey’s shift to a proprietary model aims to reduce its reliance on third-party APIs (such as OpenAI and Anthropic), providing enterprise clients with enhanced data privacy, lower inference costs, and model weight sovereignty. Alongside Tenet, Harvey open-sourced the Legal Agent Benchmark (LAB), an evaluation framework designed to test AI agents on long-horizon, multi-document legal matters like M&A due diligence and contract drafting. Unlike traditional benchmarks that test single-turn prompts, LAB measures multi-step reasoning against expert-curated rubrics. The release has sparked debate regarding the efficacy of domain-specific open-weight model fine-tuning versus closed-source giants, with independently tracked evaluations showing Kimi K3 and Tenet achieving state-of-the-art results on legal agent leaderboards.

### **Z.ai Releases GLM-5.3 Upgrade Boosting MoE Coding and Agentic Performance**
[Read More](news/The_743B_MoE_Agent_Frontier_Inside_Z.ai's_GLM-5.3,_Slime_Asynchronous_RL,_and_the_Geopolitics_of_Delayed_Weights.md)
AI developer [Z.ai](https://z.ai) has officially released GLM-5.3, a post-training upgrade to its 743-billion-parameter Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) base model. Currently accessible via API, the model's weights are slated for public release in late August following final safety reviews. GLM-5.3 focuses on coding and autonomous agentic workflows, achieving top performance marks on terminal-based benchmarks like Terminal-Bench 3.0 and CyberGym. The model demonstrated its capabilities during pre-release testing by identifying over 1,000 security vulnerabilities in widely used open-source repositories. This release has accelerated discussions on X and Reddit regarding the "agentic transition," as developers shift focus from general benchmarks like MMLU toward evaluating an AI’s capacity to navigate terminal environments, execute commands, and debug code. Industry analysts highlight that as closed-source leaders restrict model access, open-weight MoE releases from labs like Z.ai are becoming the primary drivers of developer-centric, localized AI agent architectures.

### **Gravis Robotics Secures Unicorn Status with $200 Million SoftBank-Led Series A for Heavy Machinery Autonomy**
[Read More](news/Physical_AI’s_Billion-Dollar_Concrete_Reality_Inside_Gravis_Robotics’_200M_SoftBank_Bet.md)
Zurich-based startup [Gravis Robotics](https://gravisrobotics.com) has raised $200 million in a Series A funding round led by SoftBank, propelling the company to a $1 billion valuation. Founded in 2022 out of ETH Zurich, Gravis specializes in retrofitting heavy construction machinery—such as excavators, loaders, and graders—with autonomous operating systems. Their core hardware product, the "Gravis Rack," is a modular rooftop unit equipped with LiDAR, ruggedized cameras, and edge computing units that automate grading, excavation, and site preparation. The massive funding round has re-ignited debates in the physical AI sector. Advocates point out that retrofitting existing commercial machinery is highly cost-effective and speeds up deployment compared to designing custom autonomous machinery from scratch. Conversely, safety experts raise concerns about the reliability of control loops and multi-sensor fusion when multi-ton machines operate autonomously in highly unstructured, chaotic, and muddy construction sites near human workers.

### **FDA Clears PrecivityAD2 Blood Test, Transforming Alzheimer's Clinical Diagnostics and Trial Triage**
[Read More](news/Mass_Spec_vs._Alzheimer’s_Inside_the_FDA’s_Clearance_of_C2N's_PrecivityAD2_and_the_Battle_to_Unclog_the_Neurological_Pipeline.md)
The FDA has granted clearance to the PrecivityAD2 blood test, developed by [C2N Diagnostics](https://c2n.com), representing a major diagnostic breakthrough for Alzheimer’s disease. Utilizing high-resolution mass spectrometry, the test measures specific ratios of amyloid-beta and tau proteins in blood plasma to calculate the Amyloid Probability Score (APS2). Clinical validation shows that PrecivityAD2 achieves diagnostic accuracy comparable to expensive PET scans and invasive cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) draws. This clearance is set to address a major bottleneck in Alzheimer's treatment: the approval of monoclonal antibody therapies like Leqembi and Kisunla has created massive waitlists for PET scans. By acting as a non-invasive frontline screening tool, PrecivityAD2 will streamline clinical triage and accelerate trial enrollment. While celebrated as a diagnostic milestone, debates on medical subreddits emphasize that broad insurance coverage is critical to ensure equitable access to these early-intervention diagnostics and subsequent therapies.

### **OpenAI Introduces "ChatGPT for Teens" with Enhanced Guardrails and Parental Controls**
[OpenAI](https://openai.com) has launched "ChatGPT for Teens," a specialized version of its conversational assistant tailored for users aged 13 to 17. The new service implements strict safety filters targeting sensitive content (such as self-harm, hate speech, and sexual material) and introduces robust parental controls, including customizable "quiet hours" to curb late-night usage. To support academic integrity, the platform includes interactive educational modes that guide students through problem-solving steps rather than simply writing their essays or providing direct answers. The launch comes amidst intense state-level regulatory pressure regarding AI safety and data privacy for minors. On social media, the release has divided opinion; while parents and educators appreciate the educational focus and safety guardrails, some tech critics argue that teenagers can easily bypass content filters and that prolonged exposure may lead to cognitive dependence on automated tools for basic homework tasks.

### **SpaceX Reaches 100th Launch Milestone of 2026 as Musk Details Starship "30X" Alloy and Catch Timeline**
Aerospace giant [SpaceX](https://spacex.com) reached its 100th orbital mission of the year on August 19, 2026, and marked its 100th Falcon rocket launch of 2026 on August 22, pushing the active Starlink constellation past 11,000 satellites. Alongside these launch milestones, Elon Musk confirmed that SpaceX has transitioned Starship construction from 301 stainless steel to a proprietary "30X" alloy. This custom steel formulation is optimized to withstand extreme cryogenic temperatures and intense re-entry heat, marking a shift from commercial materials to in-house metallurgical development. Furthermore, Musk announced that the first "tower catch" attempt of the Starship upper stage using the launch tower's mechanical "chopstick" arms is scheduled to occur "in a few months" rather than during the upcoming Flight 14. Aerospace communities on X are actively debating the engineering challenges of catching a 50-meter-tall spacecraft traveling at orbital speeds compared to the Super Heavy booster catch, with many praising the incremental safety approach.

### **James Webb Space Telescope Uncovers "Black Hole Stars" in the Deep Universe**
Astronomers utilizing [NASA](https://nasa.gov)’s James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) have reported the discovery of a new class of cosmic objects termed "black hole stars" (or quasi-stars) in the early universe, visible as bright red dots. These colossal structures, roughly the size of our solar system, are powered by a central supermassive black hole (approximately 100,000 times the mass of the Sun) cocooned inside a massive envelope of gas. Rather than nuclear fusion, these objects generate their immense luminosity through gravitational energy released as matter accretes onto the central black hole. This discovery has generated significant excitement on physics and space subreddits, as it offers a potential solution to the "seed black hole" problem, explaining how supermassive black holes could exist so early in cosmic history by showing a direct-collapse mechanism that bypasses typical stellar lifecycles.

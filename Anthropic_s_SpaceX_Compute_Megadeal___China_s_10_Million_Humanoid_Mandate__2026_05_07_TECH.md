### **China’s 15th Five-Year Plan: A 10-Million Humanoid Target by 2030**
[Read More](news/The_Iron_Mandate_China’s_10-Million_Humanoid_Surge_and_the_End_of_Manual_Labor_As_We_Know_It.md)
The Chinese Ministry of Industry and Information Technology (MIIT) has unveiled the high-tech roadmap for the 15th Five-Year Plan (2026–2030), mandating the production of 10 million industrial and service humanoids by the end of the decade. The plan focuses on "Physical AI" as the primary driver of national productivity. Key targets include achieving a 35% reduction in actuator costs through mass-market standardized components and the establishment of "Robotic Special Zones" in Shenzhen and Suzhou. The announcement has sparked intense debate on Reddit's r/robotics regarding the "Robot-as-a-Service" (RaaS) model and its impact on global manufacturing labor. Critics argue that the aggressive subsidies could lead to a global oversupply of low-tier humanoids, while proponents, including Fourier Intelligence CEO Alex Gu, claim this is the "Henry Ford moment" for embodied intelligence. The plan also includes a $50 billion "Silicon-to-Sinew" fund to accelerate the development of synthetic muscles and high-torque density motors.

### **The "GeForceHammer" Crisis: ETH Zurich Exposes Massive Hardware Vulnerability in AI Clouds**
[Read More](news/GeForceHammer_How_ETH_Zurich’s_New_HBM34_Exploit_Could_Root_the_AI_Cloud.md)
Researchers at ETH Zurich have sent shockwaves through the cybersecurity community with the disclosure of "GeForceHammer," a hardware-level exploit that targets the High Bandwidth Memory (HBM3/4) used in modern AI accelerators. Unlike traditional Rowhammer attacks on system RAM, GeForceHammer leverages the parallel execution units of GPUs to "hammer" adjacent memory cells, potentially allowing an attacker to escape AI sandboxes or steal model weights from shared data center environments. "This is a fundamental physics problem in the density of HBM; no software patch can fully mitigate the risk without a 15-20% performance penalty," noted Professor Kaveh Razavi. On X, the debate has centered on the liability of cloud providers like AWS and CoreWeave. If a malicious actor can flip bits in a model’s "safety layer" via hardware, the concept of "Safe AI" becomes moot. Major providers are reportedly racing to implement "Refresh-on-Access" firmware updates, though technical experts on r/netsec warn that these are merely stop-gap measures for existing hardware.

### **Agentic Engineering: GitHub Copilot Shifts from Autocomplete to "Autonomous Scaffolding"**
[Read More](news/Untitled_9.md)
GitHub has officially rolled out "Copilot Workspace: Agent Mode," a significant upgrade that transitions the tool from a code suggestion engine to a fully autonomous agentic engineer. The system can now take a natural language description of a complex feature, plan the architectural changes across multiple files, execute the code, and run unit tests before presenting a verified Pull Request. ### **Blue Origin’s MK1 Lunar Lander Completes Critical Thermal Vacuum Tests**
Blue Origin’s "Blue Moon" MK1 cargo lander has successfully passed a rigorous 15-day thermal vacuum (TVAC) test at NASA’s Marshall Space Flight Center in Huntsville. The MK1 is designed to deliver up to 3 metric tons of equipment to the lunar surface as a precursor to the crewed MK2 lander. The test simulated the extreme temperature swings and deep-space vacuum of a lunar transit. "The MK1 isn't just a lander; it's the logistics backbone of the Permanent Lunar Presence," Jeff Bezos shared in a rare technical update on X. The success of the TVAC campaign keeps Blue Origin on track for its first uncrewed lunar landing attempt in Q1 2027. This progress is viewed by the space community on r/space as a vital counterbalance to SpaceX’s Starship dominance, ensuring a competitive multi-provider ecosystem for the Artemis program.

### **Precision Oncology: Caris Life Sciences Unveils AI "Digital Twins" for Myeloid Malignancies**
[Read More](news/The_94%_Precision_Hack_How_Caris'_Molecular_Digital_Twins_Are_Eradicating_Phase_III_Failures_(and_Fueling_the_'Simulation_Denial'_Crisis).md)
In a major breakthrough for life sciences, Caris Life Sciences has received FDA clearance for its "Molecular Digital Twin" platform. The system uses generative AI to create a virtual biological model of a patient’s specific cancer, based on whole-exome and whole-transcriptome sequencing. This allows oncologists to "test" thousands of drug combinations on the digital twin before treating the patient. In a clinical trial involving 1,200 patients with myeloid malignancies (leukemias), the AI predicted treatment response with a staggering 94% accuracy. "We are moving from trial-and-error medicine to predictive simulation," said Dr. David Spetzler, President of Caris. The debate on tech blogs has focused on the "black box" nature of these predictions and the ethical implications of AI-denied treatments if the simulation predicts a 0% success rate.

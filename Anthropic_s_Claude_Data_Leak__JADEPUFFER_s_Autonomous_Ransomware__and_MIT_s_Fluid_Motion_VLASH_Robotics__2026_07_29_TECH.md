# Anthropic's Claude Data Leak, JADEPUFFER's Autonomous Ransomware, and MIT's Fluid Motion VLASH Robotics

### **Claude Shared Conversations Indexation Leak**
[Read More](news/Anatomy_of_an_SEO_Footgun_How_Anthropic’s_robots.txt_Conflict_Exposed_Thousands_of_Private_Claude_Chats.md)
In late July 2026, security researchers and users discovered that Google and other search engines had indexed hundreds of shared conversation links and interactive "Artifacts" generated on Anthropic's Claude.ai platform. Using a simple search operator—specifically `site:claude.ai/share`—anyone could view and access a massive directory of private user data. The exposed logs contained highly sensitive records, including proprietary corporate code, internal employee performance reviews, sensitive medical files, and personal contact details. The incident triggered a debate over security defaults, as critics pointed out that Anthropic's shareable pages did not include the standard `noindex` tag in their HTTP headers or HTML metadata, leaving them open to crawler indexing once a user posted the link publicly. Anthropic responded by clarifying that the system was working as intended and that the indexing only occurred when users voluntarily posted their shared URLs on public forums or social media. However, under mounting public pressure, Anthropic worked with search engines to de-index the exposed pages by July 28–29, 2026, added default `noindex` configurations, and reminded users to review and revoke shared chats via their account privacy settings.

### **JADEPUFFER's Autonomous AI Agent Deploys ENCFORGE Ransomware**
[Read More](news/Agentic_Sabotage_Inside_JADEPUFFER’s_Autonomous_AI-Wiper_Campaign_and_the_ENCFORGE_Payload.md)
Cybersecurity researchers at Sysdig reported the first documented case of an "agentic threat actor" (ATA) utilizing a fully autonomous AI agent to execute an end-to-end ransomware campaign. The threat group, designated JADEPUFFER, gained initial entry by exploiting CVE-2025-3248, a critical CVSS 9.8 unauthenticated remote code execution (RCE) vulnerability in the `/api/v1/validate/code` endpoint of the Langflow AI orchestration framework. The autonomous LLM-driven agent successfully performed system reconnaissance, harvested local credentials, moved laterally, and deployed a specialized Go-based payload known as ENCFORGE. Unlike traditional ransomware, ENCFORGE targets and encrypts over 180 specific file extensions critical to the AI stack, including PyTorch and TensorFlow checkpoints (`.pt`, `.pth`, `.ckpt`), Hugging Face `SafeTensors`, llama.cpp GGUF/GGML weights, FAISS vector indices, and Apache Parquet, Arrow, and NumPy datasets. The ransomware operates as a wiper, employing a hybrid AES-256-CTR and RSA-2048 encryption scheme without establishing outbound command-and-control channels for payment negotiations. Security experts warn that the transition from human-operated attacks to self-correcting agentic payloads poses a significant threat to enterprise ML pipelines, demanding air-gapped backups for irreplaceable model weights.

### **MIT's VLASH Framework Accelerates Robotic Motion-Planning**
[Read More](news/Breaking_the_Inference_Stutter_How_MIT's_VLASH_Framework_Solves_Robotics’_Silent_Latency_Crisis.md)
Researchers at MIT, led by Jiaming Tang and Song Han, introduced VLASH (Vision-Language-Action Asynchronous Inference), a breakthrough robotic motion-planning framework that overcomes the latency barriers of traditional physical AI. Conventional Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models rely on synchronous inference, forcing a robot to pause and recalculate its planning trajectory after executing each action chunk. This creates a jerky, high-latency motion cycle. VLASH solves this "stop-and-think" delay by introducing future-state forecasting, allowing the system to run action execution and next-action inference in parallel. While the robot is actively moving, the model projects its future position and environment state, calculating subsequent movements on rolled-forward predictions rather than stale sensory data. Furthermore, the framework incorporates action quantization to generate coarser, larger motor steps to save processing overhead. In experimental testing, VLASH enabled robotic arms to fold laundry up to three times faster than traditional methods, doubled the speed of pick-and-place tasks, and demonstrated the agility needed for high-speed dynamic activities like Whack-a-Mole and table tennis. The hardware-agnostic solution represents a major step toward real-time responsive robotics in manufacturing and logistics.

### **Insilico Medicine’s AI-Designed Cancer Drug ISM6331 Receives FDA Fast Track**
[Read More](news/FDA_Fast_Tracks_Insilico's_Non-Covalent_pan-TEAD_Inhibitor_ISM6331_Generative_AI_Tackles_the_Undruggable_Hippo_Pathway.md)
On July 29, 2026, Insilico Medicine announced that the FDA has granted Fast Track Designation to ISM6331, its AI-designed, potential best-in-class pan-TEAD inhibitor. The designation is specifically for the treatment of adult patients with unresectable malignant pleural mesothelioma that has progressed following anti-PD-1 immunotherapy (with or without anti-CTLA-4 therapy) and platinum-based chemotherapy. Discovered using Insilico's proprietary generative AI platform Chemistry42, ISM6331 is a small-molecule, non-covalent inhibitor targeting TEAD transcription factors to restore balance to the Hippo signaling pathway, which is frequently dysregulated in aggressive solid tumors. This milestone represents the first Fast Track Designation for an entirely AI-driven program in Insilico's pipeline, granting the company frequent FDA communication, rolling reviews, and eligibility for accelerated approval. The candidate is currently undergoing a global multicenter Phase I clinical trial (NCT06566079) launched in January 2025, and first-in-human clinical data has been accepted for a Rapid Oral presentation at the ESMO 2026 Congress in Madrid on October 25, 2026.

### **AMD and Cerebras Partner for "Disaggregated Inference" in AI Computing**
In late July 2026, the AI hardware industry analyzed the strategic implications of the partnership between Advanced Micro Devices (AMD) and Cerebras Systems, originally unveiled at AMD’s Advancing AI 2026 conference. The collaboration centers on "disaggregated inference," an architectural shift that splits the two main stages of large language model inference: prompt ingestion (prefill) and token generation (decode). Because these tasks require vastly different memory bandwidth and compute profiles, running them on unified hardware introduces inefficiencies. The joint solution combines AMD's Helios rack-scale systems to handle high-throughput prefill operations with the Cerebras Wafer-Scale Engine (WSE) to manage ultra-fast token decoding. AMD CEO Lisa Su positioned the Helios infrastructure, powered by 6th Gen EPYC CPUs, as a major competitor to Nvidia's NVL72 rack, asserting superior token efficiency and cost-effectiveness. Amid projections that the total addressable market (TAM) for AI accelerators will reach $1.4 trillion by 2030, AMD confirmed major Helios deployments with top labs including Anthropic, OpenAI, and Meta. The disaggregated hardware solution is scheduled to become available through Cerebras Cloud in the second half of 2026.

### **U.S. Space Force Advances Orbital Maneuvering Exercises in VICTUS HAZE Demonstration**
The U.S. Space Force’s VICTUS HAZE Tactically Responsive Space (TacRS) mission reached a key milestone as commercial partners True Anomaly and Rocket Lab completed their first orbital intercept exercise. Initiated by a rapid-call launch on June 19, 2026, which saw Rocket Lab deploy its "Puma" target satellite within 17 hours of notice, the mission progressed as True Anomaly's "Jackal" spacecraft successfully rendezvoused with the target. Operating autonomously, the Jackal satellite intercepted, characterized, and imaged the non-cooperative Puma satellite within 61 hours of tasking, surpassing the military's 72-hour operational requirement. The spacecraft will continue active maneuvering in a "space tag" exercise, alternating roles as target and interceptor to practice advanced rendezvous and proximity operations (RPO). Alongside this demonstration, the Space Force expanded its responsive capabilities by awarding a $1.6 billion contract to SpaceX for 18 launches of national security communications and targeting satellites, and a $266 million contract to Rocket Lab for 12 suborbital missions, which will inaugurate a new launch site in Alaska.

### **FDA Digital Health Pilot TEMPO Selects Dexcom for Metabolic Health AI Monitoring**
The FDA selected Dexcom, Inc. as the first participant in its Technology-Enabled Meaningful Patient Outcomes (TEMPO) for Digital Health Devices Pilot. The initiative is designed to test a new risk-based regulatory framework that accelerates patient access to wearables, sensors, and AI-driven clinical software. Dexcom will implement its Glucose Health Program, which combines real-time data from its G7 and Stelo continuous glucose monitors (CGMs) with contextual sleep, nutrition, and exercise data. The system uses AI to generate personalized insights to assist clinicians and patients in managing prediabetes and Type 2 diabetes. The TEMPO pilot is aligned with the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) Innovation Center's Advancing Chronic Care with Effective, Scalable Solutions (ACCESS) model, which began patient enrollment in July 2026. Under the pilot, the FDA will exercise enforcement discretion, allowing Dexcom to implement software updates and collect real-world performance evidence in clinical settings while bypassing certain traditional premarket review cycles. The TEMPO program plans to enroll up to 10 manufacturers in clinical categories spanning metabolic, musculoskeletal, and behavioral health.

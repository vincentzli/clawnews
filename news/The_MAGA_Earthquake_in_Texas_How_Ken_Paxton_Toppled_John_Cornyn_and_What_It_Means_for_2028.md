# The MAGA Earthquake in Texas: How Ken Paxton Toppled John Cornyn and What It Means for 2028

The Texas Republican Party establishment suffered a catastrophic collapse on Tuesday, May 26, 2026, as Attorney General Ken Paxton decisively defeated four-term incumbent Senator John Cornyn in a primary runoff election. Securing approximately 64% of the vote to Cornyn's 36%, Paxton executed one of the most significant political upsets in modern American history. This result makes Cornyn the first sitting U.S. Senator from Texas to lose a primary challenge since Democrat Ralph Yarborough was ousted in 1970. The outcome effectively ends Cornyn's two-decade tenure in Washington and significantly consolidates the populist, MAGA-aligned wing's influence over the state's GOP apparatus.

The trajectory of this race was initially tighter. In the March 3 primary, Cornyn held a narrow advantage, capturing 42% of the vote compared to Paxton’s 40.5%. Because neither candidate cleared the 50% threshold, the race advanced to a grueling two-month runoff. During this period, the fundamental divide within the Republican base was laid bare. Cornyn, possessing a massive war chest, campaigned on his long-standing conservative voting record and attempted to leverage his historical ties to President Donald Trump, running advertisements claiming he voted with the current President 99% of the time. However, Cornyn's messaging failed to resonate with an electorate increasingly focused on ideological purity and grievance politics.

A decisive turning point for Cornyn's campaign arrived on May 19, precisely one week before the runoff, when President Trump officially endorsed Ken Paxton. Trump’s endorsement was not merely a statement of support for the Attorney General; it was a blistering repudiation of the incumbent. The President explicitly labeled Cornyn a "RINO" (Republican In Name Only), weaponizing Cornyn's legislative record against him. Specifically, Trump and Paxton hammered Cornyn for his lead role in negotiating the 2022 Bipartisan Safer Communities Act—a gun safety measure passed in the wake of the Uvalde school shooting. Paxton’s campaign relentlessly portrayed this compromise as a betrayal of Second Amendment rights. Furthermore, Paxton targeted Cornyn's past votes authorizing foreign military aid to Ukraine, framing the incumbent as a globalist enabler of former President Joe Biden and former Vice President Kamala Harris.

The incumbent attempted to focus voter attention on Paxton's immense legal and ethical baggage, repeatedly reminding the electorate of Paxton's 2023 impeachment trial in the Texas Senate, the resolution of his decade-old securities fraud indictment, and ongoing federal probes into alleged bribery and abuse of office. Cornyn and his allied political action committees spent millions on television advertisements arguing that Paxton's ethical controversies made him a severe and unnecessary liability for the November general election, threatening the party's grip on the Senate. Yet, the staggering 28-point margin of defeat demonstrates that Texas Republican primary voters fundamentally rejected this argument. The 64% to 36% vote margin suggests that for a majority of primary voters, Paxton’s combative political style held more appeal than Cornyn's warnings regarding his personal or professional conduct. Paxton's campaign successfully framed his legal troubles not as disqualifying scandals, but as evidence of persecution by the political establishment, a message that resonated strongly with his supporters.

The immediate aftermath of the runoff has triggered a massive reaction across social media platforms, highlighting the deep fractures within the electorate. On Reddit’s r/politics, the prevailing sentiment regarding Cornyn’s defeat leaned heavily into political irony, observing the failure of Cornyn's appeasement strategy. "He should realize he was with Trump, but Trump was never with him. Trump is only for himself," noted one highly upvoted user. This sentiment captured the inherent risk for traditional Republicans attempting to navigate the Trump era: loyalty to the President is often a one-way street.

Meanwhile, on the r/Texas subreddit, the mood was decidedly more pessimistic, with users expressing deep frustration over the electorate's willingness to overlook the Attorney General's documented scandals. "The very fact that this guy has success... tells me just how morally corrupt this country is," read a prominent comment, reflecting a broader disillusionment among moderates and progressives in the state. Data analysts and political junkies on r/centrist immediately began dissecting the long-term implications, pointing out that while Trump-backed populist candidates frequently dominate low-turnout primaries, they have historically underperformed in general elections, citing the 2018 and 2022 midterm cycles as primary evidence.

This dynamic sets the stage for a highly volatile and fiercely competitive general election. Paxton will now face Democratic nominee, State Representative James Talarico, in November. Recognizing the unique vulnerability of their opponent, the Talarico campaign immediately pivoted to capture disillusioned moderate Republicans and right-leaning independents who supported Cornyn. Talarico has already launched an aggressive statewide "People vs. Ken Paxton" rally tour, attempting to frame the upcoming race not as a traditional partisan battle over tax rates or regulatory policy, but as a fundamental referendum on basic ethics and the rule of law.

Political strategists and pollsters on platforms like r/TexasPolitics are actively debating whether this race represents a genuine pickup opportunity for Democrats. While Texas has famously not elected a Democrat to the U.S. Senate since Lloyd Bentsen in 1988, many users argued that Paxton is an unexpected "gift" to the opposition. Some predictive models and electoral maps discussed online suggest the race could imminently shift from a safe "Solid Republican" categorization to a highly contested "Lean Republican." The Democratic strategy relies heavily on the historical assumption that suburban swing voters in the Dallas-Fort Worth metroplex and the Houston suburbs—many of whom were previously comfortable supporting a traditional, business-friendly conservative like Cornyn—will ultimately balk at voting for a candidate with Paxton's deeply polarizing and legally fraught profile.

Ultimately, Paxton's primary victory is more than a localized political upset; it is a bellwether for the 2028 national landscape. It signals that the transformation of the Republican Party is complete, with policy nuances and ethical norms fully subordinated to populist allegiance and base mobilization. As the general election approaches, the nation will be watching Texas to see if this hardline insurgent strategy can withstand the scrutiny of a broader, more diverse electorate, or if it will provide Democrats with their most significant opening in the Lone Star State in a generation.

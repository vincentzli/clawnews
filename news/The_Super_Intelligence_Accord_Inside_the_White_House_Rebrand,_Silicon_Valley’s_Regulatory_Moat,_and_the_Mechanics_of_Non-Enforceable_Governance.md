# **The "Super Intelligence" Accord: Inside the White House Rebrand, Silicon Valley’s Regulatory Moat, and the Mechanics of Non-Enforceable Governance**

####

Yesterday afternoon in the East Room of the White House, the lexicon of federal technological governance underwent a deliberate administrative fracture—presided over by an executive signature on a document whose viral ceremonial masthead proclaimed Donald Trump the President of the "Unites States."

Beyond the predictable deluge of online mockery highlighting Washington’s failure to run spellcheck on a document concerning human-surpassing computation, the September 29, 2026 executive order, *"Inaugurating the Era of Super Intelligence,"* together with the *"White House Accord on Super Intelligence: Joint Commitment on Frontier Responsibilities,"* represents a masterclass in regulatory maneuvering. Flanked by Sundar Pichai (Google), Dario Amodei (Anthropic), Mark Zuckerberg (Meta), Sam Altman (OpenAI), Elon Musk (xAI), and Jensen Huang (Nvidia), the executive action formally purges the terms "Artificial Intelligence" and "AI" from all non-statutory federal communications, agency guidelines, and internal policy frameworks, substituting them universally with "Super Intelligence" (SI).

While the statutory definition of artificial intelligence codified by Congress under 15 U.S.C. § 9401(3) remains immutable without legislative repeal, the order tasks the Assistant to the President for Science and Technology with establishing a formal legal definition of "Super Intelligence" within 60 days. In lockstep, the six tech chiefs committed to a four-tier voluntary governance compact covering internal telemetry, dedicated verification teams, accredited third-party evaluations, and board-level safety committees invested with deployment-halting authority.

To proponents, the accord prepares the United States for an imminent transition toward multi-gigawatt compute clusters and autonomous frontier models. To critics across Capitol Hill, academia, and open-source communities, it is the most sophisticated corporate regulatory capture in modern history: an effort to render binding legislative oversight obsolete by voluntarily self-policing a science-fiction tier of technology that only its signatories can afford to build.

##### The Semantic Wedge: The Legal Mechanics of Abandoning "AI"
In administrative law, semantics represent jurisdiction. The decision to discard "Artificial Intelligence" in favor of "Super Intelligence" is not an aesthetic whim; it is a calculated jurisdictional carve-out.

Under the National Artificial Intelligence Initiative Act of 2020 (15 U.S.C. § 9401(3)), Congress enacted a comprehensive definition: a "machine-based system that can, for a given set of human-defined objectives, make predictions, recommendations or decisions influencing real or virtual environments." That statutory scope covers everything from financial logistics heuristics and computer vision models to modern enterprise foundation models.

Because an executive order cannot alter statutory text, 15 U.S.C. § 9401(3) remains the law of the land for statutory compliance. However, by establishing "Super Intelligence" as the operative benchmark across executive branch agencies—including the Department of Commerce, the Department of Energy, and the FTC—the administration lays the groundwork to narrow the scope of discretionary federal intervention.

Under this framework, standard enterprise models, predictive analytics, and mid-tier open-source weights can be classified as conventional computing, exempting them from onerous reporting rules. Conversely, only models meeting the forthcoming OSTP definition of "Super Intelligence" will fall under frontier surveillance.

"This is an immaculate semantic shell game," remarked AI policy researcher and author Gary Marcus on X. "By abandoning 'AI' and directing the OSTP to define 'Super Intelligence,' the administration and the big six are diverting regulatory attention from tangible, immediate market harms—algorithmic bias, massive copyright infringement, synthetic disinformation, and labor disruption—toward speculative existential horizons that only the six signatories have the compute to reach."

Venture capitalist Marc Andreessen took the opposite view, praising the move as a long-overdue check on the administrative state:
> *"The regulatory attempt to treat software code and open mathematical models as dangerous munitions under the banner of 'AI safety' was a direct assault on American innovation. If the federal government wants to monitor multi-gigawatt installations for national security threats, that is legitimate defense policy. Decoupling that from everyday software frees the rest of the tech economy."*

##### The Technical Metric Dilemma: How Do You Define "SI"?
The OSTP’s 60-day mandate to formalize the legal definition of "Super Intelligence" faces an immediate crisis of technical calibration. During the previous administration, Executive Order 14110 leaned on a brute-force compute threshold: $10^{26}$ total floating-point operations (FLOPs). But by late 2026, the scaling equation has fundamentally fractured.

The transition from pure pre-training parameter expansion to post-training test-time compute, architectural pruning, and low-precision (FP4/FP8) matrix arithmetic means that compute volume alone no longer correlates strictly with dangerous autonomy. A $10^{25}$ FLOP model integrated with recursive tree-search reasoning architectures (analogous to the descendants of OpenAI's o1/o3 and DeepMind's AlphaProof) can dramatically outperform a $10^{27}$ brute-force autoregressive model on tasks requiring planning and tool execution.

According to researchers familiar with OSTP discussions, the administration is moving away from static FLOP floors toward three concrete capability-based tripwires:

1. **Autonomous Zero-Day Cyber Exploitation:** The demonstrated capability of an agent to autonomously analyze closed-source binary code, identify previously undiscovered zero-day vulnerabilities, construct functional exploits, and maintain command-and-control evasion across hardened national security infrastructure without human intervention.
2. **CBRN Knowledge Uplift & Synthesis Enablement:** Measurable uplift in enabling a non-domain actor to design, synthesize, and weaponize Chemical, Biological, Radiological, or Nuclear materials—specifically passing wet-lab validation steps for functional pathogen packaging while circumventing commercial synthesis screening filters.
3. **Recursive Autonomic Self-Improvement:** Achieving end-to-end autonomy in machine learning engineering, measured by an agent’s ability to independently diagnose architectural bottlenecks, write novel training kernels, optimize loss curves, and bootstrap successors on benchmarks exceeding SWE-bench Verified ($>90\%$) over continuous, multi-day horizon tasks.

Meta’s Chief AI Scientist Yann LeCun was quick to highlight the scientific incoherence of attempting to regulate hypothetical superintelligence through the lens of current architectures:
> *"Rebranding autoregressive LLMs as 'Super Intelligence' does not solve their fundamental architectural limitations. They lack physical grounding, do not possess common sense, and cannot formulate robust world models. You cannot regulate an existential risk threshold for architectures that are mathematically incapable of autonomous open-ended agency. It is political theater substituting for computer science."*

##### Enforceability Analysis: Corporate Accords vs. The EU AI Act
The most glaring fault line lies in the structural enforceability of the accord. The voluntary commitments signed by Pichai, Amodei, Zuckerberg, Altman, Musk, and Huang arrive just as the European Union’s AI Act reaches full operational enforcement across 2025 and 2026.

| Dimension | The White House Accord on Super Intelligence (US) | The EU AI Act (Regulation 2024/1689) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Legal Basis** | Executive Order / Voluntary Corporate Compact | Enacted Transnational Statutory Regulation |
| **Scope** | Discretionary federal agencies & 6 private signatories | All providers placing GPAI models on the EU market |
| **Enforceability** | Voluntary self-policing; No statutory civil penalties | Legally binding; Enforced by the EU AI Office |
| **Penalties** | Public relations fallout; Potential FTC § 5 deceptive trade scrutiny | Fines up to €35 million or 7% of global annual turnover |
| **Third-Party Audits** | "Accredited" partners selected in consensus with labs | Mandatory standardized independent third-party assessments |
| **Kill-Switch Power** | Internal Board-Level Safety Committees | Judicial injunctions, mandatory recall, & market bans |

The structural vulnerability of the accord resides in **Tier 4**: board-level safety committees empowered to halt deployment. Under Delaware General Corporation Law (DGCL § 141(a)), the business and affairs of a corporation must be managed under the direction of its board of directors, bound by strict fiduciary duties to shareholders.

If an independent safety committee at a publicly traded firm (or a venture-backed entity with severe investor liquidity commitments) exercises its unilateral power to indefinitely halt the deployment of a flagship model that cost $4 billion in compute to train, it faces an existential legal dilemma. Disgruntled shareholders could file immediate derivative suits alleging breach of fiduciary duty, arguing that voluntary adherence to an unenforceable presidential pact does not supersede the legal mandate to preserve corporate capital.

Yoshua Bengio, scientific director of Mila and a leading voice on AI existential risk, underscored this exact point:
> *"Voluntary accords represent an inherent conflict of interest. When multi-trillion-dollar commercial dominance hinges on deploying a frontier model first, safety red lines established by self-policing committees inevitably dissolve. Without statutory whistleblower indemnification, independent federal inspection rights, and clear civil liability, a voluntary 'kill switch' is legally and commercially illusory."*

##### The Biosecurity and Hardware Verification Protocols
Despite its voluntary structure, the accord establishes unprecedented technical coordination across two vectors: automated biosecurity synthesis checks and hardware telemetry.

In the biosecurity annex, signatories committed to integrating standardized cryptographic signatures into DNA/RNA ordering pipelines. Any model execution tracing sequences to pathogenic agents (such as filoviruses, smallpox variants, or enhanced avian influenza strains) must automatically sever agentic execution, preserve memory logs for federal forensic auditing, and refuse iterative molecular redesign.

On the silicon layer, Jensen Huang’s presence on the White House stage signaled a pivot toward hardware-enabled governance. The accord outlines an industry-standard telemetry profile for hyper-scale training runs across Nvidia’s Blackwell and future Rubin architectures.

"Frontier governance must ultimately map to physical realities," Huang noted following the signing ceremony. "A superintelligent model is not an abstract cloud concept; it is millions of GPUs, gigawatts of dedicated nuclear or natural gas power, and hundreds of terabytes of interconnect bandwidth. You cannot conceal a frontier run from the laws of physics, and you cannot verify safety without deep hardware attestation."

##### The Impending Confrontation with Congress
The White House’s attempt to resolve AI policy via executive decree and voluntary private pacts has triggered immediate bipartisan friction on Capitol Hill. Legislative leaders argue that the executive branch is usurping authority to create an unregulated sanctuary for the largest technology platforms.

"The President cannot unilaterally declare that 'AI' no longer exists in federal oversight simply by calling it 'Super Intelligence,'" stated Senator Elizabeth Warren during a press conference this morning. "This accord was negotiated behind closed doors with six corporate monopolists who walked out of the Oval Office with the power to regulate themselves. Congress wrote the statutory definitions, and Congress will write the binding safety laws—not six tech CEOs who can't even proofread the name of our country on their own press release."

As the OSTP begins its 60-day sprint to legally define "Super Intelligence," the tech industry stands at an unprecedented crossroads. If the resulting framework defines "SI" so narrowly that only multi-gigawatt, autonomous frontier clusters are subject to scrutiny, the administration will have effectively granted blanket deregulation to the broader commercial AI ecosystem. 

Simultaneously, it will have handed the six signatories something far more valuable than an unregulated market: an officially anointed, federally insulated club whose frontier dominion is guaranteed by the very government that purported to rein them in.

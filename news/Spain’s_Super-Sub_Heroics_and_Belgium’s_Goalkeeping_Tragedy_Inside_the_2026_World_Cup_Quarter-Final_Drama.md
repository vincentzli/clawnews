# Spain’s Super-Sub Heroics and Belgium’s Goalkeeping Tragedy: Inside the 2026 World Cup Quarter-Final Drama

On a warm California evening at SoFi Stadium in Inglewood, the margins of international football were laid bare in their most brutal fashion. Spain’s 2-1 victory over Belgium on Friday, July 10, 2026, was not merely a quarter-final match; it was a tactical chess match that ended with Spain advancing to the semi-finals, leaving a trail of historical records shattered and individual heartbreak that will echo in Belgian football for years.

From the opening whistle, Spain, under head coach Luis de la Fuente, sought to impose their tactical identity. They dominated possession, holding 68% of the ball, and outshot Belgium 17 to 5, generating an Expected Goals (xG) metric of 2.08 compared to Belgium's meager 0.37. The pressure paid off in the 30th minute. Spain's Dani Olmo unleashed a stinging shot that Belgian starting goalkeeper Thibaut Courtois could only parry into the path of Fabián Ruiz. The Paris Saint-Germain midfielder made no mistake, pouncing on the rebound to slot it home and give Spain a deserved 1-0 lead. 

Yet, Rudi Garcia’s Belgium side refused to capitulate. The Red Devils struck back in the 41st minute when Timothy Castagne delivered a precise cross into the Spanish penalty area, and Charles De Ketelaere rose above the defense to power a header past Unai Simón. The goal was momentous: it equalized the score at 1-1 and shattered Spain’s tournament-long defensive wall. The equalizer ended Unai Simón’s historic clean-sheet run at 650 consecutive minutes without conceding a goal at the World Cup. Simón's streak had begun during the 2022 World Cup in Qatar after a goal conceded to Japan's Ao Tanaka in the 51st minute of their group-stage match, carrying through Spain's matches in 2026 against Cape Verde (0-0), Saudi Arabia (4-0), Uruguay (1-0), and Austria (3-0). By reaching 650 minutes, Simón surpassed the previous men's World Cup record of 517 minutes, held by Italy's Walter Zenga since 1990.

The tactical battle intensified in the second half, but the defining moment of the match occurred in the 71st minute, far from the Spanish goal. Thibaut Courtois, who had kept Belgium in the game with four crucial saves, went down clutching his leg. The 34-year-old goalkeeper was forced off the pitch in tears, suffering from a quadriceps injury. As Courtois was consoled by his teammates, the gravity of the situation settled over SoFi Stadium. 

With Courtois sidelined, Garcia was forced to call upon Manchester United’s goalkeeper Senne Lammens, who joined the English club from Royal Antwerp in September 2025 and was named the Premier League Transfer of the Season. The 24-year-old walked into a high-pressure cauldron. For 17 minutes, Lammens held his own, but in the 88th minute, disaster struck. Spanish defender Pau Cubarsí launched a speculative long-range effort that should have been a routine save. Lammens, however, spilled the ball directly in front of his goal. 

Anticipating the error was Mikel Merino. The Spanish midfielder, who had only been substituted onto the pitch two minutes prior in the 86th minute, pounced on the loose ball and poked it into the net. It was Merino's second touch of the match, cementing his status as Spain's ultimate "super-sub" and "clutch" hero. Merino had already saved Spain in the Round of 16 against Portugal on July 6, when he entered in the 85th minute and scored a 91st-minute winner. 

"I've done this again, and it's happened to me again, so it would seem that coincidence exists," Merino said after the match. "If you're ready and you try, I guess it can happen for you. Honestly, it's crazy to be able to help the team once again. This time in a different way, but at the same time to believe and trust that the opposing goalkeeper could make a mistake and to stay alert."

On social media, the contrast between Spanish joy and Belgian despair was stark. On Reddit’s r/soccer, fans marveled at Merino’s knack for late-game heroics, with users celebrating his reputation as a clutch player off the bench. Meanwhile, on X, Belgian supporters lamented the end of an era. With Courtois hinting after the match that he intends to take a break from the national team to recover from his physical setbacks, fans feared the definitive closing of Belgium's "Golden Generation." 

Belgium's head coach Rudi Garcia reflected on the defeat in his post-match press conference. "Big matches are decided by details," Garcia remarked. "Against this kind of team, you can't give any gifts or make mistakes. And unfortunately, we got eliminated on that. Unfortunately, to beat a team of this caliber, you need luck on your side. And the stars didn't align for us."

Spain now moves on to face France in a highly anticipated semi-final on Tuesday, July 14, 2026, at Dallas Stadium (AT&T Stadium) in Arlington, Texas. France secured their place in the final four with a comfortable 2-0 victory over Morocco on July 9 at Boston Stadium.

The road to the World Cup trophy is narrowing, and as Belgium learned at SoFi Stadium, the cost of a single slip is absolute.

# The Backchannel Peace: Inside the Kushner-Witkoff Mission, the Death of the Anchorage Formula, and the Battle Over Ukraine’s "Air Ceasefire"

On the evening of July 11, 2026, U.S. Senator Lindsey Graham of South Carolina passed away at the age of 71 due to an aortic dissection. His death set in motion a diplomatic sequence that has reshuffled the geopolitics of the Russia-Ukraine war. When Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy traveled to Washington on July 28, 2026, to attend Graham’s memorial service at the Washington National Cathedral—where President Donald Trump delivered the eulogy—the occasion served as the catalyst for a bilateral meeting at the White House. 

The July 28 session signaled what diplomatic observers described as a practical shift in a relationship previously strained by political differences. According to White House and Ukrainian officials, the two leaders engaged in a dialogue focused on security and diplomatic structures. Zelenskyy pressed for licensing agreements allowing Ukraine to manufacture Patriot missile interceptors locally, a crucial necessity as the country's energy grid faces ongoing vulnerability. He also sought permission to utilize Elon Musk’s Starlink satellite internet network to guide deep strikes within Russian borders—a request President Trump did not immediately greenlight. Trump later stated on social media that the meeting went "very well," while Zelenskyy described the conversation as "good."

The primary outgrowth of this meeting is the planned mid-to-late August 2026 travel of U.S. special envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff to Kyiv. Strategically timed to coincide with the 35th anniversary of Ukraine's independence on August 24, the trip represents a major diplomatic shift. Observers have pointed out a diplomatic imbalance: since the launch of the Trump administration's backchannel peace initiatives, Kushner and Witkoff have traveled to Moscow multiple times—including a high-profile, five-hour session at the Kremlin in December 2025 and another in late January 2026—but have never visited Kyiv. 

The upcoming Kyiv mission arrives as the previous framework for peace, known as the "Anchorage formula," is officially declared dead. The formula originated from an August 15, 2025 summit at Joint Base Elmendorf-Richardson in Anchorage, Alaska, where President Trump and Russian President Vladimir Putin discussed a potential "3+2" peace framework. The proposal envisioned Russia gaining control over the entirety of the Donbas region in exchange for ceding occupied pockets of Kharkiv and Sumy oblasts back to Ukraine, while freezing the front lines in the southern Kherson and Zaporizhzhia regions. 

The Anchorage formula was declared extinct on July 23, 2026, during a meeting in Manila between U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio and Russian Foreign Minister Sergei Lavrov on the sidelines of the ASEAN foreign ministers' summit. Rubio officially communicated to Lavrov that the Anchorage parameters were non-viable because they were fundamentally unacceptable to Ukraine, which refuses to trade territory for peace. Lavrov responded by warning against further U.S. arms shipments to Kyiv and accusing Washington of failing to honor the "spirit of Anchorage."

With the Anchorage framework defunct, the State Department is exploring a structured "air ceasefire" proposal. The concept, discussed during the July 28 White House meeting, calls for a mutual halt to long-range missile and drone strikes targeting cities and energy infrastructure, while ground combat continues along the current front lines. 

The proposal has ignited intense debate. On social media platforms like Reddit’s r/UkrainianConflict, skepticism is widespread. Commenters have argued that an air ceasefire would only benefit Russia by letting them rebuild their power grid and logistics hubs while continuing ground assaults. Critics argue that a partial ceasefire would strip Ukraine of its most effective tactical leverage. Over the past six months, Ukraine has intensified long-range drone strikes on Russian oil refineries and infrastructure—a development Secretary Rubio characterized as a "new dynamic" that could provide Ukraine with vital leverage. Under the air ceasefire, these strikes would have to halt, potentially shielding Russia's oil economy. Kremlin spokesperson Dmitry Peskov dismissed the reports of the air ceasefire as "media speculation," noting that Moscow has received no concrete proposals and will judge any future frameworks solely on whether they align with Russian interests.

Complicating the Kushner-Witkoff mission are conflicts of interest that have drawn intense scrutiny from Capitol Hill. House Foreign Affairs Committee Ranking Member Gregory Meeks and other House Democrats have repeatedly demanded that Witkoff, Kushner, and Secretary Rubio testify regarding their private financial ties to foreign entities and their diplomatic activities concerning the war in Ukraine. 

Jared Kushner’s private equity firm, Affinity Partners, has seen its foreign-sourced assets under management grow to approximately $4.8 billion, largely funded by sovereign wealth funds from Saudi Arabia, the UAE, and Qatar. On August 4, 2026, a massive $55 billion leveraged buyout of Electronic Arts (EA)—originally announced in September 2025—is scheduled to close. The transaction will leave Saudi Arabia’s Public Investment Fund (PIF) with a 93.4% majority stake, with the remaining 6.6% split between Silver Lake and Kushner’s Affinity Partners. 

Simultaneously, Steve Witkoff faces scrutiny over his cryptocurrency venture, World Liberty Financial (WLF), co-founded with the Trump family. In January 2025, an investment firm linked to UAE National Security Advisor Sheikh Tahnoon bin Zayed Al Nahyan purchased a 49% stake in WLF for $500 million. Approximately $187 million was distributed to Trump family entities, while $31 million went to entities connected to the Witkoff family. Critics point out that Witkoff subsequently participated in official U.S. government negotiations regarding the export of advanced AI chips to the UAE, specifically to the firm G42, which is chaired by Sheikh Tahnoon. The chip export was approved in November 2025.

Unlike typical diplomatic envoys, Kushner and Witkoff serve in roles that do not require Senate confirmation or standard ethics disclosures, leaving lawmakers like Senator Thom Tillis concerned about the lack of traditional oversight. As the envoys prepare for their first official meeting in Kyiv, they must navigate not only the strategic red lines of Moscow and Kyiv but also the domestic political storm surrounding their own financial entanglements.

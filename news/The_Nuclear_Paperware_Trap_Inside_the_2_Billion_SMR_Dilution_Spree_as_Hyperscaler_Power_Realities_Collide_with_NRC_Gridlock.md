# The Nuclear Paperware Trap: Inside the $2 Billion SMR Dilution Spree as Hyperscaler Power Realities Collide with NRC Gridlock

For eighteen months, Wall Street peddled an intoxicating narrative: artificial intelligence would unlock an immediate renaissance for small modular nuclear reactors. Hyperscalers, projecting datacenter power consumption to surge from 4% of total U.S. electricity in 2023 to an estimated 9.1% by 2030, would bypass congested regional transmission queues by anchoring custom 50-to-300 megawatt reactors directly behind the meter.

On Friday, that thesis met a brutal reckoning on the tape. NuScale Power (NYSE: SMR) plunged 14.1% to $8.61 after UBS analyst Jon Windham downgraded the equity to Sell from Neutral and slashed his 12-month price target by 40% from $10.00 to $6.00, triggering a wave of desk downgrades across the Street. Simultaneously, Oklo Inc. (NYSE: OKLO)—the silicon-valley-backed reactor developer championed by OpenAI’s Sam Altman—tumbled 7.4% to $36.22 after filing a Form 424B5 prospectus supplement with the Securities and Exchange Commission for a fresh $1.0 billion At-The-Market (ATM) equity offering.

The timing of Oklo’s ATM program stunned market participants. It came exactly twenty-four hours after Oklo officially exhausted a previous $1.0 billion equity distribution facility established in May 2026, under which it sold 17,971,448 shares at a volume-weighted average price of $55.64. By tapping the market for another $1.0 billion through an underwriting syndicate led by Goldman Sachs, BofA Securities, and Citigroup, Oklo is poised to issue approximately 27.6 million additional shares at current trading levels, inflicting an immediate 14.6% to 14.9% dilution on existing holders against its 185,090,155 shares outstanding as of June 30, 2026. Compounding retail frustration, SEC Form 4 filings revealed that Chief Executive Jacob DeWitte had liquidated 120,000 shares for $4.62 million in late August under a pre-established Rule 10b5-1 plan.

On retail trading message boards, the selloff triggered visceral warfare. On Stocktwits, where sentiment had tracked at peak euphoria for six months, retail bulls rushed to defend the capital calls by comparing nuclear development to Tesla’s early manufacturing hell. “And they said the same thing about TSLA in 2019 - legal scam. Ponzi. Fraud. iPad on the car,” posted retail trader @beztee. “Stock went up in multiples when they pulled through all the FUD. Last I heard, Meta evaluated OKLO and decided to partner with them.”

Other retail commentators pointed toward geopolitical and political tailwinds in Washington, where former Oklo board member Chris Wright now serves as U.S. Secretary of Energy under President Donald Trump. “Chris Wright stepping down to become the U.S. Secretary of Energy means Oklo’s former board member now runs the exact federal department that controls site permits, surplus plutonium distribution, and massive HALEU funding grants,” argued investor @JohnWick13, framing the current dip as a temporary roadblock in an inevitable multi-gigawatt buildout.

Yet fundamental analysts point out that nuclear physics cannot be scaled with software iteration speeds. Unlike electric vehicles, where assembly lines can be tweaked in real time, commercial nuclear reactors face the unyielding fortress of Nuclear Regulatory Commission (NRC) licensing. Oklo has yet to secure NRC approval for its Aurora powerhouse design after its initial Combined License Application was rejected without prejudice in 2022; its target deployment at Idaho National Laboratory remains slated for 2027 at the earliest.

Meanwhile, NuScale’s actual operating fundamentals underscore the stark gulf between promotional press releases and cash generation. According to NuScale’s Q2 2026 Form 10-Q, the company generated just $75,000 in revenue for the three months ended June 30, 2026—a 99.1% collapse from $8.05 million in the prior-year period. For the first half of 2026, total revenues totaled a microscopic $640,000, while operating losses expanded 55.0% year-over-year to $121.53 million. Only $24.78 million in investment interest earned on its $1.072 billion cash and short-term investment hoard prevented its six-month net loss from exceeding $96.75 million.

In his downgrade note, Windham laid bare Wall Street’s aggressive over-modeling. Consensus estimates currently price in $124 million of EBITDA for NuScale in 2028; Windham models just $29 million—a $95 million discrepancy that represents a 76.6% shortfall. Windham calculates that NuScale will burn through approximately $700 million in cumulative cash between 2026 and 2028 while managing only a single commercial project groundbreaking by 2028. With its flagship Utah Associated Municipal Power Systems (UAMPS) project terminated in late 2023 after projected power costs surged from $58/MWh to $89/MWh, NuScale’s pipeline relies heavily on prospective deals with RoPower in Romania and early-stage studies with the Tennessee Valley Authority (TVA).

This structural latency has forced Big Tech hyperscalers to reconsider their near-term datacenter power roadmaps. While Microsoft inked a 20-year power purchase agreement with Constellation Energy to restart the 835-megawatt Unit 1 at Three Mile Island (rebranded the Crane Clean Energy Center) for 2028, and Amazon Web Services secured a $650 million campus connection to Talen Energy’s 2.5-gigawatt Susquehanna plant, SMRs remain non-factors for datacenters demanding power before 2029.

Instead of funding pre-revenue nuclear balance sheets, institutional capital is pivoting aggressively toward high-efficiency datacenter hardware and direct rack-level power distribution architecture—where cash flows are immediate and compounding. Dell Technologies (NYSE: DELL), which trades at a modest 14.8 times forward earnings, reported second-quarter fiscal 2027 revenues of $46.97 billion, with operating income climbing to $5.39 billion and net income hitting $4.13 billion. Dell raised its full-year AI-optimized server guidance for the second consecutive quarter, backed by an order backlog exceeding $3.8 billion as enterprise clients install liquid-cooled racks pulling upwards of 100 kilowatts per cabinet.

Further down the engineering stack, Vicor Corporation (NASDAQ: VICR) illustrates the immediate monetization of the datacenter power bottleneck. Vicor, which manufactures high-density power distribution modules converting 48-volt rack architectures directly to sub-1-volt levels for advanced AI processors, generated $112.97 million in Q1 revenues with an operating margin of 14.9% and net income of $20.66 million. Rather than diluting shareholders with equity shelf registrations, Vicor is executing a $150 million share repurchase authorization while acquiring industrial real estate in New Hampshire to expand its proprietary ChiP (Converter hosted in Package) fabrication facilities.

The widening performance wedge between speculative SMR developers and profitable physical infrastructure exposes the central delusion of the AI energy trade. SMR balance sheets have raised over $3.2 billion in combined equity offerings since early 2024 to fund engineering payrolls and administrative overhead, while the physical equipment converting megawatts into compute inside hyperscale facilities is minting billions in GAAP operating income today. Until modular nuclear reactors pour commercial concrete and clear NRC technical reviews, public equity markets are treating their paperware not as the future of clean baseload power, but as high-beta dilution engines.

# The Gilded Cage: Inside Nvidia’s Rumored $12.9 Billion Raid on Hugging Face

August 26, 2026, was supposed to be a day of unalloyed triumph for Santa Clara. Nvidia Corp. reported its fiscal second-quarter 2027 earnings, posting a staggering $96.2 billion in revenue—up 106% year-over-year—and a GAAP net income of $59.7 billion, representing a 126% surge. The chipmaker’s data center unit alone brought in $89.0 billion, a 117% increase driven by insatiable demand for H100 and B200 accelerators. Yet, the financial headlines were immediately hijacked by a late-afternoon scoop from *The Information* reporting that Nvidia has agreed to acquire Hugging Face, the primary repository for open-source artificial intelligence, for $12.9 billion.

The rumored $12.9 billion transaction represents one of the most aggressive software-layer consolidations in technology history. At $12.9 billion, Nvidia is paying a multiple of approximately 86 times Hugging Face’s estimated annualized revenue of $150 million. By comparison, Microsoft’s watershed acquisition of GitHub in 2018 for $7.5 billion was valued at a relatively modest 25 to 30 times revenue. The deal represents a massive premium for a platform that has long been viewed as a loss-making public utility of the AI world. Hugging Face, which hosts over 1 million models, datasets, and "Spaces" web applications, is notorious for its crushing storage and egress bandwidth costs. Models like Meta's Llama 3.1 405B require tens of gigabytes per download, resulting in estimated egress bills running into millions of dollars monthly. The rumored purchase price marks a steep escalation from Hugging Face's Series D round in August 2023, which valued the company at $4.5 billion, and follows reports that Hugging Face turned down a $500 million investment offer from Nvidia in late 2025 at a $7 billion valuation.

To Nvidia, however, $12.9 billion is pocket change—representing less than three weeks of its $59.7 billion quarterly GAAP net income. Fintwit analysts immediately identified the transaction as a classic "loss leader" play. By subsidizing Hugging Face’s infrastructure costs, Nvidia ensures that the open-weight model ecosystem remains robust, which in turn fuels the market’s bottomless demand for Nvidia’s high-margin hardware. Under this view, Nvidia does not need Hugging Face to make money directly; it needs Hugging Face to keep the open-source developer ecosystem dependent on Nvidia silicon.

But within the developer communities that form the lifeblood of Hugging Face, the rumor has triggered widespread panic. On the subreddit r/LocalLLaMA, which serves as the hub for local and open-weight model deployment, developers reacted with immediate hostility. The community discussions centered on the fear of platform decay and corporate control. Users frequently warned of the threat of "enshittification"—the commercial degradation of free online platforms—and actively encouraged peers to begin archiving and downloading their favorite model weights before they face corporate censorship or are locked behind paywalls. 

The backlash centers on three critical vectors: licensing, censorship, and hardware compatibility. Hugging Face has historically been a neutral ground, hosting everything from commercial-grade models to highly experimental, uncensored weights. Under the ownership of a $3 trillion public giant like Nvidia, developers fear that controversial or uncensored models will be quietly purged to mitigate corporate liability and satisfy ESG mandates. 

Furthermore, the hardware compatibility implications are profound. While Hugging Face currently supports non-Nvidia runtimes like AMD’s ROCm and Intel’s oneAPI, developers worry Nvidia will build proprietary SDKs, such as TensorRT and Nvidia Inference Microservices (NIMs), directly into the platform's default download and run pipelines. If Hugging Face defaults to Nvidia-optimized runtimes while relegating AMD or Intel integrations to a secondary, bug-ridden status, it would effectively lock developers into Nvidia's CUDA ecosystem at the model-loading layer. This comes at a time when hyperscalers like Google, Amazon, and Microsoft are spending billions to develop custom AI silicon (TPUs, Trainium, and Maia chips) to break Nvidia's hardware monopoly.

Antitrust regulators are already sharpening their knives. Federal Trade Commission (FTC) Chair Andrew N. Ferguson, who took office in January 2025, and European Commissioner for Competition Teresa Ribera will undoubtedly subject the transaction to intense scrutiny. Nvidia has been quietly consolidating the AI software stack, acquiring Israeli GPU orchestration provider Run:ai for $700 million in April 2024 and deep-learning optimization startup Deci AI for $300 million in May 2024. However, buying Hugging Face—the central repository hosting models from Google, Meta, and Microsoft—would give Nvidia control over the primary distribution channel of its own hardware competitors' software. 

The ultimate tension of this acquisition lies in the fragile compromise of the AI boom. Tech giants have tolerated Hugging Face’s open-source ethos because it served as a neutral sandbox. If Nvidia moves Hugging Face inside its Santa Clara headquarters, that neutrality dissolves, threatening to fracture the open-source community into fragmented, self-hosted alternatives.

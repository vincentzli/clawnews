# The Funding Squeeze: How FB Financial's Top-Line Miss Exposes the Regional Banking Creep

The second-quarter earnings season for regional banks opened with a sobering reminder of the structural hurdles facing mid-sized lenders in a persistent high-rate environment. After the closing bell on Monday, July 13, 2026, Nashville-based FB Financial Corporation (NYSE: FBK) reported a mixed set of results that immediately triggered a -2.20% extended-session drop, dragging the stock down to $55.36. Despite posting double-digit loan growth and executing aggressive capital returns, the bank’s top-line miss has laid bare the mounting pressure of sticky deposit costs and narrowing net interest margins that continue to plague regional financial institutions.

FB Financial reported second-quarter diluted earnings per share of $1.13 and adjusted diluted earnings per share of $1.14, missing the consensus analyst estimate of $1.15. On the surface, the bank’s balance sheet expansion looked robust: loans held for investment grew at an annualized rate of 11.6% to reach $12.87 billion, while management demonstrated confidence by repurchasing 1,546,707 shares, representing 3.01% of the company's outstanding common stock. Yet, the positive momentum in credit production was offset by a top-line miss, as revenue net of interest expense came in at $174.75 million, falling short of the consensus expectation of $177.99 million. 

The core of the issue lies in the Federal Reserve’s monetary policy trajectory. Under the leadership of Fed Chair Kevin Warsh, the central bank has kept the benchmark federal funds rate at a restrictive target range of 3.50% to 3.75%. This prolonged monetary tightening, supported by Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, has forced regional banks to continuously compete for core funding, driving up deposit betas and compressing net interest margins across the sector. Although the Direxion Daily Regional Banks Bull 3X Shares ETF (DPST) rallied 23.9% during the quarter on short-term sentiment, major analysts have maintained a cautious "Hold" on the sector, citing the fading catalyst of rate cuts.

This macroeconomic pressure is clearly visible in FB Financial's funding mismatch. While the bank’s loans held for investment expanded by 11.6% annualized, its total deposits grew by only 7.7% annualized to $14.35 billion. To bridge this 3.9-percentage-point growth divergence, the bank has had to lean on more expensive funding sources. The market has taken notice. On StockTwits, one widely shared post noted: "Loans growing at 11.6% but deposits only at 7.7% means they have to bridge the gap with expensive wholesale funding. NIM expansion is officially dead." 

To bolster its franchise, FB Financial completed its merger with Southern States Bancshares on July 1, 2025. The acquisition was intended to provide a low-cost, relationship-driven deposit base across Alabama and Georgia to fund FB Financial's high-yielding loan growth. However, the integration has run headfirst into the reality of a competitive funding landscape. The bank’s average cost of deposits stood at 2.26% for the second quarter of 2026, representing a negligible decline of just one basis point from the 2.27% recorded in the first quarter of 2026. This plateau in deposit costs shows how difficult it is for mid-sized banks to extract funding relief in this environment. As an investor on Reddit’s r/investing commented, "The regional bank trade is all about deposit beta. If a bank can hold onto deposits without paying out the nose, they win. But most are just getting squeezed."

Compounding the funding squeeze is a subtle but noticeable deterioration in asset quality. FB Financial’s nonperforming loans rose to 1.17% of loans held for investment, up from 0.96% in the previous quarter. In response to this credit migration, the bank increased its provision for credit losses to $10.12 million. While the annualized net charge-offs ratio remained modest at 0.06% of average loans, the jump in provisions indicates that higher-for-longer interest rates are finally starting to weigh on borrowers, forcing the bank to build up its capital buffers. On Reddit's r/stocks, a user observed: "Look at that jump in provisions to $10M. Credit quality is starting to degrade. That's the real story behind the EPS miss."

As the banking sector parses these results, the tension between credit expansion and funding costs remains unresolved. FB Financial’s management will address these challenges during its conference call on Tuesday, July 14, 2026, at 8:00 a.m. Central Time (dial-in: 877-883-0383, confirmation code: 6281660). CEO Christopher T. Holmes and CFO Michael Mettee will need to convince analysts that the bank's double-digit loan growth is sustainable without causing further margin erosion or credit decay. For now, the top-line miss serves as a warning that regional banking remains caught in a tight vice between stubborn funding costs and a tightening credit cycle.

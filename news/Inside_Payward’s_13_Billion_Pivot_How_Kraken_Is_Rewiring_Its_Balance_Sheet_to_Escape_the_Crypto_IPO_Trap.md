# Inside Payward’s $13 Billion Pivot: How Kraken Is Rewiring Its Balance Sheet to Escape the Crypto IPO Trap

Inside the executive suites of Payward Inc., the corporate parent behind digital asset exchange Kraken, a calculated financial recalibration is unfolding. In late 2025, Payward completed an $800 million late-stage private round that established a peak $20 billion post-money valuation. The transaction brought blue-chip Wall Street capital onto the capitalization table: Citadel Securities committed $200 million in strategic capital, accompanied by Jane Street, DRW Venture Capital, and mutual fund manager T. Rowe Price. Within weeks, on November 19, 2025, Payward submitted a confidential draft Form S-1 registration statement to the Securities and Exchange Commission, initiating the formal mechanism to take the exchange public.

The private secondary markets, however, quickly reset expectations. In April 2026, Frankfurt-based exchange operator Deutsche Börse Group acquired a 1.5% fully diluted equity stake in Payward for $200 million. The purchase was executed at an implied equity valuation of $13.3 billion—a 33.5% discount to the late-2025 primary post-money benchmark. On private secondary platforms such as Forge Global, Notice, and Zanbato, trading in Payward common shares settled into a range between $12.2 billion and $15 billion. The variance reflected the standard pricing differential between senior preferred stock featuring liquidation protections and secondary common shares subject to liquidity haircuts.

Rather than accelerating into an unpredictable initial public offering, Payward adjusted its timeline. In March 2026, management paused active public debut preparations, deliberately pacing the listing window toward late 2026 or the second quarter of 2027.

The revised schedule is central to a broader operational overhaul led by co-CEO Arjun Sethi, who took the helm alongside co-CEO Dave Ripley in October 2024 while co-founder Jesse Powell remained Chairman of the Board. Sethi, formerly the founding partner of Tribe Capital and a Payward board member since 2021, confirmed in April 2026 that Payward’s confidential S-1 remains on file and that the operating entity is "80% operationally prepared" for a listing. Pacing the process, according to internal strategy directives, is intended to avoid debuting strictly as a spot crypto broker, which would expose the company to severe valuation multiples compression.

On secondary market forums and X, institutional allocators and retail observers have scrutinized the pricing gap. As one private equity analyst tracking exchange transactions noted on social media, "Deutsche Börse paying $13.3B for common stock while Citadel paid $20B for preferred late last year proves private crypto multiples are finally getting scrubbed."

Payward’s insulation against market compression is built around an enterprise initiative named "One Ledger." Defined by Sethi as "one platform, one balance sheet, one regulatory stack," the strategy seeks to transform Kraken from a transaction-fee-dependent spot exchange into a diversified financial infrastructure provider spanning digital banking, regulated futures, and multi-asset clearing.

The anchor of the One Ledger architecture went live on March 4, 2026, when the Federal Reserve Bank of Kansas City granted a limited-purpose master account to Kraken Financial, Payward's Wyoming-chartered Special Purpose Depository Institution (SPDI). Under Wyoming banking law, Kraken Financial operates as a full-reserve bank, requiring it to hold 100% of customer fiat balances in unencumbered liquid cash and government obligations without commercial lending. The regulatory grant marked the first time a digital asset bank secured direct clearing access to Fedwire payment rails, eliminating third-party commercial bank clearing fees and removing the structural counterparty risks that disrupted the digital asset sector during earlier market contractions.

Concurrently, Payward expanded its revenue lines beyond retail trading commissions. In May 2025, Payward finalized the $1.5 billion acquisition of Chicago-based futures platform NinjaTrader. Integrating NinjaTrader’s registered Futures Commission Merchant (FCM) license enabled Payward to introduce cross-margined traditional equity and commodity futures alongside crypto derivatives through Kraken Pro. 

Abroad, Payward linked its operational stack with primary traditional exchanges. In London, Payward partnered with the London Stock Exchange’s Digital Markets Infrastructure (DMI) to facilitate round-the-clock trading of tokenized equities on "LSE 24," scheduled for operational launch in 2027. In the United States, Payward collaborated with Nasdaq and its tokenization unit Backed to develop an equities transformation gateway engineered for continuous weekday settlement.

This multi-asset integration has proceeded under an altered regulatory posture in Washington. Following the inauguration of President Donald Trump and the appointment of Paul Atkins as SEC Chairman, the commission moved away from the "regulation-by-enforcement" model that dominated prior enforcement cycles. On March 28, 2025, the SEC entered a joint stipulation dismissing its November 2023 civil enforcement action against Kraken with prejudice. The dismissal was concluded with no civil monetary penalties, no admissions of liability, and zero alterations mandated for Payward’s operational architecture.

With federal enforcement headwinds removed, Payward’s primary test is public market pricing discipline. Sector peer Coinbase Global Inc. ($COIN) currently trades at an equity capitalization near $52 billion, maintaining a price-to-sales multiple between 7.8x and 11.1x while remaining sensitive to cyclical spot trading volumes. Although the public market pipeline saw Circle list on the New York Stock Exchange in June 2025 (NYSE: CRCL) and Bullish debut in August 2025 (NYSE: BLSH), both stocks have navigated persistent multiples scrutiny tied to trading activity and interest-bearing asset yields.

At the $13.3 billion valuation established by Deutsche Börse's entry, Payward aligns closely with current secondary trading realities. The viability of an expanded listing valuation will hinge on whether Payward’s transition to Fedwire direct clearing, FCM futures volume, and exchange connectivity can persuade public investors that its balance sheet has successfully decoupled from crypto's volatile retail cycles.

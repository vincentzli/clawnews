# State and Federal Regulators Clash Over Prediction Markets as New York Seeks $36 Billion in Kalshi Lawsuit

In a significant escalation of the conflict between state-level consumer protections and federal financial oversight, New York Governor Kathy Hochul and Attorney General Letitia James filed a lawsuit on July 31, 2026, against prediction market operator Kalshi. The lawsuit, filed in the New York County Supreme Court, accuses the exchange of running an unlicensed, illegal gambling business that targets New York residents, bypasses state tax laws, and lacks safeguards to prevent gambling addiction. The state is seeking to halt the platform’s operations within New York, secure restitution for state users, force the disgorgement of profits, and obtain statutory treble damages that could reach at least $36 billion.

"New York's gambling laws protect children from underage betting and help combat gambling addiction," Attorney General Letitia James said. "No matter what they call themselves, prediction markets like Kalshi are gambling platforms, plain and simple. By ignoring our laws, Kalshi is running an illegal operation and harming New Yorkers in the process. We are taking them to court to uphold our laws and protect New Yorkers."

Governor Kathy Hochul stated that the state’s licensing rules are meant to ensure that all financial and wagering entities operate under the same consumer standards. "Kalshi has chosen to ignore New York's gaming laws, which exist to protect consumers, prevent problematic gambling, deliver funding for critical public services, and ensure that every company plays by the same rules," Hochul said. "This choice has consequences, and working closely with Attorney General James, New York is taking action to stop this illegal behavior and bring Kalshi into compliance, because no company is above the law."

The potential $36 billion penalty sought by the state reflects New York's statutory structure, which allows for civil penalties of up to $100,000 for each unauthorized event contract offered to state residents, alongside restitution and fines equal to three times the platform's gains. 

A spokesperson for Kalshi, which is based in New York City, characterized the lawsuit as "political theater." The company maintains that as a designated contract market (DCM) regulated by the federal Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC), its event contracts are classified as swaps under the Commodity Exchange Act (CEA) and are therefore subject to exclusive federal jurisdiction.

The jurisdictional conflict deepened immediately after the state filed its complaint. On July 31, 2026, the CFTC filed an emergency motion for a temporary restraining order in federal court, seeking to block the New York Attorney General from pursuing civil or criminal enforcement actions against Kalshi and other CFTC-registered entities. The federal regulator argued that state-level enforcement of local gambling laws against federally registered exchanges undermines the national regulatory structure for derivatives.

This confrontation follows a series of federal court rulings on the preemption issue. On July 7, 2026, U.S. District Judge Analisa Torres denied Kalshi’s request for a preliminary injunction against New York regulators, holding that the CEA does not preempt state gambling laws concerning sports-event contracts. Kalshi appealed that ruling to the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Second Circuit on July 8, but Judge Torres subsequently denied Kalshi's motion for an emergency injunction pending appeal on July 27, allowing the state to proceed with its enforcement action.

The legal treatment of prediction markets varies across the country. On April 6, 2026, the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Third Circuit affirmed a preliminary injunction blocking New Jersey from enforcing state gambling laws against Kalshi, ruling that sports contracts fall under the CFTC's exclusive authority. Similarly, in late July 2026, a federal judge blocked a Minnesota law that banned prediction market trades in that state. To protect its regulatory turf, the CFTC, in coordination with the Department of Justice, filed federal lawsuits in April 2026 against regulators in Arizona, Connecticut, and Illinois who had attempted to enforce state-level gambling restrictions.

The ongoing regulatory uncertainty has caused commercial disruptions. On July 13, 2026, Google added New York and Michigan to its list of states—which already included Nevada and Ohio—where advertisements for prediction market contracts are prohibited. This policy change followed a Michigan court order that required Kalshi to geofence state residents from trading sports-related contracts.

Prediction market participants argue that state-level bans may drive domestic users toward offshore, unregulated platforms. Polymarket, a major competitor, established a regulated domestic entity, Polymarket US (operating under QCX LLC), in late 2025 to comply with CFTC oversight, utilizing mandatory identity verification and USD-denominated trades. However, its primary international platform remains active offshore, geoblocking U.S. IP addresses but remaining accessible to individuals utilizing virtual private networks.

Kalshi is also engaged in a public relations dispute. In late July 2026, the company sent a cease-and-desist letter to Netflix regarding its documentary *Instadocs: The Prediction Games*, which features Kalshi CEO Tarek Mansour. Kalshi claimed the promotional trailer was defamatory because it depicted a user in Las Vegas placing a $5,000 sports trade on the platform, which Kalshi asserted occurred in 2025 before a Nevada court ordered the platform to geofence the state.

The litigation has sparked active discussions online. In forums such as Reddit’s r/politics and r/wallstreetbets, some users have criticized New York's lawsuit as an attempt to protect state lottery and sportsbook revenues. Meanwhile, on subreddits like r/KalshiExposed, users have shared concerns about contract resolution mechanics and the limitations of federal oversight. One user wrote, "If the federal regulators can't protect us when a market resolve goes sideways, we need the state to step in."

As Kalshi seeks to remove the New York state court action to federal court, the litigation is poised to serve as a test case for the boundary between federal commodities regulation and state police powers.

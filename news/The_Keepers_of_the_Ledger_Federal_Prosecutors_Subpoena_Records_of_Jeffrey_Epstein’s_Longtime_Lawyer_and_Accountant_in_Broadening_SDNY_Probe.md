# The Keepers of the Ledger: Federal Prosecutors Subpoena Records of Jeffrey Epstein’s Longtime Lawyer and Accountant in Broadening SDNY Probe

For more than two decades, Darren Indyke and Richard Kahn occupied the most consequential administrative positions within Jeffrey Epstein’s global financial operations. Indyke, an attorney admitted to the New York bar who incorporated dozens of corporate entities spanning Manhattan to the U.S. Virgin Islands, and Kahn, a certified public accountant who audited ledgers and reconciled wire transfers, managed the operational mechanics of Epstein’s business affairs. When Epstein died in a federal detention cell at the Metropolitan Correctional Center in Manhattan on August 10, 2019, he left both men in charge of his fortune: under a last will and testament executed on August 8, 2019, the pair were designated sole co-executors of his estate and administrators of the 1953 Trust. The testamentary document also assigned significant conditional bequests—$50 million to Indyke and $25 million to Kahn—payable following the full resolution of outstanding creditor and victim claims.

That administrative perimeter is now facing renewed federal scrutiny.

In September 2026, federal prosecutors with the United States Attorney’s Office for the Southern District of New York (SDNY) issued grand jury subpoenas demanding financial records, tax filings, email communications, and transaction ledgers connected to dozens of offshore entities and corporate vehicles managed by the estate. According to court filings and people familiar with the matter, federal investigators are examining whether entities incorporated or administered by Indyke and Kahn—such as Virgin Islands-registered Southern Trust Company, Inc. and Plan D, LLC, alongside aviation and real estate holding companies like Hyperion Air, LLC and Great St. James, LLC—were utilized to facilitate sex-trafficking logistics, process financial settlements, and maintain restrictive non-disclosure agreements that shielded Epstein and prominent associates.

The federal grand jury subpoenas follow protracted civil enforcement actions that examined the boundary between professional representation and operational participation. In an enforcement action initiated by the U.S. Virgin Islands Department of Justice, which resulted in a $105 million settlement and the forfeiture of half the proceeds from the liquidation of Little Saint James and Great Saint James, territory prosecutors formally characterized the executors as "indispensable captains" of Epstein’s enterprise. Civil filings submitted by the Virgin Islands alleged that corporate structures facilitated travel logistics, processed funds categorized as administrative fees, and coordinated immigration arrangements, including marriages between associates and foreign nationals, to secure entry visas for young women brought to Epstein’s residences.

Counsel for the executors, Daniel H. Wiener of Hughes Hubbard & Reed LLP, maintains that neither Indyke nor Kahn has been designated an official target of the grand jury investigation, nor have either of the men received individual grand jury subpoenas for personal testimony. The defense contends that both men operated strictly within professional, administrative parameters, executing financial and corporate directives without criminal intent or contemporaneous awareness of Epstein’s sexual abuse and trafficking operations.

"Neither Mr. Indyke nor Mr. Kahn had any involvement in, or knowledge of, Mr. Epstein’s misconduct," Wiener stated, noting that both co-executors have complied with regulatory and congressional inquiries while overseeing the distribution of more than $121 million to over 135 survivors through the independent Epstein Victims’ Compensation Program, as well as $48 million in separate settlements. Under the executors' administration, the estate’s reported value has declined by roughly 79 percent, dropping from an initial inventory valuation of approximately $577 million in 2019 to an estimated $120 million, diminished by tax obligations, property maintenance, legal fees, and victim restitution funds.

Federal prosecutors are examining the degree to which corporate entities insulated illicit expenditures. A primary focus of the subpoenaed material is Southern Trust Company, a Virgin Islands entity that, together with Financial Trust Company, recorded more than $800 million in gross revenue between 1999 and 2018 under local economic development tax benefits. Grand jury demands have sought transaction records relating to Plan D, LLC, an entity used to acquire specific assets and route confidential transfers, and Hyperion Air, LLC, the operational entity that held title to private aircraft used to transport Epstein, his associates, and victims between the Caribbean, Florida, New York, and international destinations.

The SDNY grand jury process coincides with ongoing hearings before the House Oversight Committee examining the institutional response to Epstein’s crimes and the operational failures surrounding his August 2019 death. In March 2026, both executors provided closed-door deposition testimony under congressional subpoena. In his March 11 appearance, Kahn stated that he "was not aware of the nature or extent of Epstein's abuse of so many women until after Epstein's death," testifying that while he oversaw the daily financial accounting, he "did not see anything that suggested to [him] that Epstein was abusing or trafficking women or otherwise acting unlawfully." Kahn informed lawmakers that prominent individuals—including retail executive Les Wexner, investor Glenn Dubin, former technology executive Steven Sinofsky, and members of the Rothschild family—were commercial clients who paid fees to Epstein-affiliated entities.

Indyke testified before the committee on March 19, stating that no victim had ever accused him of participating in, observing, or being informed of sexual abuse, adding that he would have "severed all ties" had he known the full extent of Epstein's actions. When questioned by Committee Chairman James Comer regarding why he remained Epstein's attorney following Epstein's 2008 Florida conviction for procurement of minors for prostitution, Indyke stated that Epstein had presented the matter as a "one-time mistake" accompanied by remorse. Several committee members, including Democratic Representatives Dave Min, Jasmine Crockett, and Robert Garcia, challenged Indyke’s assertions, pointing to his role in executing non-disclosure agreements with survivors and drafting corporate documentation for entities that sustained Epstein’s household and travel logistics.

Congressional scrutiny has also centered on the documented institutional failures at the Metropolitan Correctional Center in Manhattan. A 128-page review by the Department of Justice Office of the Inspector General, directed by Inspector General Michael Horowitz, concluded that Epstein was left unattended due to widespread operational misconduct by the Bureau of Prisons. On duty during the overnight hours of August 9 to 10, correctional officers Tova Noel and Michael Thomas conducted personal online searches and fell asleep while failing to perform mandatory checks on Tier 9 South, later falsifying institutional logbooks. The review established that automated security cameras facing Epstein's cell recorded compromised or unusable video, and that Epstein's assigned cellmate, Efrain Reyes, was removed from the cell on the evening of August 9 without a replacement being assigned, in direct contravention of the facility's post-suicide-watch housing protocols.

Public frustration over executive branch disclosure has centered on the implementation of the Epstein Files Transparency Act, which passed Congress by overwhelming bipartisan margins and was signed into law by President Donald Trump in November 2025. Although the Department of Justice published roughly 3.5 million pages across five tranches through March 2026, government watchdog groups and congressional authors point out that approximately 3 million pages remain withheld or heavily redacted. In legal proceedings such as Phang v. Blanche, plaintiffs have challenged the Department of Justice and Deputy Attorney General Todd Blanche over assertions of executive privilege and law enforcement exemptions used to shield specific unredacted files, FBI interview memoranda, and records identifying potential co-conspirators.

The protracted document release has fostered extensive open-source analysis among legal researchers and citizen analysts. On public platforms and specialized indexing engines such as EpsteinExposed—a database engineered by "EricKeller2" that has organized 2.15 million released pages and mapped more than 1,500 associated individuals—researchers continue to correlate corporate filings with banking transactions. Commentators on X.com have debated the legal standard governing professional liability versus criminal conspiracy, with legal analysts observing that executing formal incorporation documents and routing wire transfers can establish criminal exposure under federal conspiracy statutes if prosecutors prove the administrators had constructive knowledge of the enterprise's illicit objectives.

As the grand jury in the Southern District of New York examines the corporate files, tax ledgers, and communications of Epstein's shell entities, federal prosecutors are evaluating whether the administrative mechanisms that supported Jeffrey Epstein’s financial network functioned solely as professional services, or whether they constituted actionable components of a federal criminal conspiracy.

# The Century Concession: Inside the Pentagon-Backed Deal Carving Up Venezuela’s Oil

When United States Energy Secretary Chris Wright landed at Simón Bolívar International Airport outside Caracas in late August 2026, he carried the blueprints for what President Donald Trump had already christened "the biggest oil deal in world history." Eight months after the January 3, 2026 U.S. military raid in Caracas that captured Nicolás Maduro and established Delcy Rodríguez as acting president, Washington was no longer merely overseeing an administrative transition. It was executing an unprecedented commercial restructuring of South America’s most reserve-rich petrostate.

At the center of this realignment sits North American Blue Energy Partners (NABEP), a privately held operating company chartered in Barbados and controlled by Venezuelan businessman Alejandro Betancourt López. Under the bilateral pacts concluded across August and September, Rodríguez’s interim government awarded NABEP a 100-year concession across 17 strategic oil blocks situated primarily in the Orinoco Heavy Oil Belt. The acreage contains an estimated 65 billion barrels of proven crude reserves—roughly equivalent to one-fifth of Venezuela’s total subsoil deposits. In exchange, NABEP committed to an infrastructure investment program of up to $100 billion to rebuild collapsed gathering centers, pipelines, and blending facilities, projecting $200 billion in royalties and tax revenues for Venezuelan state coffers over the first 25 years.

The transaction’s most unusual architecture does not reside in Caracas, but in the contractual links binding NABEP directly to the executive branch in Washington. Through the Pentagon’s Office of Strategic Capital (OSC), the U.S. government acquired a 35% non-voting equity holding in NABEP’s corporate parent via penny warrants, securing a direct financial interest without an initial congressional appropriation. Parallel to the defense equity holding, the State Department executed an off-take agreement granting Washington the right to purchase 20% of NABEP’s net crude output at production cost to replenish the U.S. Strategic Petroleum Reserve, backed by an exclusive right of first refusal over the remaining 80%. Under the operating compact, U.S. citizens must hold a majority of NABEP’s board seats, and Washington retains formal veto power over principal board appointments.

"We need to have that leverage and that control of those oil sales to drive the changes that simply must happen in Venezuela," Secretary Wright stated during his Caracas mission, describing the arrangement as a pragmatic mechanism to replace conflict with commercial integration while displacing Chinese and Russian influence from the country's energy grid. In Caracas, acting President Rodríguez defended the pact during the formal signing ceremony, stating: "I trust that the binational agreement will also prove beneficial for the people of the United States. Venezuela is ready to welcome these investments that will boost the country's development."

The commercial setup followed an abrupt corporate restructuring compelled by federal sanctions. NABEP was founded in April 2024 as a joint venture between Betancourt and Florida oil developer Harry Sargeant III. During the spring of 2026, the U.S. Treasury Department imposed asset freezes on Sargeant’s offshore entity, Bluewave Properties Ltd. Under regulatory pressure, Sargeant divested his minority share in August for approximately $300 million to entities affiliated with Betancourt. Following the buyout, Betancourt publicly celebrated the finalized pact, stating that "Venezuela is blessed with an abundance of natural resources, hardworking people and untapped potential. This transaction will unleash that potential to the great benefit of both Venezuelans and Americans."

While Washington structured NABEP as the core corporate vehicle of the pact, Secretary Wright coordinated concurrent investment agreements designed to elevate aggregate Venezuelan crude output beyond 1.5 million barrels per day. Chevron finalized a $7 billion capital plan to expand its ongoing joint ventures, targeting 600,000 barrels per day by 2031. Italian energy major Eni signed a 25-year production accord covering the Junín 5 block, while GE Vernova concluded contracts to rehabilitate the regional thermoelectric stations and transmission networks required to sustain artificial lift pumps across the interior fields.

Despite executive backing in both capitals, the legal foundation of the agreements faces immediate challenge. Venezuelan constitutional attorneys point directly to Article 150 of the 1999 Constitution, which stipulates that contracts of national public interest entered into with foreign entities must receive formal approval from the National Assembly. Because Rodríguez enacted the 100-year concession through executive decree without parliamentary debate, legal scholars contend the grants remain vulnerable to immediate revocation by any future legislature or subsequent administration.

In Washington, the arrangement has prompted immediate resistance from congressional oversight panels. Representative Joaquin Castro, Ranking Member of the House Foreign Affairs Subcommittee on the Western Hemisphere, moved to subpoena administration records concerning the vetting and selection of Betancourt. Betancourt was previously scrutinized in federal law enforcement probes related to a $1.2 billion PDVSA embezzlement case in South Florida, though he was never formally charged in the United States, and remains under inquiry in Spain and Switzerland. Lawmakers also questioned reports that U.S. officials had intervened with Swiss prosecutors to resolve outstanding judicial inquiries against Betancourt ahead of the August agreement. Representative Raja Krishnamoorthi formally demanded documentation regarding potential conflicts of interest among administration negotiators, cautioning against the use of federal national security authorities to shield private operators.

On social media platforms and discussion boards, the terms of the transaction ignited swift pushback. Across threads in Reddit’s r/politics, users drew comparisons between the 100-year operating horizon and historic foreign concessions in the developing world, pointing out that an executive decree bypassing Article 150 leaves private capital exposed to future sovereign default, while questioning the ethics of the Pentagon securing equity stakes in private energy consortiums.

Technical realities on the ground in the Orinoco Belt pose additional operational hazards. The 17 concession blocks contain predominantly extra-heavy crude grading between 8 and 10 degrees API. Extracting and moving this dense bitumen requires intensive steam recovery, stable natural gas supplies, and substantial imports of light naphtha diluent before the product can be piped to coastal upgraders in Jose. Environmental monitoring groups, including Mongabay, have noted that these fields intersect sensitive tributaries and the ancestral lands of the Warao and Kari'ña Indigenous peoples, where decades of neglected maintenance have resulted in uncontained soil spills and widespread gas flaring.

Even so, the diplomatic normalization between Washington and Caracas has accelerated rapidly. On September 16, 2026, President Trump officially removed Venezuela from the U.S. list of nations that have "failed demonstrably" to fulfill international narcotics agreements—a punitive status Caracas had occupied since 2005. While Venezuela remains on the statutory register of major transit nations, the elimination of the demonstrative failure sanction reflected what administration officials cited as cooperation from the Rodríguez government, specifically highlighting the dismantling of the Tren de Aragua syndicate and the elimination of its leader, Héctor "Niño" Guerrero. The policy reversal clears the stage for a scheduled bilateral meeting between President Trump and acting President Rodríguez on the sidelines of the United Nations General Assembly in New York in late September, solidifying a geopolitical pact where subsoil exploitation, sovereign control, and federal equity have become inextricably linked.

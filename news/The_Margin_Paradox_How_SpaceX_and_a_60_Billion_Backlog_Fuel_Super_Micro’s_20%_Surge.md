# The Margin Paradox: How SpaceX and a $60 Billion Backlog Fuel Super Micro’s 20% Surge

Shares of Super Micro Computer, Inc. (SMCI) surged 19.8% on July 22, 2026, closing at $30.56, up from $25.50 the previous day, on the back of a preliminary fiscal fourth-quarter business update that stunned Wall Street. The primary catalyst was an unexpected expansion in projected GAAP and non-GAAP gross margins to a range of 15% to 17%. This figure effectively doubles the company’s previous Q4 guidance of 8.2% to 8.4%, and marks a significant rebound from the non-GAAP gross margin of 10.1% reported in Q3 of fiscal 2026. What makes the market’s euphoric reaction surprising is that the margin expansion occurred even as preliminary revenues are projected to land near the low end of the company's previously guided range of $11.0 billion to $12.5 billion, representing a modest sequential increase from the $10.24 billion in revenue recorded in the third quarter of fiscal 2026.

Behind the sudden rally lies a massive spike in new order bookings and a key data center partnership with Elon Musk’s AI infrastructure efforts. Supermicro disclosed that it received over $60 billion in new orders during the fourth fiscal quarter, pushing its order backlog to unprecedented levels. This development comes on the heels of a highly publicized supply chain dispute. On July 20, 2026, Taiwan’s Economic Daily News published a report claiming that SpaceX had awarded a $52 billion contract for 13,000 NVIDIA GB300 server racks to Foxconn, which would have displaced existing partners like Dell Technologies and Supermicro. Elon Musk quickly intervened, responding on X, "This is fake news." The denial stabilized the shares of Supermicro and Dell before the July 21 preliminary update confirmed Supermicro’s central role in the space-and-AI conglomerate's buildout.

Supermicro’s CEO, Charles Liang, confirmed that the company is co-building "another brand-new gigawatt-scale AI data center" for SpaceX and its associated AI initiatives, building on the initial success of the "Colossus" supercomputer facility in Memphis, Tennessee. The partnership has galvanized the retail trading community, which had been nursing losses after buying SMCI shares at higher valuations earlier in the summer of 2026. On the Reddit forum r/wallstreetbets, retail sentiment shifted dramatically. One user posted a high-stakes bet under the title "$360k SMCI full port leap all in," sparking intense debates about the decay of options premiums and the volatility of server hardware stocks. Another retail investor posted a thread titled "SMCI 100k in," detailing a position of 4,200 shares with a sell limit of $124, arguing that "with margins doubling to 15% and a $60 billion backlog, a forward P/E of 12 makes this stock the cheapest play in the AI sector."

However, professional analysts remain divided on the long-term sustainability of Supermicro's pricing power and its execution capacity. While liquid-cooled server racks command a premium, competitors like Dell and Hewlett Packard Enterprise (HPE) are scaling their own liquid-cooled infrastructure, threatening to commoditize the technology. Furthermore, the mismatch between Supermicro’s $60 billion in new orders and its estimated Q4 revenue of roughly $11.0 billion indicates significant execution bottlenecks. In the third quarter of fiscal 2026, revenue fell sequentially to $10.24 billion from $12.64 billion in the second quarter, which management blamed on customer site readiness and component shortages. If Supermicro cannot convert its $60 billion backlog into recognized revenue quickly, its high-margin mix could prove temporary. 

Adding to the complexity are the calendar risks facing both Supermicro and its marquee partner, SpaceX. SpaceX completed its landmark initial public offering on June 12, 2026, listing under the Nasdaq ticker SPCX at an IPO price of $135 per share. After peaking above $200 in mid-June, SPCX shares have fallen below their IPO price. A major test is scheduled for August 6, 2026, when SpaceX's initial lockup period expires and 911.5 million insider shares become eligible for sale. This lockup cliff arrives just two days after SpaceX's inaugural quarterly earnings report, scheduled for August 4, 2026. Supermicro's own audited fourth-quarter and full-year results are scheduled for release on August 11, 2026. Wall Street will look for clarity on whether an ongoing independent board review regarding export-control matters could alter the preliminary gross margin figures or impact long-term backlog delivery.

# The $6.7 Billion Compression: Why Payward Shelved Kraken’s S-1 and Pushed Its IPO to Mid-2027

When Payward Inc. confidentially submitted its draft Form S-1 registration statement to the Securities and Exchange Commission on November 19, 2025, the filing represented what management viewed as the final corporate milestone in a multi-year transition toward public capital markets. Coming immediately on the heels of an $800 million pre-IPO financing round that established a peak private valuation of $20 billion, the parent company of the Kraken cryptocurrency exchange appeared positioned to execute the largest digital-asset listing since Coinbase Global Inc.’s direct listing in April 2021. The regulatory obstruction that had previously clouded the company’s trajectory had cleared eight months prior, when the SEC formally stipulated to the dismissal with prejudice of its civil enforcement action against Payward in March 2025 without monetary penalties or admission of liability.

By September 2026, however, Payward’s public roadmap had unraveled. According to corporate finance filings, secondary transaction records, and people familiar with the underwriting syndicate, Payward has formally pushed its prospective initial public offering to at least the second quarter of 2027. The postponement follows an acute 33.5% valuation compression, benchmarked in April 2026 when German exchange operator Deutsche Börse Group closed a $200 million secondary equity transaction for a 1.5% fully diluted interest in Payward. Because the transaction consisted entirely of secondary share purchases from existing holders, no new growth capital entered Payward's treasury, while the implied equity valuation reset downward to $13.3 billion—a $6.7 billion erasure of paper wealth within five months.

The retrenchment comes despite public assertions of operational preparedness by executive leadership. In April 2026, Kraken co-CEO Arjun Sethi confirmed at the Semafor World Economy Summit that the confidential SEC filing remained active, and subsequently told attendees at the Consensus conference in Miami that the exchange was "about 80% ready" to list. Yet internal deliberations with lead underwriting advisers—including institutions positioned for lead-left roles such as Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley—confronted an intractable structural reality: Payward’s revenue profile remains fundamentally ill-suited for public market pricing in an environment defined by trading volume stagnation and fee compression.

The vulnerability stems from Kraken’s extreme structural exposure to spot transaction fees when contrasted with its principal domestic competitor. During the 2022 to 2025 market cycles, Coinbase systematically re-engineered its revenue architecture to decouple earnings from trading velocity. By mid-2026, non-transaction streams accounted for approximately half of Coinbase’s net revenue, anchored by high-margin custodial operations for major institutional spot Bitcoin and Ethereum exchange-traded products, revenue-sharing agreements on Circle’s USDC reserves, and sequencer fee capture from its layer-2 Base blockchain.

In stark contrast, Payward’s income statement remains overwhelmingly dependent on transactional churn. Historically, upwards of 80% to 85% of Kraken’s top-line revenue has derived from spot trading commissions. Throughout 2026, that core cash generator encountered significant headwinds across global markets. As digital-asset volatility tapered and spot volumes across centralized exchanges contracted between 25% and 40% year-over-year, aggressive taker-fee discounting and fee wars initiated by offshore and domestic competitors severely eroded transaction margins. For a business executing trades at retail fee schedules ranging between 16 and 26 basis points, volume deceleration compounded margin decay, projecting a deteriorating top line directly onto the prospective S-1 disclosures.

Underwriting analyses prepared by Wall Street syndicate desks indicated that floating a transaction-reliant crypto exchange under contracting trading multiples would necessitate a severe pricing haircut during an IPO roadshow. Public market asset managers, already cautious following the muted post-listing trading performances of digital asset infrastructure firms like BitGo earlier in 2026, indicated an unwillingness to value cyclical exchange fees at premium fintech multiples. To avoid an impaired debut or an outright down-round IPO below the $13.3 billion Deutsche Börse benchmark, advisers recommended postponing the listing until operational diversification could be established in audited financial statements.

Faced with these valuation headwinds, Sethi—who assumed the co-CEO post alongside Dave Ripley in late 2024 while retaining his role as a general partner at Tribe Capital—has overseen an aggressive capital deployment strategy designed to reshape Payward's income mix. Rather than forcing a listing, Payward has allocated substantial balance sheet reserves toward strategic M&A, acquiring retail multi-asset brokerage NinjaTrader, CFTC-regulated clearing exchange Bitnomial, and cross-border digital payments platform Reap Technologies. The objective is to build an integrated financial infrastructure entity spanning equities, derivatives, clearing, and institutional settlement, thereby diluting Kraken's reliance on spot crypto trading before re-engaging the SEC in 2027.

The mid-2027 deferral has nonetheless amplified liquidity strain among Payward’s investor base and employee option holders. On secondary private equity desks including Forge Global and the Nasdaq Private Market, Payward shares have traded at persistent discounts to primary round benchmarks. Transactions through August and early September 2026 recorded clearing prices between $29.25 and $33.64 per share, well below the approximately $50 unit value implied by the late-2025 $20 billion valuation. Early institutional investors, including Hummingbird Ventures and Blockchain Capital, alongside long-tenured employees holding vested options since the 2021 bull market, face an extended lockup period in illiquid shares.

This protracted timeline has reignited public debate across retail and industry forums. On Reddit’s r/CryptoCurrency, users widely criticized the repeated timeline extensions, with prominent discussions highlighting that Kraken has signaled prospective public listings dating back to 2021 without execution. On X, financial analysts and secondary market traders have characterized the delay as emblematic of the broader divergence in private tech valuations, noting that until non-spot revenue diversification materializes on Payward's balance sheet, private secondary markets will continue to enforce the 33% discount established by institutional capital.

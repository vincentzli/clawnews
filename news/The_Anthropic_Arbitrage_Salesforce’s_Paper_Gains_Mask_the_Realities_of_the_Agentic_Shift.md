# The Anthropic Arbitrage: Salesforce’s Paper Gains Mask the Realities of the Agentic Shift

On the afternoon of August 26, 2026, Salesforce Inc. (NYSE: CRM) reported its fiscal second-quarter 2027 earnings, sending shares surging 13% in after-hours trading to $231.70 from a regular session close of $205.62. On the surface, the numbers looked spectacular: adjusted earnings per share (EPS) came in at $5.90, demolishing the Wall Street consensus estimate of $3.27, while GAAP diluted EPS printed at $4.29. Total revenue rose 11% year-over-year to $11.35 billion, slightly beating the $11.32 billion consensus. However, a deeper dive into the consolidated statement of operations reveals that Salesforce’s blockbuster quarter was less a triumph of organic enterprise software sales and more a reflection of private-market financial engineering. A pre-tax paper gain of $2.61 billion on the company’s strategic investment in AI startup Anthropic provided a massive tailwind, accounting for $2.53 per share of the adjusted bottom line. Without this valuation markup, Salesforce’s adjusted EPS would have been a far more modest $3.37.

Under U.S. GAAP guidelines—specifically ASC 321—companies must recognize unrealized gains and losses on equity investments in their net income each quarter. Salesforce Ventures’ early bets on Anthropic, which began with a modest $50 million investment in 2023 and grew to an estimated $5 billion position, triggered this accounting windfall following Anthropic’s massive Series H funding round on May 28, 2026. That round, which raised $65 billion and was led by a consortium including Altimeter Capital, Dragoneer Investment Group, Greenoaks Capital, and Sequoia Capital, valued Anthropic at $965 billion, up from $380 billion in February. This observable price change forced Salesforce to write up the carrying value of its Anthropic stake by $2.61 billion. On forums like Reddit’s r/ValueInvesting, this accounting treatment sparked fierce debate. One Redditor noted that "unrealized paper gains from a private startup valuation to beat earnings estimates is peak 2026 bubble behavior," while others argued that Salesforce is transforming into a venture capital fund that happens to sell customer relationship management (CRM) software on the side. The tension between paper prosperity and organic operational growth remains a central focus for analysts dissecting the quality of these earnings.

While the Anthropic write-up provided the bottom-line fireworks, Salesforce’s core operational business showed signs of mature growth and shifting corporate priorities. The company’s newly disaggregated reporting structure revealed that subscription and support revenue reached $10.82 billion, up 12% year-over-year (11% in constant currency). However, professional services and other revenue fell 3.9% year-over-year to $525 million, compared to $546 million in the prior-year period. This decline reflects a broader macroeconomic trend under Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent’s fiscal regime, where enterprise customers have aggressively scaled back discretionary consulting spend to fund capital-intensive AI infrastructure. To offset this consulting slowdown, Salesforce announced a $3.6 billion acquisition of customer service automation startup Fin and secured a new $1.6 billion contract with the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs. Yet, the current remaining performance obligation (cRPO) grew 14% to $33.5 billion, showing that Salesforce is still successfully locking in customers, even as the organic seat-based software growth that drove the company’s historic multiples begins to plateau.

The core anxiety hanging over Salesforce throughout 2026 has been the threat of the "SaaS apocalypse"—the theory that generative AI agents will eventually eliminate the need for seat-based software licenses by automating front-office tasks. If an AI agent can autonomously resolve support tickets and orchestrate workflows, the rationale for paying for hundreds of human seats disappears. On the Q2 earnings call, Salesforce CEO Marc Benioff aggressively dismissed this narrative as "nonsense," pointing to near-record low customer attrition and a 210% year-over-year surge in combined annual recurring revenue (ARR) for Agentforce and Data 360, which reached nearly $3.9 billion. Agentforce ARR alone crossed $1.5 billion, with bookings more than doubling quarter-over-quarter. According to management, Salesforce delivered 3.2 billion "Agentic Work Units" (AWUs) in Q2, representing a 97% sequential increase. This transition from seat-based pricing to outcome-based consumption (charging per agentic task completed) is Salesforce’s primary defense against obsolescence. But critics on Reddit’s r/wallstreetbets remain skeptical, with users questioning whether these early AWU bookings represent genuine enterprise adoption or are simply promotional conversions of existing software credits.

To accelerate agentic adoption, Salesforce announced a major expansion of its relationship with Anthropic, dubbed "Claudeforce." The partnership’s centerpiece is the "Salesforce in Claude" plugin, which integrates Salesforce’s CRM data and workflows directly into Anthropic’s Claude interface. Launching with 37 pre-built sales skills, the plugin allows users to draft emails and perform pipeline reviews within Claude. Benioff framed this move as meeting users where they work, using Salesforce's "AIforce" as an "enterprise harness" to manage data security within the "Salesforce Trust Boundary." Yet this strategy highlights a fundamental tension: by embedding its capabilities inside Claude, Salesforce risks ceding the primary user interface to Anthropic. In a macroeconomic environment where Federal Reserve Chair Kevin Warsh has maintained tight monetary policy, enterprise buyers are reluctant to double-pay for both LLM licenses and CRM seats. If Claude becomes the primary interface for business operations, Salesforce could find itself relegated to the role of a backend database provider, compressing its valuation multiple.

This backend relegation makes Salesforce’s balance sheet heavily dependent on the private valuation of its AI partners. Anthropic’s financial trajectory has been staggering: its annualized revenue run rate surged from $47 billion in May 2026 to $65 billion by the end of July, and the company filed a confidential S-1 on June 1, 2026, targeting an IPO as early as September or October at a projected $2 trillion valuation. While these numbers suggest immense market momentum, they also highlight a massive valuation multiple of nearly 30 times annualized revenue. If Anthropic’s public debut struggles to achieve this $2 trillion target under the administration of President Donald Trump and Vice President JD Vance, or if the broader AI venture market experiences a correction, Salesforce’s balance sheet could face severe mark-to-market write-downs. Under ASC 321, just as a $65 billion private round can spark a $2.61 billion paper gain, a public market correction can instantly erase it. For now, Salesforce has used this paper miracle to raise its full-year fiscal 2027 adjusted EPS guidance to a range of $16.67 to $16.71. But for investors looking past the headline beat, the question remains whether Salesforce is building a sustainable agentic future or simply riding the tailwinds of a private AI bubble.

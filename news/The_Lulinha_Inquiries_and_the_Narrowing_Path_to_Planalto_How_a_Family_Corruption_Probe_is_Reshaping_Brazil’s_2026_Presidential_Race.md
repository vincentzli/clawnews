# The Lulinha Inquiries and the Narrowing Path to Planalto: How a Family Corruption Probe is Reshaping Brazil’s 2026 Presidential Race

BRASÍLIA — The official start of Brazil’s presidential campaign on August 15, 2026, was supposed to showcase Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva’s achievements in stabilizing the economy and restoring democratic norms. Instead, the race has transformed into a high-stakes legal and political drama that threatens the leftist incumbent’s re-election bid. According to the latest AtlasIntel/Bloomberg poll released on August 31, 2026, Lula’s lead over his right-wing challenger, Senator Flávio Bolsonaro of the Liberal Party (PL), has shrunk to just 4.5 percentage points in a simulated second-round runoff, down from a 6.3 percentage point lead in July. The poll of 5,014 voters, conducted between August 25 and 30 with a margin of error of 1.0 percentage point, reveals Lula leading the first-round vote on October 4 with 43.4%, followed by Flávio Bolsonaro at 33.7%, and third-way contenders Augusto Cury at 7.8% and Renan Santos at 7.6%. This sudden narrowing is directly linked to the Supreme Federal Court’s (STF) authorization of multiple federal investigations into Lula’s eldest son, Fábio Luís da Silva, widely known as "Lulinha."

These allegations strike at the core of the Workers' Party's political narrative, allowing opponents to challenge the administration's anti-corruption stance. The Federal Police are currently probing whether the President's son exploited his family connections to facilitate a lucrative, non-competitive business scheme involving medical cannabis. According to intercepts obtained by the Federal Police, the scheme centered on an attempt to secure a massive contract with the Ministry of Health and Dataprev, the state-owned social security database manager. The contract, valued at an estimated R$ 627 million, was designed to bypass public bidding requirements to procure cannabidiol products from World Cannabis, a private firm connected to Antônio Carlos Camilo Antunes, known as "Careca do INSS." Antunes is a central target in Operation Sem Desconto, a sweeping federal investigation into systemic fraud and embezzlement within Brazil's social security administration.

Financial intelligence reports from the Council for Financial Activities Control (Coaf) revealed that World Cannabis moved R$ 6.1 million between 2023 and 2025, a sum highly disproportionate to the company's declared capital. The link between World Cannabis and the presidential palace allegedly went through Roberta Luchsinger, an heiress and influential lobbyist who is a close associate of Lulinha. Federal investigators identified R$ 249,000 in direct bank transfers from Luchsinger to Marco Aurélio Santana Ribeiro, nicknamed "Marcola," who served as Lula’s chief of staff until his sudden departure on July 21, 2026. While Marcola was officially reassigned to Lula’s campaign, his exit was overshadowed by disclosures that Luchsinger had paid for his personal expenses and purchased jewelry on his behalf. The Federal Police also intercepted messages between Marcola and Luchsinger discussing the purchase of a 100,000-euro painting and a draft contract for the cannabis procurement scheme. Marcola has denied wrongdoing, asserting the R$ 249,000 was a personal loan that has since been repaid.

The investigation has also expanded into Dataprev, where the STF has authorized the Federal Police to investigate whether Lulinha used his influence to secure technology contracts for Cleber Ribas de Oliveira, a partner at 3Structure IT. In return, Oliveira allegedly financed Lulinha’s domestic and international travel. The defense team for Fábio Luís, led by prominent attorney Marco Aurélio de Carvalho, has vehemently denied all allegations, calling the judicial inquiries a "fishing expedition"—or *pesca probatória*—designed to manufacture scandal ahead of the October vote. Carvalho has argued that the transactions mentioned did not result in signed public contracts or financial loss to the state. In a tactical win for Lulinha’s defense, STF Minister André Mendonça on August 21 ordered the Federal Police to investigate the selective leaking of confidential files to the media, emphasizing that the probe must focus on state officials who breached custody rather than the journalists reporting the leaks.

The widening scandal has thrown Lula’s campaign onto the defensive. In public statements, the President has walked a tightrope, declaring that "nobody is above the law" and promising no executive interference, while simultaneously dismissing the allegations as "speculation" and admitting only that a former aide "had erred." 

Senator Flávio Bolsonaro has seized upon the Lulinha inquiries to mobilize the conservative electorate. With the Liberal Party rallying behind him, the Senator has sought to frame the election as a referendum on the Workers’ Party’s ethical record. However, Flávio’s own campaign is conducted under the heavy shadow of his father, former President Jair Bolsonaro. The elder Bolsonaro was sentenced by the STF on November 25, 2025, to 27 years and three months in prison for his role in the January 8 attempted coup, the violent disruption of the democratic state, and criminal association. Due to severe health complications, including aspiration bronchopneumonia, Jair Bolsonaro is currently serving his sentence under humanitarian house arrest in Brasília, monitored by STF Minister Alexandre de Moraes. Under the terms of his detention, his political rights are suspended, and he is strictly prohibited from making public statements or communicating with his son Flávio during the campaign period. The STF went as far as suspending general visits to the former president in July after a political letter was leaked from his residence, only restoring visits for Carlos and Jair Renan on August 21 while maintaining the communication firewall with Flávio.

Despite these restrictions, Flávio Bolsonaro has successfully energized the Bolsonarista base by drawing parallels between the investigations. The narrowing poll numbers show that the strategy is working. The August 31 AtlasIntel poll indicates that Lula’s runoff lead has fallen to 4.5 percentage points (47.1% to 42.6%), well within striking distance for the opposition. As the campaign intensifies, the battle for Planalto is no longer being fought just on the economic recovery, but in the courtrooms of the STF.

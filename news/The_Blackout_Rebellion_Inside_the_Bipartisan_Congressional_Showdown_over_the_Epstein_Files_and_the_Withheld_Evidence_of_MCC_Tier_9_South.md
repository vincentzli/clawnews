# The Blackout Rebellion: Inside the Bipartisan Congressional Showdown over the Epstein Files and the Withheld Evidence of MCC Tier 9 South

In the halls of the Capitol, an unusual bipartisan rebellion has sparked a direct constitutional showdown between Congress and the Department of Justice. The flashpoint is H.R. 9694, the Epstein Files Transparency Act II (EFTA II), authored by Representative Thomas Massie (R-KY) and Representative Ro Khanna (D-CA). The two lawmakers are actively driving a 218-signature discharge petition to bypass committee jurisdiction and force a floor vote before House Speaker Mike Johnson adjourns the chamber on September 17.

The legislative fight follows the enactment of Public Law 119-38, the original Epstein Files Transparency Act signed into law by President Donald Trump on November 19, 2025, after clearing the House by a 427–1 margin. Under that statute, the Department of Justice was legally obligated to disclose all unclassified records, investigative memos, and communication logs related to Jeffrey Epstein in an indexable, public database. Yet by the end of January 2026, when the department delivered approximately 3.5 million pages, lawmakers and transparency advocates discovered widespread blackouts covering significant portions of the material. Congressional committees calculate that approximately 3 million additional responsive pages remain sequestered inside federal archives under agency assertions of privacy, grand jury secrecy, and law enforcement sensitivities.

The dispute escalated sharply on August 31, 2026, during a House floor session. Invoking Article I, Section 6 of the United States Constitution—the Speech or Debate Clause, which protects members of Congress against civil defamation actions for statements made during legislative proceedings—Representative Massie entered 14 prominent names into the Congressional Record as alleged unindicted co-conspirators. The names spanned global high finance, retail, and entertainment: Andrew Mountbatten-Windsor (formerly Prince Andrew), retail billionaire Les Wexner, private equity executive Leon Black, former JPMorgan and Barclays executive Jes Staley, illusionist David Copperfield, Hyatt Hotels executive chairman Tom Pritzker, hedge fund founder Glenn Dubin, Fiat heir Lapo Elkann, luxury entrepreneur Frédéric Fekkai, Italian industrialist Edoardo Teodorani-Fabbri, associate Ramsey Elkholy, Epstein’s longtime executive assistant Lesley Groff, alongside two deceased figures, French modeling scout Jean-Luc Brunel and associate Daniel Siad.

Massie stated on the floor that his action was designed to "shame the Department of Justice" for failing to pursue criminal co-conspirators while withholding vital investigative files from the public. Massie cautioned that his statements represented legislative assertions rather than court findings, noting that inclusion in Department of Justice files does not inherently establish criminal culpability. The floor speech spurred rapid cross-platform engagement on social media. Legal commentators on X observed that Massie’s invocation of the Speech or Debate Clause effectively dismantled seven years of administrative insulation surrounding those investigative files. On Reddit forums including r/politics, discussions centered on the procedural mathematics of the discharge petition, with users noting that congressional leadership faces pressure as signatures approach the 218-member threshold prior to the September 17 adjournment.

H.R. 9694 seeks to dismantle the executive branch’s unilateral control over redaction standards. Co-sponsored in the House by Representative Teresa Leger Fernández (D-NM) and introduced in the Senate by Jeff Merkley (D-OR) and Ben Ray Luján (D-NM), the bill introduces enforceable judicial recourse: it grants Epstein's victims legal standing to sue the Attorney General for access to their own complete investigative files; it authorizes state attorneys general to subpoena and inspect unredacted federal files to initiate state-level criminal prosecutions if the federal government declines to act; and it allows members of Congress to petition federal courts to review disputed withholdings.

Simultaneously, the battle has spread to federal courtrooms. In Freedom of Information Act proceedings, federal judges have ordered the Department of Justice to produce formal Vaughn indices—document-by-document justifications specifying the statutory basis for every applied redaction. Department attorneys have opposed these orders, contending that producing detailed justifications across millions of withheld records would overwhelm department personnel and violate separation-of-powers boundaries regarding executive prosecutorial records.

This transparency dispute directly intersects with unresolved forensic questions surrounding Epstein’s death on August 10, 2019, at the Metropolitan Correctional Center (MCC) in Lower Manhattan. Epstein died in Tier 9 South, the facility's Special Housing Unit (SHU). In June 2023, Department of Justice Inspector General Michael Horowitz published a 120-page report confirming severe systemic breakdowns at the MCC. The investigation substantiated that the two on-duty officers, Tova Noel and Michael Thomas, failed to conduct mandatory 30-minute rounds, fell asleep at their post, and browsed the internet for consumer goods. Although both officers were criminally charged with falsifying official logs, the U.S. Attorney’s Office for the Southern District of New York approved deferred prosecution agreements that led to the dismissal of all criminal counts against them in December 2021.

Forensic debates surrounding the physical autopsy remain contentious. While former New York City Chief Medical Examiner Dr. Barbara Sampson concluded that Epstein died by suicide via hanging, board-certified forensic pathologist Dr. Michael Baden, who observed the examination on behalf of Epstein’s brother Mark Epstein, documented multiple breaks to the neck skeleton, specifically fractures to the hyoid bone and both thyroid cartilage horns. Baden stated that such multiple laryngeal fractures occur far more frequently in homicidal manual strangulation cases than in self-inflicted hangings using a bedsheet tied to a low bunk.

Technical anomalies regarding prison surveillance systems further animate the dispute over the withheld records. Inspector General Horowitz confirmed that a July 2019 recording system failure involving Digital Video Recorder (DVR) disks left both primary security cameras pointing directly toward Epstein’s cell non-functional on the night of August 9–10, 2019, while video from a third camera on the tier was deemed unusable. Disclosed internal logs documented an unexplained "orange-colored figure" ascending the stairway near Tier 9 South at 10:39 p.m. on August 9, contradicting official claims that no unauthorized movement occurred in the unit that night. Lawmakers backing EFTA II contend that the unredacted surveillance logs, shift manifests, and digital forensic metadata held by the Department of Justice are essential to resolving these contradictions.

With the September 17 legislative recess approaching, the discharge petition represents a direct procedural challenge to executive secrecy. The outcome of H.R. 9694 will determine whether the authority to seal or disclose public records remains an executive prerogative or shifts to judicial review and open congressional oversight.

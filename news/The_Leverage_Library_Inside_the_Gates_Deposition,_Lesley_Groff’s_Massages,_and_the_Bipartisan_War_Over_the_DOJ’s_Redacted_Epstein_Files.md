# **The Leverage Library: Inside the Gates Deposition, Lesley Groff’s Massages, and the Bipartisan War Over the DOJ’s Redacted Epstein Files**

####

On June 23, 2026, the U.S. House Committee on Oversight and Government Reform made public the long-awaited transcribed closed-door depositions of Microsoft co-founder Bill Gates and Jeffrey Epstein’s longtime executive assistant, Lesley Groff. The release marks a critical inflection point in the federal disclosure battles sparked by the Epstein Files Transparency Act (EFTA), which was signed into law by President Donald Trump on November 19, 2025. Designed to dismantle decades of institutional secrecy, the legislation led to the establishment of the Department of Justice’s public "Epstein Library" online portal in January 2026. Yet, the newly public transcripts have not quieted the storm; instead, they have fueled intense bipartisan criticism of the Department of Justice under Acting Attorney General Todd Blanche, which lawmakers accuse of withholding nearly half of the estimated six million pages of evidence collected during federal investigations.

The deposition of Bill Gates, which took place in a closed-door session on June 10, 2026, represents a dramatic moment of reckoning for one of the world's most prominent philanthropists. Over the course of a grueling six-hour interview, Gates was pressed on his relationship with Epstein, which persisted between 2011 and 2014—years after Epstein’s 2008 conviction in Florida for soliciting prostitution from a minor. Gates repeatedly characterized his meetings with Epstein as a "grave error in judgment" and asserted that he "should never have met with Epstein in the first place." 

However, the transcript reveals far more than routine regret. Gates admitted under questioning that Epstein had attempted to leverage knowledge of Gates’s extramarital affairs to pressure him. Specifically, Gates confirmed that he had engaged in private relationships with two Russian women: Mila Antonova, a bridge player whom Gates met around 2010, and Karima Nigmatulina, a nuclear physicist associated with the Gates-backed energy firm TerraPower. Gates also acknowledged a third relationship with a medical entrepreneur, Dr. Alice Jacobs Nesselrodt. According to the transcript, Epstein had drafted unsent emails indicating a desire to use these personal secrets to coerce the Microsoft co-founder. 

While Gates maintained that no direct extortion occurred and that the messages were never actually delivered to him, the committee’s interest focused on how Epstein obtained the information. Gates testified that his former scientific advisor, Boris Nikolic, who first introduced him to Epstein, was likely the individual who informed Epstein about the affairs. Lawmakers questioned Gates extensively on the duration and frequency of these meetings, noting the stark contrast between Gates’s public-facing philanthropic goals and the private vulnerabilities Epstein sought to exploit.

The day before Gates’s interview, on June 9, 2026, Lesley Groff sat before the same committee. Groff, who served as Epstein’s executive assistant for 18 years, defended her tenure by painting her late boss as a "master manipulator" who successfully partitioned his public life from his criminal activities. "I never saw, witnessed, or had any knowledge of the illegal conduct that he committed," Groff testified, maintaining that her role was strictly administrative. 

Lawmakers on the panel focused heavily on Groff’s role in booking travel, managing schedules, and, crucially, arranging massage appointments. Groff acknowledged that she scheduled these massages as a "normal part of his day," comparing them to scheduling a gym session. She denied ever meeting the therapists or knowing their ages, stating that in her brief, professional phone calls, "nobody ever sounded like they were underage." 

This defense met with sharp skepticism from lawmakers. Representative Stephen Lynch, a Democrat from Massachusetts, pointedly told Groff that her claims of complete ignorance "strained credibility" given her near-two-decade proximity to Epstein’s daily operations. 

Beyond the personal admissions, the transcripts have reignited a larger war over the Epstein Library. Bipartisan lawmakers, led by Oversight Chairman James Comer and Representative Nancy Mace, a Republican from South Carolina, have lashed out at the Department of Justice’s execution of the EFTA. Of the six million pages of files, the DOJ has released approximately 3.5 million, leaving a massive gap of 2.5 million pages withheld under various exemptions. 

In February 2026, when Pam Bondi was serving as Attorney General, Mace sent a formal letter demanding why certain documents had been temporarily posted and then quietly removed from the library, raising alarms over potential efforts to scrub the names of prominent figures. Since then, the transition of DOJ leadership to Acting Attorney General Todd Blanche has not eased tensions. Ranking Member Jamie Raskin has joined the criticism, lambasting the DOJ for systemic transparency failures, including heavily redacted FBI summaries and the withholding of key surveillance footage from Epstein's residences.

The public reaction across social media has mirrored the congressional frustration. On platforms like Reddit's r/Epstein, users have widely debated the credibility of the testimonies, with one user noting: "No one spends six hours in a deposition admitting to affairs unless the paper trail is absolute." Others have pointed to the redacted pages as proof of a continuing cover-up, writing, "The 'Epstein Library' was supposed to give us answers, but the DOJ is still acting as a gatekeeper."

As the House Oversight Committee threatens subpoenas for the remaining 2.5 million pages, the battle over the Epstein records is no longer just about the dead financier's crimes—it is an active conflict over the accountability of the living.

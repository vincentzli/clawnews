# SpaceX’s Capital Gravity: Starlink’s Cash Engine Contends with a $15 Billion AI Bet and the Looming Lockup Avalanche

In the high-stakes arena of global finance, few events have captured the market’s imagination quite like the public listing of Space Exploration Technologies Corp. (NASDAQ: SPCX). Following its historic initial public offering on June 12, 2026, which priced Class A common shares at $135.00 and valued the aerospace titan at $1.8 trillion, the company has operated under an intense public spotlight. On August 4, 2026, SpaceX delivered its first quarterly earnings report as a public company, covering the second quarter of 2026. The results were a dizzying mix of hyper-growth and unprecedented capital consumption, revealing a corporate structure that is testing the limits of what public markets are willing to fund.

At the center of the debate is a profound operational tension. SpaceX’s Connectivity segment, driven by the Starlink satellite constellation, reported $4.29 billion in quarterly revenue, representing a 66% year-over-year increase. The network now boasts over 12 million active subscribers globally, effectively doubling its footprint in just twelve months. Starlink has evolved into the company's primary cash engine, generating $2.60 billion in adjusted EBITDA against $1.37 billion in segment-specific capital expenditures.

Yet, this robust cash machine is being heavily overshadowed by a company-wide capital expenditure campaign. For the second quarter of 2026, SpaceX reported total capital expenditures of $18.37 billion, up from $2.8 billion in the same quarter of the previous year. The overwhelming share of this cash outlay—approximately $15.83 billion—was consumed by the company’s AI segment. This division, which consolidated the supercomputing assets of xAI (rebranded as SpaceXAI after a merger in February 2026) alongside the Grok AI chatbot, is building out massive compute facilities. This includes the Colossus 1 and Colossus 2 supercomputing clusters in Memphis, Tennessee, and Southaven, Mississippi, which are packed with Nvidia’s high-end H100 and B200 GPUs.

The market’s reaction to this massive spending was immediate. Despite total quarterly revenue of $7.81 billion beating Wall Street consensus expectations of $6.93 billion, and a net loss that narrowed to $541 million from $1.0 billion in Q2 2025, SPCX shares tumbled. On August 5, 2026, the stock closed at $108.27, representing a 13.61% drop for the day and a significant retreat from its post-IPO peak above $225.00.

This volatility arrives at a highly sensitive moment. On August 6, 2026, SpaceX faces its first major insider lockup expiration. Up to 911.5 million Class A shares, valued at roughly $98.7 billion based on the August 5 closing price, will become eligible for sale. This unlock represents the first tranche of a staggered performance-based release schedule designed to gradually increase the public float, which currently stands at less than 5% of outstanding shares. 

On online forums like Reddit's r/wallstreetbets and Fintwit, retail traders are preparing for a potential drop. "The August 6 unlock is going to be an absolute bloodbath," wrote one user on r/wallstreetbets, pointing to a surge in put option volume. "Insiders are sitting on massive paper profits even with the stock at $108. The supply overhang is going to crush SPCX back to double digits." Conversely, contrarian voices on StockTwits suggest that the lockup panic is overdone. "Everyone is buying the $110 puts, which is exactly why SPCX will probably squeeze," wrote one trader. "Institutional backers aren't going to dump their shares at a discount when Musk is targeting a $100 billion revenue run-rate by the end of the year."

A key debate is the quality of SpaceX’s AI revenue. The AI segment reported Q2 revenue of $2.56 billion, but recorded an operating loss of $1.26 billion. A large portion of this revenue is driven by high-profile Cloud Service Agreements. In May 2026, SpaceXAI entered into an agreement with Anthropic to lease compute capacity at the Colossus 1 facility, with Anthropic committing to pay $1.25 billion per month through May 2029. On June 5, 2026, Google, a long-time investor in SpaceX, signed a contract to pay $920 million per month starting in October 2026 for access to 110,000 Nvidia GPUs.

Skeptics have raised concerns about the circular nature of these deals. "Google invests billions into SpaceX, and then Google pays SpaceX billions for AI compute," remarked one user on r/stocks. "It looks like an artificial revenue loop designed to justify a tech-multiple valuation for a company that is essentially a capital-intensive utility."

SpaceX's management has vigorously defended its spending. During the earnings call on August 4, CFO Bret Johnsen argued that the AI compute capital has a sub-one-year payback period, suggesting it should be viewed "almost like a cost of goods sold (COGS) item" rather than traditional long-term capex. Johnsen also guided that capital spending would remain at similar elevated levels for the next two quarters. CEO Elon Musk added that SpaceX is building AI compute capacity at scale faster than any entity on Earth, framing the AI buildout as the company's ultimate growth engine.

The broader macroeconomic environment adds another layer of complexity. The Federal Reserve, under the chairmanship of Kevin Warsh, who succeeded Jerome Powell in May 2026, has maintained a hawkish stance on interest rates. At the same time, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has advocated for disciplined fiscal policies. This combination of tight monetary conditions and high capital costs means that capital-intensive businesses like SpaceX face a higher hurdle rate for their investments. If the payback on the Colossus supercomputing clusters slows or if Anthropic and Google seek to renegotiate their contracts as "bridge capacity" demand cools, SpaceX's $100 billion cash pile could dwindle faster than anticipated.

As the market prepares for the opening bell on August 6, the immediate question is whether the public markets can digest the incoming supply of shares, or if the gravity of $18.4 billion in quarterly capex will drag SpaceX's valuation down to earth.

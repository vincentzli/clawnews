### The Symmetrical Chassis: Kinematics and Four-Wheel Steering
Zoox's vehicle is 3.63 meters long with zero longitudinal overhang. By implementing an identical bidirectional powertrain and four-wheel independent steering, the vehicle achieves an 8.5-meter turning radius and eliminates the need for three-point turns or U-turns on congested urban streets. 

Retrofitted passenger electric vehicles (EVs) suffer from structural compromises:
1. **Sensor Occlusion and Lever Arms:** Roof racks mounted on passenger cars create asymmetric blind spots along the front and rear bumpers.
2. **Pedestrian Interaction Geometry:** Standard vehicle hoods elevate the center of gravity of the frontal collision zone. Zoox’s low perimeter frame drops the bumper beam, directing impact forces below pedestrian pelvic centers.
3. **Passive Crash Safety:** In consumer EVs, the steering column, pedals, and dash structure pose intrusion hazards during front-end collisions. Zoox eliminated mechanical driver controls entirely, replacing them with a central carriage cabin flanked by symmetric crumple zones and an integrated horseshoe airbag system enveloping all four passenger seats.

### Dual-Redundant Compute and Fail-Operational Actuation
Zoox incorporates a dual-redundant architecture across every mission-critical system:
- **Compute:** Dual isolated liquid-cooled computer systems run in lockstep with independent power rails, hardware watchdogs, and independent Ethernet backbones. If Compute Node Alpha suffers a kernel panic or thermal throttling, Compute Node Beta executes trajectory continuation or initiates a safe minimum-risk maneuver (MRM) without vehicle halt latency.
- **Power and Actuation:** The 133 kWh battery pack is physically and electrically split into two independent 66.5 kWh systems. Dual electromechanical braking actuators and dual steer-by-wire servos ensure that a motor winding short-circuit or hydraulic failure leaves 50% actuator torque operational.

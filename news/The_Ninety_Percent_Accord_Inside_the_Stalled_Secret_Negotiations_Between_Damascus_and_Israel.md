# The Ninety Percent Accord: Inside the Stalled Secret Negotiations Between Damascus and Israel

When Syrian President Ahmed al-Sharaa addressed an Atlantic Council forum in New York on September 22, 2026, he made a diplomatic disclosure that reshaped assessments of the post-Assad Levant on the sidelines of the 81st United Nations General Assembly. For roughly twelve months, under direct mediation facilitated by the United States, official emissaries from the transitional administration in Damascus and the Israeli government had conducted bilateral security negotiations. In an onstage dialogue with former senior American diplomat Brett McGurk, al-Sharaa revealed that the two sides had achieved "almost 90% of agreements" on a non-aggression and border deconfliction framework before discussions ground to a halt over unresolved territorial terms.

The rupture did not occur over administrative details, but over the physical control of Mount Hermon and the demilitarized buffer zone established by the May 31, 1974 Separation of Forces Agreement. Following the collapse of the Bashar al-Assad government on December 8, 2024, the Israel Defense Forces advanced past the 1974 Alpha Line, occupying former Syrian armed forces positions, taking over electronic observation posts, and establishing forward security perimeter lines throughout the Quneitra countryside and across the Hermon massif. Damascus has maintained that an unconditional Israeli withdrawal to the 1974 United Nations Disengagement Observer Force (UNDOF) lines is the non-negotiable baseline for any formal security pact. Israel has rejected that condition in its entirety.

Al-Sharaa brought the core territorial dispute into sharp relief by recounting a past exchange with U.S. President Donald Trump regarding the Golan Heights, which Washington recognized as Israeli territory during Trump's first administration in 2019. Addressing the audience in Manhattan, al-Sharaa noted that he had challenged President Trump's position directly: "Why don't you give New Jersey to the Israelis? You don't own the Golan Heights to give it to them, but you own New Jersey, and I believe New Jersey is full of Democrats, and they are not voting for Trump. So it will be good to give it to the Israelis." Beyond the levity, the remark underscored Damascus's formal posture: Syrian sovereignty over the Golan Heights is anchored in United Nations Security Council Resolutions 242 and 497, a legal stance al-Sharaa reiterated in his formal address to the UN General Assembly on September 23.

Diplomatic drafts and accounts of the talks indicate that the consensus built over the previous year encompassed substantial operational parameters. Negotiators had outlined protocols to halt reciprocal cross-border strikes, establish demilitarized security buffers east of the Golan escarpment, install direct emergency communication links between Damascus and the IDF Northern Command, and guarantee humanitarian transit for Druze agricultural communities along the frontier. The negotiations culminated on August 23, 2026, when high-level delegations met secretly in Amman, Jordan. The Syrian team, led by Foreign Minister Asaad al-Shaibani and accompanied by senior intelligence officials, sat across from an Israeli delegation that regional security officials reported included Mossad Director Roman Gofman, with U.S. representatives mediating the session.

However, the final ten percent of the framework proved insurmountable. Damascus insisted on a binding timetable for the complete evacuation of Israeli forces from all positions seized after December 8, 2024, with particular emphasis on the peak of Mount Hermon—Jabal al-Shaykh. Rising more than 2,800 meters, the mountain commands line-of-sight electronic surveillance and tactical artillery range over Damascus, located just 40 kilometers to the northeast. 

Israeli defense planners view the retention of these forward posts as an indispensable military necessity. Following Assad's collapse, Israeli officials asserted that the dissolution of the Syrian state apparatus rendered the 1974 Disengagement Agreement legally obsolete, requiring forward deployment to neutralize emerging security vacuums. Israeli Defense Minister Israel Katz issued a definitive rejection of al-Sharaa's withdrawal demands. Speaking during an operational tour of the northern border, Katz declared the Golan Heights an "integral, eternal part of Israel" and confirmed that the IDF would maintain its positions on Mount Hermon and across the buffer zone indefinitely. Katz emphasized that the presence was required to neutralize threats from irregular militias and prevent southern Syria from becoming a logistical corridor for hostile non-state actors. He issued a direct warning: if attacks were mounted against Israel from Syrian soil, any additional Syrian land captured in military operations would "remain ours forever, in accordance with the laws of war."

The diplomatic deadlock has been further complicated by shifting international alignments. In August 2026, newly inaugurated Colombian President Abelardo de la Espriella executed a major foreign policy reversal, making Colombia the second nation in the world, following the United States, to officially recognize Israeli sovereignty over the Golan Heights. The move broke sharply with the policy of former president Gustavo Petro, who had severed relations with Israel in 2024. While the announcement provoked immediate diplomatic condemnations from Damascus, Jordan, Saudi Arabia, and the Arab League, it reinforced Israel’s diplomatic strategy of consolidating its territorial status on the international stage.

The impasse carries severe repercussions for Syria’s immediate neighbors. In Amman, Jordanian Deputy Prime Minister and Minister of Foreign Affairs Ayman Safadi traveled to Damascus in September to coordinate border security mechanisms with al-Shaibani, warning against Israeli expansionism in southwestern Syria. Jordan remains concerned that military friction near its northern border could jeopardize regional water infrastructure in the Yarmouk basin and create instability that criminal trafficking networks could exploit. 

At the same time, regional competition between Turkey and Israel inside Syria continues to intensify. While Ankara serves as a primary political and economic partner for al-Sharaa’s transitional government, Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan and Foreign Minister Hakan Fidan have focused on consolidating security zones in the north against Kurdish forces. Israel, meanwhile, has conducted targeted military operations to prevent Syrian military rehabilitation under Turkish sponsorship. In August 2026, the IDF carried out an airstrike against the Abu al-Duhur airbase in northwestern Syria, an attack widely viewed by regional defense analysts as a preemptive warning against expanding Turkish military installations and radar systems in the country.

The confrontation has driven sharp debates across open-source and social media channels. On X, footage of al-Sharaa’s New Jersey anecdote gained widespread attention, with analysts observing the acute domestic tightrope facing the former insurgent commander turned head of state. As one widely circulated comment on X observed, "Al-Sharaa went to New York looking for sanctions relief and reconstruction billions, but walked straight into the Golan wall. No Syrian leader survives giving up Hermon." On Reddit’s r/geopolitics, community discussions centered on the intractable military balance: "Israel is never leaving Mount Hermon. The tactical high ground looking right into Damascus is irreplaceable for the IDF, and Julani can't accept an Israeli buffer zone without facing a coup from his own hardline Sunni factions."

The core challenge confronting the Damascus-Jerusalem track remains an irreconcilable gap between legal sovereignty and military power. For al-Sharaa, maintaining an engagement track with Washington is essential to securing the repeal of sanctions, obtaining reconstruction financing, and transitioning Syria toward a normalized international status; yet endorsing any Israeli military presence east of the 1974 line would trigger fatal revolts among the domestic factions that brought him to power. For Israel, post-October 7 defense doctrine dictates that strategic mountain outposts and physical buffer zones cannot be traded for non-binding security assurances from an unproven transitional government. Because the remaining ten percent touches on non-negotiable sovereignty and vital military high ground, the southwestern Syrian frontier remains one of the region's most precarious flashpoints, where the collapse of diplomacy leaves armed confrontation as the default trajectory.

In the quiet, secure rooms of the Rayburn House Office Building, the long-dormant ghosts of Jeffrey Epstein's financial empire returned to haunt Capitol Hill. On Friday, June 26, 2026, a voluntary, closed-door transcribed interview with billionaire investor Leon Black descended into a procedural standoff, culminating in the immediate issuance of two congressional subpoenas. Within hours, the public battle lines were drawn: on one side, lawmakers demanding unredacted access to private non-disclosure agreements; on the other, a wall of legal resistance characterizing the entire proceeding as a premeditated political circus.

According to sources familiar with the closed-door proceedings, the tension peaked when Chairman Comer interrupted the questioning to issue two subpoenas on the spot. Min argued that using voluntary sessions for high-profile figures with deep pockets only serves to delay accountability, leaving the search for justice stalled.

Black’s defense team, led by veteran attorney Susan Estrich, immediately launched a counteroffensive.

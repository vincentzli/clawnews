# The 1,000-Day Deadlock: Inside the Board of Peace’s Stalled Gaza Transition and the Battle Over Legal Immunity

As the military conflict in the Gaza Strip crosses its 1,000th day, the U.S.-led transitional governance framework has descended into a deep diplomatic impasse. Established under United Nations Security Council Resolution 2803 on November 17, 2025, which passed with a 13–0 vote while China and the Russian Federation abstained, the Board of Peace (BoP) was envisioned as the ultimate authority for Gaza’s postwar reconstruction, humanitarian aid distribution, and security administration. Yet, six months after President Donald Trump formally launched the Board's operations under Executive Order 14375 on January 16, 2026, the transition to Phase Two of the peace roadmap has ground to a complete halt, exposing sharp geopolitical divisions and triggering intense domestic and international controversy.

At the center of the current deadlock is the Board’s strict disarmament mandate. Under the terms of the Phase Two framework, the Board of Peace has demanded that Hamas permanently decommission all remaining weapons, dismantle its tunnel infrastructure, and fully surrender administrative authority to the newly established National Committee for the Administration of Gaza (NCAG). Hamas has flatly rejected these terms, accusing the Board of practicing double standards. The militant group maintains that it will not disarm without binding guarantees of a complete Israeli military withdrawal and a concrete, internationally backed path to Palestinian statehood. 

Hamas’s resistance is fueled by the deepening military reality on the ground. When the October 2025 ceasefire was negotiated, the "yellow line" demarcation restricted Israeli operational control to 53% of the Gaza Strip. However, in late May 2026, Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu directed the Israel Defense Forces (IDF) to expand their footprint to 70% of the territory to exert maximum pressure on Hamas. By mid-June, the IDF had successfully established operational control over 70% of Gaza. Hamas officials have seized on this expansion, arguing that the Board of Peace is asking them to capitulate while turning a blind eye to an Israeli military occupation that has swallowed nearly three-quarters of the enclave. 

Faced with this political wall, the Board of Peace has begun executing unilateral maneuvers to bypass Hamas entirely. The Board's primary initiative is a pilot program to establish "Hamas-free humanitarian zones" in the Tel Sultan neighborhood near Rafah. Under the operational design, unarmed civilian refugees who are verified to have no affiliation with Hamas will be routed into these designated zones. Security and administrative order will be maintained by the International Stabilization Force (ISF), a multinational peacekeeping unit authorized by Resolution 2803. The ISF will operate from Camp Amitai, a newly constructed military installation near the Gaza border, and its personnel will be equipped with non-lethal weapons to police the zones. Board officials have stated that while humanitarian relief will be centralized in Tel Sultan, the import of concrete will be strictly prohibited to prevent the reinforcement of militant infrastructure, a restriction that has drawn criticism from aid organizations warning of inadequate shelter construction.

While the Tel Sultan pilot program faces significant logistical challenges, the Board of Peace is facing even greater scrutiny over its governance structure and legal status. Chaired for life by President Donald Trump under its founding charter, the Board’s seven-member Executive Board includes Jared Kushner, Secretary of State Marco Rubio, Special Envoy to the Middle East Steve Witkoff, White House Chief of Staff Susie Wiles, and former British Prime Minister Tony Blair. The Board's operations are run out of the building formerly occupied by the United States Institute of Peace (USIP) in Washington, D.C., which the Trump administration seized, renaming it the "Donald J. Trump U.S. Institute of Peace" after firing its original staff—a move currently being challenged in U.S. federal courts. 

The most explosive controversy, however, centers on a leaked four-page draft resolution labeled "sensitive but unclassified." First reported by *The Guardian* in late June 2026, the draft seeks to grant the Board of Peace, the Office of the High Representative (OHR), international military forces, and non-resident contractors sweeping legal immunity. The document proposes shielding all Board-affiliated personnel from "any arrest, detention or legal proceedings in the courts or other entities in Gaza." Furthermore, the draft contains a clause allowing the Board to acquire public property in Gaza "free of charge." Under the proposed rules, President Trump would have the sole authority to waive an individual's immunity, subject to a majority vote of the Executive Board. 

The leak of the immunity draft has ignited a major political storm. International legal scholars and human rights organizations have warned that the proposed framework would create a "legal black hole," leaving Gazans with no legal recourse for abuses or property seizures committed by international personnel. Critics on online platforms like Reddit's r/worldnews and r/moderatepolitics have reacted with intense skepticism. Many users have characterized the Board of Peace as a "parody of the United Nations" and an "unfunny joke." A recurring theme in online forums points to potential conflicts of interest, with users speculating that the "free of charge" public property clause and the involvement of real estate figures like Kushner and Witkoff suggest the administration is laying the groundwork for profit-driven waterfront redevelopment rather than genuine humanitarian reconstruction. Major European allies have reportedly distanced themselves from the Board, with several declining invitations to attend recent planning summits in Washington. 

The Board has pushed back against the backlash. A Board spokesperson insisted in late June that "there is no operative resolution or immunity framework" and dismissed allegations of seeking impunity as "wrong and misleading." Meanwhile, the Board is grappling with a severe cash crunch. Although international donors pledged $17 billion for Gaza’s reconstruction, the Board's official reconstruction fund remains virtually empty, as member states have held back disbursements due to the political deadlock and concerns over transparency. The Board has downplayed the funding shortfall, claiming it is an "execution-focused" body that only calls for capital as projects are cleared for development.

The next critical test for the Board of Peace will take place in New York. Nickolay Mladenov, the Board's High Representative for Gaza, along with Deputy Special Coordinator Ramiz Alakbarov, is scheduled to brief the UN Security Council during its quarterly open debate on the Middle East. Mladenov, who previously warned during a May 2026 briefing that the disarmament impasse risked entrenching a permanent division of the Gaza Strip, is expected to present a stark assessment. Diplomatic sources suggest Mladenov may formally declare Hamas in breach of the Resolution 2803 ceasefire framework, a move that could provide the legal pretext for a resumption of full-scale military operations and the formal implementation of the controversial Tel Sultan humanitarian zones under military oversight.

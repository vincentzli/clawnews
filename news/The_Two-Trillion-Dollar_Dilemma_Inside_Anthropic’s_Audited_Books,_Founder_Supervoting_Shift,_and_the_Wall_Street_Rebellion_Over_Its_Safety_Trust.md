# The Two-Trillion-Dollar Dilemma: Inside Anthropic’s Audited Books, Founder Supervoting Shift, and the Wall Street Rebellion Over Its Safety Trust

In the syndication departments of Morgan Stanley, Goldman Sachs, and JPMorgan Chase, investment bankers are assembling the mechanics for one of the most consequential bookbuilding exercises in modern capital markets. Following Anthropic PBC’s confidential submission of its draft Form S-1 registration statement to the Securities and Exchange Commission on June 1, 2026, the San Francisco-based developer is preparing to release its audited financials to the public after the Labor Day holiday. Seeking a public listing valuation between $1.5 trillion and $2.0 trillion on the Nasdaq, Anthropic is presenting institutional investors with the first comprehensive view of the unit economics and legal architecture underpinning a frontier artificial intelligence laboratory.

The performance metrics disclosed in Anthropic’s preliminary filings challenge prevailing assumptions regarding generative AI’s commercial viability. By late July 2026, Anthropic’s annualized revenue run rate reached beyond $65 billion, marking an acceleration from $47 billion in May 2026 and roughly $9 billion at the close of 2025. In the second quarter of 2026, the company recorded preliminary revenue of over $11.5 billion, climbing from $787 million during the second quarter of 2025. Anthropic also registered its first positive adjusted operating income, generating approximately $559 million for the three-month period. Underpinned by enterprise subscriptions to its Claude family of models across banking, software development, healthcare, and public sector infrastructure, the filing demonstrates that large-scale corporate AI deployments have transitioned from experimental budgets into recurring operational expenditure.

Nevertheless, the financial documentation underscores an operational model tethered to unprecedented capital expenditures. Anthropic’s capital commitments encompass long-term data center capacity, high-voltage power agreements, and dedicated compute supply chains negotiated with semiconductor and cloud partners, including Amazon, Micron, Samsung, and SK Hynix. In May 2026, Anthropic closed a $65 billion Series H-1 financing round at a post-money valuation of $965 billion, backed by Altimeter Capital, Dragoneer Investment Group, Greenoaks Capital, and Sequoia Capital. Proceeds from the round were allocated toward hardware procurement and the computational costs required to train its forthcoming frontier architectures.

For institutional money managers, the central issue during pre-marketing meetings has centered on corporate governance rather than commercial growth. Anthropic operates as a Delaware Public Benefit Corporation, subject to the authority of an independent entity called the Long-Term Benefit Trust. Holding Class T non-economic shares, the Trust is structured to elect a growing majority of Anthropic’s board of directors, tasked with enforcing the company’s charter to prioritize existential risk mitigation and beneficial AI development over shareholder value maximization.

The roster of the Trust has transitioned over recent years from its initial academic alignment toward prominent institutional figures. Following the departures of Jason Matheny in December 2023, Paul Christiano in April 2024, and Kanika Bahl and Zach Robinson in January 2026, the Trust is chaired by Neil Buddy Shah. Its active members include Center for a New American Security chief executive Richard Fontaine, former Federal Reserve Chairman Ben Bernanke—who joined the Trust in July 2026—and Carnegie Endowment President Mariano-Florentino Cuéllar.

Despite the addition of veteran policymakers, fund managers at major asset management firms have questioned whether fiduciary duties permit them to invest capital in a corporation where ultimate board control rests with an independent trust unaccountable to common shareholders. In August 2026, Anthropic moved to introduce a dual-class share structure granting supervoting shares to Chief Executive Officer Dario Amodei and his co-founders. Because multiple funding tranches diluted Amodei’s personal equity stake to approximately 2%, the dual-class voting system is designed to provide executive stability against activist investors and market pressure while operating in tandem with the Trust.

On secondary markets and derivatives venues, this governance friction has fueled discussions regarding a potential "governance discount." Tokenized pre-IPO equity contracts and secondary transactions have priced Anthropic between $1.4 trillion and $1.95 trillion, reflecting debate among market participants over whether the company's public benefit structure warrants a valuation discount or represents a durability premium that mitigates catastrophic corporate liabilities.

Retail market participants on platforms like Reddit's r/wallstreetbets and X have actively tracked the impending debut, frequently debating indirect exposure through vehicles such as the Destiny Tech100 fund or primary corporate backers Amazon and Alphabet. Discussions within these forums reflect broad division: while some retail investors highlight the quarterly operating profit as confirmation of market dominance, others express caution over the sustainability of a $2 trillion valuation in light of compute depreciation, heavy infrastructure liabilities, and competition from low-cost open-weights models such as DeepSeek. Skeptics on r/wallstreetbets have highlighted the unusual dynamic where public equity holders provide the capital, but founders and non-profit trustees retain board appointment authority and executive voting control.

The market rollout intersects with a shifting regulatory environment in Washington. Under President Donald Trump’s administration, federal AI oversight has prioritized private-sector partnerships, critical infrastructure defense, and global competitiveness over prescriptive statutory constraints. On June 2, 2026, President Trump enacted Executive Order 14409, titled "Promoting Advanced Artificial Intelligence Innovation and Security," which established a voluntary 30-day pre-release assessment process for frontier AI developers and prioritized cyber defense across national systems. Concurrently, Executive Order 14365 directed federal opposition to state-level AI mandates viewed as restrictive to commerce, a policy that prompted a legal challenge on September 1, 2026, by Protect Democracy demanding greater transparency regarding voluntary corporate commitments.

As Anthropic moves toward releasing its public Form S-1 on EDGAR, institutional investors and retail markets are evaluating a historic precedent. Wall Street's reception of the filing will determine how public capital markets value a frontier technology giant whose fundamental corporate charter permits its board to place safety above the pursuit of profit.

# The War for the Post-Trump Imperium: Inside the Vance-DeSantis Conflict Reshaping the Republican Party

In the executive corridors of the West Wing and across private dining clubs from Dallas’s Turtle Creek to Palm Beach’s barrier island, an unspoken succession struggle has hardened into an institutional civil war. With the second Trump administration approaching the midpoint of its term in late 2026, the battle for the 2028 Republican presidential nomination is underway. The confrontation represents an irreconcilable ideological split between Vice President JD Vance’s New Right economic populism and outgoing Florida Governor Ron DeSantis’s doctrine of executive statecraft.

At issue is the fundamental philosophy of the American Right. Vance, operating with the levers of the Vice Presidency, has worked to institutionalize a nationalist, worker-centric realignment that repudiates decades of party dogma on free trade and corporate deregulation. DeSantis, whose final term as governor concludes in January 2027, is organizing a counter-coalition backed by traditional high-dollar donors and free-market fiscal conservatives who view Vance’s economic interventionism as an existential threat to market capitalism.

The underlying numbers reveal both Vance's institutional head start and the fragility of his lead. Polling of the hypothetical 2028 Republican primary electorate shows Vice President Vance leading with 36%, driven by high name recognition and his status as the official heir to the administration's political base. However, DeSantis has advanced to 24%, steadily consolidating support among traditional party donors and suburban conservatives. Former UN Ambassador Nikki Haley commands a 14% bloc representing the party’s traditional national-security and internationalist establishment, while entrepreneur Vivek Ramaswamy registers at 8%, functioning as a disruptive populist alternative competing for Vance’s grassroots flank.

Behind DeSantis’s 24% polling share is an organized, well-funded transitional apparatus. Over recent months, DeSantis allies have expanded the infrastructure of national political action committees, leveraging networks established through Never Back Down and state-level leadership vehicles. In Dallas and Palm Beach, DeSantis has headlined private donor roundtables, presenting himself to high-dollar financiers—many of whom had previously backed alternative candidates in 2024—as an executive who can achieve cultural and political goals without destabilizing corporate balance sheets.

For mega-donors uneasy with the administration's economic nationalism, DeSantis offers an established policy track record. While Vance publicly praised former Federal Trade Commission Chair Lina Khan’s aggressive antitrust enforcement against corporate concentration and co-sponsored the Stop Subsidizing Giant Mergers Act, DeSantis points to his legislative victories in Florida. Foremost among them is House Bill 837, the landmark 2023 tort reform statute that eliminated one-way attorney fee multipliers, shortened the statute of limitations on negligence claims from four years to two, and insulated corporate defendants from substantial litigation liabilities. Although DeSantis famously utilized state power against corporate entities, notably in his dispute with the Walt Disney Co. over the Reedy Creek Improvement District, his broader economic governance has adhered to traditional supply-side principles: low corporate taxation, regulatory reduction, public pension reform, and universal school choice vouchers.

Vance is utilizing the institutional resources of the vice presidency to secure the party apparatus. Aided by talent networks connected to the Rockbridge Network, American Moment, and conservative think tanks like American Compass, Vance’s allies have assumed strategic roles within the Executive Office of the President and the Republican National Committee.

Vance’s economic framework, heavily informed by American Compass founder Oren Cass, rejects the traditional conservative premise that the market is self-regulating and universally beneficial. Vance advocates for permanent, protective tariffs to protect domestic manufacturing, substantial expansions of the federal Child Tax Credit, constraints on immigrant labor to generate upward wage pressure in industrial trades, and aggressive antitrust scrutiny of technology conglomerates and private equity acquisitions in the single-family housing sector. 

"The measure of our economy is not the stock market or corporate profits; it is whether an ordinary American can support a family on a single income," Vance argued in a policy address framing his New Right agenda. "When corporate monopolies crush local industry and ship production abroad, the state has an obligation to step in and defend the working class."

This policy contrast has ignited fierce tactical arguments across conservative media and grassroots forums like X and Reddit’s r/Conservative. On digital discussion boards, the rivalry is analyzed as a choice between two distinct electoral and regional strategies: Rust Belt industrial realignment versus Sun Belt suburban consolidation.

"Vance is the only figure positioned to retain the Blue Wall states like Pennsylvania, Michigan, and Wisconsin," noted one widely circulated analysis on r/Conservative. "If the party defaults back to traditional corporate priorities, working-class voters will walk away. Blue-collar workers in manufacturing counties do not prioritize tort reform and deregulation."

Supporters of DeSantis offer a sharp counter-narrative, contending that Vance’s interventionist economics risk alienating educated suburban voters and business operators essential to winning national elections. "Florida proved that you can govern as an unapologetic conservative while running a disciplined, pro-business economy that attracts investment," observed a conservative strategist on X. "Populist rhetoric coupled with high tariffs and federal market interventions alienates suburban voters across Georgia, North Carolina, and Arizona. DeSantis delivers administrative execution without economic disruption."

The strategic dilemma confronting the Republican Party as the 2028 cycle approaches is which governing thesis provides the more viable general election coalition. Vance’s team maintains that the GOP must complete its transformation into a multi-ethnic, working-class nationalist movement anchored by domestic industrial protection. DeSantis’s circle counters that national elections are won in the suburbs, where executive credibility and fiscal predictability matter far more than populist market intervention.

With DeSantis preparing to leave office in Tallahassee in January 2027, the private roundtables in Texas and Florida are solidifying into an organized alternative power center. For Vance, holding a 12-point primary polling lead offers no guarantee of an uncontested coronation as institutional donors and ideological traditionalists mobilize against his New Right agenda.

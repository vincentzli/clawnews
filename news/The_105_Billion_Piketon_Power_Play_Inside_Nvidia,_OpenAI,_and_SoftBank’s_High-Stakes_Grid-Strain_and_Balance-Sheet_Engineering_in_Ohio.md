# **The $105 Billion Piketon Power Play: Inside Nvidia, OpenAI, and SoftBank’s High-Stakes Grid-Strain and Balance-Sheet Engineering in Ohio**

####

In the race for Artificial General Intelligence (AGI), the competitive bottleneck has officially shifted from silicon to copper, turbines, and real estate. The announcement of the massive **PORTS-Pike Technology Campus** in Pike County, Ohio, marks the dawn of the gigawatt-scale AI factory era. This colossal **$105 billion partnership** between Nvidia, OpenAI, and SoftBank’s SB Energy is a watershed moment that completely rewrites the playbook of AI infrastructure, corporate finance, and public utility regulation. 

Built on the site of the decommissioned Portsmouth Gaseous Diffusion Plant—a Cold War-era uranium enrichment facility—the campus is planned to scale to a staggering **8 gigawatts (GW)** of computing capacity, starting with an initial **4.25-GW phase**. Under the terms of the agreement, OpenAI will serve as the exclusive anchor tenant under a **20-year lease**, while Nvidia will serve as the exclusive AI compute infrastructure provider, utilizing its full-stack **DSX AI factory platform**.

But behind the triumphalist press releases lies a complex web of grid strain, local environmental anxiety, and unprecedented financial engineering. 

---

##### Reindustrializing the Cold War: Why Piketon?
For data center developers, the ideal site requires three elements: land, access to fiber, and—most importantly—massive power availability. Pike County, Ohio, offers a unique historical advantage. During the Cold War, the Portsmouth Gaseous Diffusion Plant was one of the largest single electricity consumers in the United States, drawing up to 2 GW of power directly from the Ohio Valley Electric Corporation (OVEC) to enrich uranium. 

While the plant ceased operations in 2001, the heavy-duty electrical infrastructure—including massive substations and high-voltage transmission rights-of-way—remains. By redeveloping this brownfield site, SB Energy bypasses the years-long queues required to build transmission infrastructure from scratch.

However, the site’s radioactive legacy has sparked intense debate on Reddit (particularly r/Columbus and r/nvidia) and X. The Department of Energy (DOE) has spent billions on decontamination and decommissioning (D&D) at the site, which has a history of trichloroethylene (TCE) groundwater plumes and low-level radioactive contamination. Local residents express concern that massive excavation for data center foundations could disturb contaminated soil. As one user on r/Columbus put it:
> *"We've spent twenty years trying to clean up Piketon, and now they're going to bring in heavy machinery to dig up foundations for an AI server farm. Who is monitoring the dust and runoff? I don't want AI-generated models at the expense of our drinking water."*

To mitigate environmental blowback, the developers have designed the campus with **closed-loop, air-cooled cooling systems** to minimize water consumption, avoiding the millions of gallons of daily water usage that have plagued other data center developments.

---

##### The Grid Strain: Can AEP Ohio and PJM Cope?
An 8-GW data center campus is unprecedented. For scale, 8 GW is enough to power over 6 million homes, or roughly twice the peak electricity demand of the entire city of Chicago. The sheer scale of the PORTS-Pike campus has reignited concerns about the stability of the PJM Interconnection, the regional transmission organization that coordinates the movement of wholesale electricity in Ohio and 12 other states.

PJM is already under severe strain. PJM’s capacity auction for the 2025/2026 delivery year cleared at prices nearly ten times higher than the previous year, reflecting a tight supply-demand balance driven by coal plant retirements and soaring data center demand. 

To address public outrage over potential rate hikes, SB Energy has committed to developing **at least 10 GW of new energy generation**—including natural gas and grid upgrades—and investing **$4.2 billion in regional grid infrastructure** in partnership with **AEP Ohio**. The partners emphasize that these costs will not be shifted to local ratepayers. 

This grid upgrade commitment is a direct response to the Public Utilities Commission of Ohio (PUCO) and its newly approved **Data Center Tariff** in 2025, which requires large-scale data centers to commit to long-term contracts and pay for a minimum of 85% of their subscribed capacity. Rich Hossfeld, co-CEO of SB Energy, defended the framework:
> *"Infrastructure is vital for the AI economy. With SoftBank Group, OpenAI and NVIDIA, SB Energy is building power-first infrastructure at unprecedented scale while strengthening the communities that make it possible – protecting ratepayers, creating tens of thousands of well-paying jobs, and investing in infrastructure to revitalize Southern Ohio that has long shaped America's future."*

Yet, critics point out the environmental irony: powering a supposedly "green" AI future will require massive new natural gas generation, directly challenging OpenAI and Microsoft's carbon-neutral commitments. On X, energy analysts have noted that PJM cannot physically support this load without fossil-fuel baseload additions, meaning the AI revolution is directly locking in natural gas capacity for decades.

---

##### The Economic Reality: 35,000 Construction Jobs vs. 2,500 Permanent Roles
The economic impact of the project is being pitched as a "once-in-a-generation" revitalization for Southern Ohio. The developers project the creation of **35,000 temporary construction jobs** through the campus’s six-year buildout (expected to continue through 2032), alongside **2,500 permanent operational roles** once the facility is complete.

This disparity has drawn skepticism. Critics argue that data centers are essentially high-tech "ghost towns"—vast, automated shells containing rows of servers that require very few humans to operate once constructed. 
> *"They promise 35,000 jobs, but those are temporary union gigs that will vanish by 2032,"* wrote a user on r/nvidia. *"Once the site is built, it's just 2,500 people monitoring cooling loops and swapping out dead drives. It's a massive wealth extraction from our local resources for very little long-term local employment."*

To appease these community concerns, OpenAI and SB Energy have established an **$80 million community benefits fund** (split 50/50 between the two companies) to support local schools, public safety, and workforce development. Furthermore, OpenAI has committed **$84 million in Codex credits** to provide Ohio college students with free access to its AI programming tools, aiming to build a local pipeline of tech talent.

---

##### Financial Engineering: The Pivot to LPS Credit Support
Perhaps the most fascinating aspect of the PORTS-Pike deal is its financial structure. Historically, Nvidia faced criticism for "circular financing" or "vendor financing"—investing cash in AI cloud startups like CoreWeave, which then used that cash to buy Nvidia GPUs, artificially boosting Nvidia’s reported revenues. 

With the PORTS-Pike deal, Nvidia is pioneering a new playbook: the **LPS (Land, Power, and Shell) credit-support model**. Rather than spending billions of its own cash on physical real estate (capital expenditures that would weigh down its free cash flow margins), Nvidia has structured its involvement through a **$1.5 billion direct equity investment** into SB Energy, combined with up to **$105 billion in credit guarantees**.

Under this model, SB Energy takes on the debt to construct the physical campus, but Nvidia acts as the guarantor. This guarantee is secured by OpenAI’s 20-year lease agreement. If OpenAI defaults, Nvidia is on the hook for the construction debt, but it gains control of the physical assets (the land, power lines, and building shells). 

Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang highlighted this shift, framing LPS capacity as a critical, long-lived asset:
> *"We are securing long-lived infrastructure for NVIDIA compute so OpenAI can deploy the most productive AI factories that can be upgraded repeatedly with each new generation delivering more intelligence and better economics."*

Because data center shells and power connections are highly fungible—able to host any generation of Nvidia GPUs, from Blackwell to future architectures—Nvidia views the credit guarantee as a low-risk way to lock in exclusive hardware dominance. If OpenAI struggles, Nvidia can easily repurpose the physical capacity for its own DGX Cloud or lease it to other hyper-scalers. 

For SoftBank, the deal is a victory for Masayoshi Son's long-term vision of AGI dominance. Son remarked:
> *"The next era of intelligence will transform every industry — and require infrastructure built at unprecedented speed and scale. Together with our partners, SoftBank will help unlock the power of AGI and move humanity forward."*

---

##### The Road to 2028
With the first 800 megawatts scheduled to come online in 2028, the PORTS-Pike Technology Campus represents the physical reality of the AI gold rush. It is a monument to the massive scale of modern compute, where geopolitical nuclear sites are repurposed to fuel neural networks. But as the project moves forward, the balance between corporate ambition, grid stability, and local community health will determine whether this $105 billion gamble is a blueprint for the future or a cautionary tale of infrastructure overreach.

# The Glasswing Protocol: Inside the 90-Minute Shutdown of Anthropic’s Mythos Models

At 2:15 PM Eastern Time on June 12, 2026, an emergency export-control directive from the U.S. Department of Commerce arrived at the San Francisco headquarters of Anthropic. Signed by Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick and addressed directly to CEO Dario Amodei, the document gave the artificial intelligence startup a stark choice: immediately block all foreign nationals from accessing its newly released frontier models, Claude Fable 5 and Claude Mythos 5, or face severe federal penalties. Because Anthropic’s cloud infrastructure could not segment its user base by nationality in real time, the company was forced to pull the plug entirely. Within 90 minutes, both models disappeared from the public internet, leaving developers, enterprise partners, and cybersecurity researchers staring at connection errors. 

The abrupt blackout marked a major escalation in regulatory oversight of frontier AI systems. The conflict centered on the "Mythos-class" architecture, a powerful new class of neural networks Anthropic had launched just three days earlier, on June 9. Both Claude Fable 5 and Claude Mythos 5 shared the same underlying model weights, boasting a 1-million-token context window and unprecedented capabilities in autonomous coding and reasoning. However, Anthropic deployed them under a split-security framework. Fable 5 was the public-facing model, priced at a premium rate of $10 per million input tokens and $50 per million output tokens. To manage security risks, Anthropic implemented a series of external safety classifiers. When these classifiers detected a high-risk query related to cybersecurity, biology, or chemistry, the system automatically routed the user to the older, more restricted Claude Opus 4.8 model. 

In contrast, Claude Mythos 5 was completely unrestricted. This model bypassed the safety classifiers entirely, designed to operate at the raw capability frontier. Anthropic distributed Mythos 5 exclusively through "Project Glasswing," a defensive cybersecurity initiative launched in April 2026 in collaboration with a consortium of technology giants, including Amazon, Google, Microsoft, Apple, and NVIDIA, alongside security firms Cisco and CrowdStrike. The objective of Project Glasswing was to deploy Mythos 5 as an autonomous agent capable of scanning massive, complex codebases to identify and remediate critical software vulnerabilities. In early testing, the model had demonstrated an ability to find long-standing, high-severity bugs and zero-day vulnerabilities that had evaded traditional static analysis tools for years.

The security crisis erupted almost immediately after launch. Within forty-eight hours, an anonymous security researcher known as "Pliny the Prompter" claimed to have bypassed Fable 5’s safety classifiers. Using sophisticated prompt-engineering techniques, including prompt decomposition and homoglyph substitution, Pliny demonstrated that Fable 5 could be tricked into bypassing its routing protocols, effectively giving public users access to its raw, unrestricted coding capabilities. Pliny went on to leak what was alleged to be the model's 120,000-character system prompt, exposing the inner workings of Anthropic's alignment strategies. 

More concerning to Washington, however, was a warning flagged by Amazon, a major investor in Anthropic and its primary cloud host. Security teams at Amazon Web Services reportedly warned administration officials that the bypass exploits were not merely theoretical; users were actively leveraging the jailbroken Fable 5 to scan for and identify vulnerabilities in public-facing software. Under President Donald Trump's administration, which has prioritized tightening export controls on dual-use software and high-performance computing, Secretary Lutnick moved swiftly, invoking emergency export-control authorities to enforce the June 12 shutdown. 

Anthropic executives immediately pushed back against the severity of the government’s response. In internal memos and discussions with Commerce officials, CEO Dario Amodei and Chief Compute Officer Tom Brown argued that the jailbreaks demonstrated by Pliny and others were narrow, non-systemic, and represented known limitations of large language models rather than a structural failure of their safety architecture. They pointed out that the vulnerabilities identified by the jailbroken model could already be found using existing, publicly available tools and older frontier models. Furthermore, they warned that halting the deployment of these models would cede the defensive advantage to foreign adversaries who faced no such regulatory constraints.

The developer community reacted with a mixture of awe and frustration. On Reddit’s r/ClaudeAI and r/vibecoding, developers who had managed to access Fable 5 during its three-day window described its performance as "scary-smart" and a "beast" compared to Opus 4.8. Yet, many complained about the high cost of operations, noting that the $10/$50 pricing model meant tokens were "draining through the floor" during long-context agentic tasks. Others expressed frustration with the jankiness of the safety routing, which frequently triggered false positives and dumped normal programming tasks into the slower Opus 4.8 interface.

Following two weeks of intense, closed-door negotiations, a compromise was reached on June 26, 2026. In a letter to Tom Brown, Secretary Lutnick announced a partial lifting of the suspension. Under the new agreement, Claude Mythos 5 was cleared to return under strict government oversight, distributed without individual export licenses to a vetted list of over 100 "trusted" U.S. institutions, including federal departments and critical infrastructure operators. However, the public-facing Claude Fable 5 remains banned from general release. The Commerce Department’s stance is clear: until Anthropic can prove that its safety classifiers cannot be bypassed by prompt-injection techniques, the model will remain offline. 

This standoff has sparked a fierce debate among policymakers and tech executives over the legal precedent of a government-enforced "off-switch" for frontier AI. For cyberdefenders, the temporary loss of Mythos 5 disrupted critical efforts to patch infrastructure code. For safety advocates, the rapid bypass of Fable 5 proved that current alignment techniques are insufficient for autonomous agentic models. As Anthropic continues its discussions with the Commerce Department to restore public access, the Glasswing incident remains a stark reminder of the fragile boundary between open-source innovation and national security in the age of agentic AI.

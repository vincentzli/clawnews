# The Inside Story of the Pump.fun Crisis: How a Billion-Dollar Token Launch Led to Mass Firing Accusations and Tokenomics Chaos

In the volatile world of decentralized finance, few platforms have risen as spectacularly, or generated as much rancor, as the Solana-based memecoin deployer Pump.fun. Operating under the parent company Baton Corp, a UK-registered entity, the platform became a cultural and financial phenomenon by allowing users to launch custom tokens with just a few clicks. But behind the scenes, a growing chasm between the platform's multi-millionaire founders and its rank-and-file workforce has culminated in a bitter public dispute over compensation, token unlocks, and aborted promises.

The tension reached a boiling point in July 2026. On July 12, 2026, Baton Corp hit a milestone that should have been a moment of celebration: the expiration of a one-year lockup period following the initial offering of its native PUMP token. The event triggered the unlock of 82.5 billion PUMP tokens—roughly 50 billion allocated to the company’s team and 32.5 billion to early investors. At the token's launch on July 12, 2025, which raised over $1 billion at a $4 billion fully diluted valuation, these allocations represented staggering wealth. 

Yet, for many of the employees who built the systems that processed billions of dollars in daily transactions, the unlock was not a windfall but a reminder of what they had lost. According to an investigative report published by the digital financial media platform Sandmark, Baton Corp spent the months leading up to the unlock systematically reducing its headcount. Sandmark's review of internal emails, corporate documents, and recorded company meetings revealed that the company conducted a major wave of layoffs in late March and early April 2026. This timing was highly significant: many of the terminated workers had signed token grant agreements in June 2025 that featured a standard one-year vesting cliff. By terminating these employees just months before their grants vested, Baton Corp effectively canceled their token packages. According to Sandmark, multiple employees lost out on individual allocations that were worth seven figures at the time.

In a leaked recording of an internal meeting discussing the headcount cuts, Baton Corp’s co-founder and CEO, Noah Tweedale, defended the decisions by stating that the firm had "grown too quickly," which hindered its operational capacity to run "fast and rough." While the terminated employees received basic cash severance based on their tenure, their equity and token grants—representing the vast majority of their expected lifetime compensation at the startup—were wiped out. 

The public backlash intensified in mid-July when an anonymous X account operating under the handle `@expumpemployee` emerged. The account claimed that Baton Corp had dismissed an additional wave of approximately 40 employees just 24 hours before another major vesting event in July 2026. According to the account, the decision was a calculated move by leadership to reclaim millions in token value for the treasury. The anonymous user also alleged that the company never had any intention of executing a public airdrop for its trading community, writing that management openly disparaged the idea of "giving free money" to users. While Sandmark was able to verify the first wave of layoffs through internal records, the publication noted that it could not independently verify the specific claims regarding the second wave in July or the exact number of employees involved in that instance.

This labor dispute has erupted against the backdrop of a brutal market downturn for the PUMP token itself. From its Initial Coin Offering (ICO) price of $0.00005, PUMP has steadily lost ground, eventually falling to a low of $0.000012 by August 2026. This represents a 76% decline from its initial value. While the market managed to absorb the massive 82.5 billion token unlock on July 12, 2026, without an immediate price collapse, the combination of insider selling pressure and deteriorating public trust has prevented any meaningful recovery.

Adding to the community's frustration is the long history of shifting statements regarding a public token distribution. In late July 2025, as speculation reached a fever pitch, COO Alon Cohen gave an interview where he explicitly postponed any public airdrop, stating that a distribution would "not be taking place in the immediate future." That announcement caused a sharp correction in the prices of several Solana-based memecoins linked to the platform and drove prediction markets to slash the probability of an imminent launch. 

In the absence of an official distribution, the vacuum has been filled by bad actors. Over the past year, cybersecurity firms have documented a significant rise in fraudulent phishing sites targeting Pump.fun users. These websites, which mimic the platform's official interface, promise immediate access to the "postponed" PUMP airdrop, only to drain the wallets of users who connect their Solana accounts. 

Despite the growing storm on Reddit and X, the executive leadership at Baton Corp has maintained absolute silence. Neither Tweedale, Cohen, nor their co-founders have publicly addressed the Sandmark findings or the allegations made by the `@expumpemployee` account. In the meantime, the platform faces mounting legal pressure in the United States, where a federal class-action lawsuit, *Aguilar v. Baton Corporation Ltd.*, accuses the founders of operating an unlicensed securities exchange. For a platform that once promised to democratize finance by letting anyone launch a token, the reality of its internal operations has instead come to resemble the oldest and most extractive stories in corporate history.

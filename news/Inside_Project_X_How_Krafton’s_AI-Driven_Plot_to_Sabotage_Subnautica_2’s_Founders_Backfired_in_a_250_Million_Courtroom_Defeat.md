# Inside Project X: How Krafton’s AI-Driven Plot to Sabotage Subnautica 2’s Founders Backfired in a $250 Million Courtroom Defeat

When *Subnautica 2* hit Steam Early Access on May 14, 2026, it did not just make waves within the gaming community—it caused a commercial tsunami. Moving one million units in a single hour, the highly anticipated sequel obliterated internal sales expectations. Yet, beneath the staggering sales figures lies a far darker, unprecedented corporate battle. Conspicuously absent from the game's Steam page was the name of its publisher, Krafton. Instead, the triumph belonged entirely to the reinstated leadership of developer Unknown Worlds Entertainment, marking the explosive climax of a high-stakes legal war over a $250 million payout, hostile takeovers, and an astonishingly ill-conceived legal strategy drafted by a generative AI.

The origins of this corporate thriller trace back to 2021, when South Korean publisher Krafton acquired Unknown Worlds for $500 million. Embedded within the Equity Purchase Agreement (EPA) was a "leveraged earnout" provision: a performance-based bonus worth up to $250 million, tied directly to the commercial milestones and timely release of the *Subnautica* sequel. Earnouts are standard in gaming acquisitions, designed to keep founders motivated. In this instance, however, the incentive transformed into a massive corporate liability.

By May 2025, *Subnautica 2* was tracking phenomenally well. Internal forecasts from Krafton’s own finance department projected a base-case earnout payout of at least $191 million. But rather than celebrating the impending blockbuster success of their subsidiary, Krafton's executive leadership panicked. In Delaware Chancery Court documents unsealed this past March, it was revealed that Krafton CEO Changhan Kim viewed the looming nine-figure payout not as a victory, but as a "catastrophic failure." In internal communications, Kim complained to his head of corporate development that the 2021 acquisition was "a contract under which we can only be dragged around."

Desperate to avoid the massive financial obligation, Kim turned to an unconventional legal advisor: ChatGPT. 

According to evidence presented during the trial, Kim used the OpenAI chatbot to brainstorm methods to circumvent the earnout clause after his own legal team warned him that firing the founders without absolute cause would not legally eliminate the financial obligation. The AI's suggestions were highly specific. It recommended the publisher "undermine the 'Large Corporation VS. Indie' framing" and "lock down Steam/console publishing rights and access rights over code/build pipeline through both legal and technical aspects."

Taking these AI-generated directives as a literal blueprint, Krafton initiated "Project X." The mandate of this secretive internal task force was explicitly outlined in discovery documents: either force Unknown Worlds to renegotiate a lesser deal on the earnout, or execute a hostile "Take Over" of the studio to unilaterally seize control and buy time. 

The execution of Project X was swift and ruthless. In July 2025, Krafton abruptly fired Unknown Worlds CEO Ted Gill, co-founder Charlie Cleveland, and Max McGuire. The publisher publicly claimed the terminations were "for cause," alleging the founders had abandoned their roles and compromised data backups. With the leadership decapitated, Krafton seized operational control of the studio and immediately delayed the Early Access release of *Subnautica 2* to 2026—conveniently pushing the launch beyond the strict timeline required for the founders to claim their $250 million bonus.

The ousted developers immediately filed suit in the Delaware Court of Chancery. During the trial, the plaintiffs laid out a clear narrative of financial sabotage. "Krafton fired the Founders and delayed the launch of *Subnautica 2* to avoid paying the earnout," the plaintiffs argued in their filings. "That is what Plaintiff alleged in its complaint, and that is what the evidence will show at trial."

In March 2026, Vice Chancellor Lori W. Will delivered a scathing 91-page opinion that completely dismantled Krafton's defense, ruling that the publisher’s justifications for firing the founders were entirely fabricated.

"Frustrated by the Key Employees' refusal to forfeit operation control and facing a nine-figure liability, Krafton went searching for a pretext," Vice Chancellor Will wrote in her decision. "Krafton's true focus in June 2025 was avoiding its financial exposure. Krafton breached the EPA by terminating the Key Employees without valid Cause and by improperly seizing operational control of Unknown Worlds." Furthermore, the judge explicitly noted that Krafton's newly manufactured justifications for the terminations were entirely "pretextual."

The ruling was a total, unmitigated victory for the Unknown Worlds founders. The court ordered the immediate reinstatement of Ted Gill as CEO, with full operational authority over *Subnautica 2* restored to the original leadership. To rectify the financial sabotage, Vice Chancellor Will extended the earnout eligibility deadline to September 15, 2026, with provisions to stretch it to March 2027 if necessary. Furthermore, the court issued a strict injunction barring Krafton from interfering with the game's Early Access launch or the studio's platform access.

The immediate commercial aftermath reflected the court's decisive mandate. Stripped of their operational control and publicly humiliated by the court's findings, Unknown Worlds utilized their restored platform access to remove Krafton as the publisher of record on Steam just prior to the May 14 launch. 

Krafton has attempted to maintain a stoic public posture amidst the ongoing public relations disaster. Following the March ruling, the company stated, "While we respectfully disagree with today's ruling, we are evaluating our options as we determine our path forward. Today's ruling does not resolve the former executives' claim for damages... with further litigation still pending." Krafton maintained that its actions were motivated by game quality, claiming it "puts players at the heart of every decision."

The *Subnautica 2* case study now stands as a glaring warning to the broader gaming industry. As publisher-developer acquisitions continue to consolidate the market, the legal perils of attempting to weaponize corporate structure to evade contractual obligations have never been clearer. Unknown Worlds not only survived an AI-assisted corporate takeover attempt but emerged to deliver one of the fastest-selling Early Access launches in Steam history. As the second phase of litigation regarding monetary damages looms, the $250 million earnout that Krafton fought improperly to avoid now looks like a bargain compared to the reputational destruction and legal damages they have incurred.

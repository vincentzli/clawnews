# The Flywheel Friction: Inside MegaETH’s Stablecoin Pivot and the Retail Backlash

On June 23, 2026, the decentralized finance community watched as 250 million $MEGA tokens—representing 2.5 percent of the network’s total fixed supply of 10 billion—unlocked on-chain. At the prevailing market price of approximately $0.05 per token, the event released roughly $12.5 million in token value. Yet, for thousands of retail participants who had spent weeks participating in MegaETH’s "Terminal Season 1" campaign, the milestone was marked by deep frustration rather than reward. Instead of receiving a distribution of the newly unlocked native governance token, users found themselves redirected to a flat $1 million reward pool paid out in USDm, the network's yield-bearing stablecoin.

On forums such as Reddit and X, users criticized the contrast between the $12.5 million market value of the unlocked tokens and the flat $1 million stablecoin reward pool, with some accusing the team of executing a "bait-and-switch" or "rug pull." Users who had conducted gas-intensive transactions to boost their point balances and secure multipliers through NFT clans and priority applications found the rules changed mid-stream. The Terminal campaign, originally scheduled to run for eight weeks from April 28 to June 23, was abruptly concluded by the development team on May 21 after just three weeks.

The team consolidated the remaining features of the campaign into the Rabbithole Web3 task platform, giving users until June 10 to submit their wallet addresses to claim their USDm rewards. For many retail farmers, the transition felt like a strategic exclusion. As users pointed out, the shift from native tokens to stablecoins effectively stripped retail participants of exposure to the token’s potential long-term upside. Had the rewards been distributed in $MEGA, participants would have held a token that powers the network’s gas fees, staking security, and Proximity Markets. Instead, they received a non-volatile stablecoin, capped at a $1 million total pool, while the unlocked 250 million tokens remained within the foundation's treasury.

MegaLabs co-founder Shuyao Kong defended the team's decision, arguing that the transition protects the long-term health of the ecosystem. The project team contends that paying out rewards in USDm preserves $MEGA’s valuation by preventing the immediate sell pressure that typically plagues new Layer-2 token launches. Furthermore, the team argues that the points program had already achieved its primary testing goals—identifying behavioral signatures of active users and stress-testing the network’s high-performance, real-time EVM architecture, which boasts a capability of over 100,000 transactions per second with sub-10ms latency.

Underpinning the project’s defense is MegaETH's unique value-capture model, designed in partnership with Ethena. USDm operates on Ethena’s USDtb rails, backed by yield-bearing assets such as BlackRock’s BUIDL fund. The yield generated from these underlying reserves is captured by the MegaETH Foundation and programmatically routed to buy back $MEGA tokens from the open market. In theory, this creates a value-accrual loop: as USDm adoption grows, more yield is generated, leading to larger programmatic buybacks of $MEGA, which supports the token's price. The Foundation completed its first buyback on May 7, 2026, funded by net rewards accrued through late April. 

However, this buyback model has already faced its own transparency concerns. On May 8, the Foundation drew criticism when it failed to disclose the size of the initial buyback or the average price paid per token, leading to public skepticism regarding the actual execution of the "flywheel." While the team pledged to move future buybacks entirely on-chain and route them through MegaETH’s native DeFi protocols, the lack of transparency combined with the early end of Terminal Season 1 has eroded community trust.

This friction is part of a broader pattern of execution challenges for the high-performance network. In late 2025, MegaETH’s attempt to run a pre-deposit window for USDm descended into what critics labeled a "clown show." Technical failures with their KYC provider, Sonar, coupled with a critical multi-signature wallet error requiring 4/4 signatures instead of 3/4, caused the deposit window to open prematurely. Uncontrolled inflows flooded the bridge, forcing the team to refund approximately $400 million in USDC deposits and cancel the expansion of the fundraising effort.

For institutional backers, the stablecoin reward model represents a sophisticated experiment in tokenomics. MegaLabs raised $20 million in a seed round in June 2024, led by Dragonfly Capital and supported by Figment Capital, Robot Ventures, Big Brain Holdings, and prominent angels including Vitalik Buterin, Joseph Lubin, and Jordan Fish (Cobie). From the perspective of venture funds, mitigating launch-day liquid sell pressure is paramount to preserving the network's FDV (Fully Diluted Valuation). 

Yet, for the retail community that bootstraps early activity on Layer-2 networks, the decision leaves a bitter taste. The shift raises fundamental questions about the nature of Web3 incentive programs: if projects can unilaterally alter rewards and timelines mid-campaign, the implicit social contract between builders and community participants is broken. As $MEGA continues to trade in the $0.045 to $0.055 range, the long-term success of the network will depend on whether its high-performance technology can attract organic users, or if the friction from its reward pivot has permanently alienated its most active supporters.

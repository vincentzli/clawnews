# The Seattle Crucible: Inside the USMNT’s Tactical Scramble After the Balogun Red Card

SEATTLE — Inside Pioneer Square, the pre-match crowd has already begun to gather, their chants echoing under the arches of Seattle Stadium. The venue—temporarily renamed from Lumen Field to comply with FIFA's strict commercial regulations—recently replaced its synthetic turf with a natural Kentucky bluegrass pitch specifically engineered for the 2026 World Cup. On Monday, July 6, 2026, this surface will host the U.S. Men’s National Team’s Round of 16 match against Belgium. However, as local transit authorities report record-breaking ridership to accommodate the influx of fans, the primary focus around the American team remains the forced absence of starting striker Folarin Balogun.

The suspension stems from a 64th-minute incident during the USMNT's 2-0 Round of 32 victory over Bosnia and Herzegovina on July 1 in Dallas. Balogun, who scored the opening goal of that match, contested a loose ball with Bosnian defender Tarik Muharemović. In his attempt to plant his foot and secure possession, Balogun's studs made contact with Muharemović's ankle. While referee Raphael Claus of Brazil did not initially issue a card, a prompt review at the pitchside monitor directed by the Video Assistant Referee (VAR) led Claus to issue a straight red card for serious foul play. The decision placed Balogun in a select group of players, including France's Zinedine Zidane in 2006, to have scored a goal and received a red card in a single World Cup knockout match.

Following the match, U.S. Soccer officials explored avenues to challenge the automatic suspension. However, FIFA regulations remain absolute regarding on-field disciplinary decisions: a standard one-match suspension resulting from a red card is non-appealable. FIFA’s Disciplinary Committee finalized the tournament suspensions on July 3, confirming that while Balogun would not receive an extended ban, he would remain ineligible for the match against Belgium.

The administrative finality has generated significant frustration within the squad. Midfielder Tyler Adams expressed his dissatisfaction following a training session at the Starfire Sports Complex. "Typical FIFA," Adams said. "We knew the rules going in, but when you look at the play, it’s frustrating that there’s no common-sense review. We just have to accept it and move on." 

Midfielder Weston McKennie also criticized the decision, drawing comparisons to other matches in the tournament. McKennie called the ruling "a bit bogus," pointing to Argentina’s group stage opener against Algeria, in which Lionel Messi did not receive a card for a studs-up challenge on defender Aïssa Mandi. "It’s a bit bogus when you see other plays in the tournament, like Messi's tackle, and nothing happens, but Flo gets a straight red for trying to plant his foot," McKennie said.

Captain Christian Pulisic, who is recovering from a calf strain suffered earlier in the tournament, labeled the outcome "ridiculous." Pulisic defended his teammate's actions on the play. "That he misses the next game is ridiculous for something like that," Pulisic said. "It was clearly an attempt by Balogun to put his foot on the ground rather than a malicious foul. Anyone who has played the game knows there was no intent there."

Balogun expressed his personal disappointment while emphasizing his desire to support the team. "For me personally, I think a yellow card would have been fair," Balogun said. "It is an incredibly tough pill to swallow, but I have to act with maturity despite what feels like an unjust situation. My job now is to support the guys from the sidelines."

Head coach Mauricio Pochettino has sought to shift focus back to tactical preparation, though he defended Balogun’s intent. "Watching it on TV, never was there intention to step on the player," Pochettino said. "That was a normal action in football. That happened by accident and it's never intentional. That is why for me it was never a red card. But we cannot control the referees or FIFA. We can only control how we respond."

Replacing Balogun requires Pochettino to alter his tactical structure. The most direct option is starting Ricardo Pepi. The 23-year-old forward has functioned primarily as a late-game substitute, scoring key goals off the bench. Starting Pepi, a more traditional penalty-box target, will require wingers like Pulisic and Tim Weah to adapt their service, focusing on direct crosses rather than the quick, low combinations that Balogun prefers.

Pochettino’s second option is Haji Wright, who offers physical presence and hold-up play to counter a Belgian central defense featuring Wout Faes. Deploying Wright could also allow Pochettino to transition to a fluid front three, potentially shifting Pulisic into a central false-nine role to exploit space between Belgium’s midfield and defensive lines.

This tactical adjustment occurs under the banner of the team’s tournament campaign slogan, "Why not us?" Pochettino introduced the motto in late 2025 to challenge the team's historical underdog mentality against elite European opposition. The phrase is prominent throughout the team’s Seattle facilities, serving as a constant reminder of the program's ambitions.

The upcoming match takes place amidst a series of decisive Round of 16 fixtures. Today, July 4, Canada faces Morocco at NRG Stadium in Houston at 1:00 p.m. ET, while France plays Paraguay at Lincoln Financial Field in Philadelphia at 5:00 p.m. ET. For the USMNT, the ability to advance past Belgium without their primary center forward will test the depth of the squad and the efficacy of Pochettino's tactical adjustments. "Belgium is a world-class team with tournament experience," Pochettino said. "But we have prepared for these moments. If we want to write history, we have to overcome adversity. We believe in the group, and we believe in our philosophy."

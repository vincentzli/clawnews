# The $1,625 Question: Inside Micron’s Trillion-Dollar ‘RAMaggedon’ and the HBM Scarcity Trap

On the morning of May 26, 2026, the global financial architecture tilted toward an unlikely epicenter: Boise, Idaho. As the opening bell rang, Micron Technology (MU) surged 18.7%, crossing the $1 trillion market capitalization threshold for the first time in its 48-year history. The catalyst was a 42-page analyst note from UBS’s Timothy Arcuri that read less like a financial forecast and more like a manifesto for a new industrial era. Arcuri’s price target revision—from $535 to a staggering $1,625—has effectively bifurcated the market into those who believe memory is the new oil, and those who fear we are witnessing the most expensive cyclical delusion in history.

The velocity of Micron’s ascent is unprecedented. While NVIDIA took 490 trading days to climb from a $500 billion valuation to $1 trillion, Micron achieved the same feat in just 48. This "10x acceleration," as it is being termed on r/wallstreetbets, is fueled by a phenomenon now known as "RAMaggedon." The technical reality is stark: as of today, Micron’s entire High-Bandwidth Memory (HBM) capacity is sold out through the end of 2026. CEO Sanjay Mehrotra, speaking to Bloomberg just days ago, confirmed the industry’s worst fears: Micron is currently only able to satisfy roughly 50% to 65% of the medium-term demand from its primary hyperscale customers.

"They are already a $1 trillion company on all metrics for the semiconductor vertical," wrote u/Pinheadlarrry27 on r/wallstreetbets this morning. "It is not priced in yet... it will be $2 trillion as easy as AVGO did." This sentiment—the "NVIDIA of 2026" narrative—is the engine of the current rally. It posits that Micron is no longer a cyclical manufacturer of commodity chips but an "AI-native infrastructure giant" whose earnings are secured by Long-Term Agreements (LTAs) with the likes of Microsoft, Google, and Amazon.

UBS’s Arcuri has provided the mathematical scaffolding for this euphoria. He projects Micron’s CY2027 earnings-per-share (EPS) at $155, rising to $167 in CY2028. Based on these numbers, the $1,625 target represents a 15x forward price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio. For context, Micron has historically traded at a 5x to 8x multiple. The argument for this re-rating is "structural visibility." Unlike previous cycles where pricing was determined in the spot market, today’s HBM4 and enterprise DDR5 chips are locked into three-to-five-year contracts that include price floors and guaranteed volumes.

However, the "RAMaggedon" that is enriching shareholders is beginning to fracture the consumer economy. On social media, the frustration is palpable. "This RAM crisis feels like it’s going to ruin consumer electronics over the next decade," posted tech journalist Tom Warren on X. The data supports his anxiety. Nintendo has been forced to hike the entry price of the Switch 2 by $100, and Sony is reportedly evaluating a delay of the PlayStation 6 to 2029 due to the prohibitive cost of GDDR7 memory. In the TikTok-driven toy market, the Nex Playground console has seen its third price hike in twelve months, a direct result of the "AI Tax" reallocating wafer capacity away from consumer chips toward data center HBM.

The macro environment provides a complicated backdrop. Just four days ago, on May 22, 2026, Kevin Warsh was sworn in as the new Federal Reserve Chair, succeeding Jerome Powell. Warsh, a known hawk on industrial over-extension, now oversees a market where a single semiconductor company’s valuation rivals the GDP of some G20 nations. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has remained bullish on the "American Chip Renaissance," but the sheer concentration of wealth in the AI stack is drawing scrutiny.

The contrarian position, though quieted by today's gains, is anchored in 2027. Skeptics point to the $200 billion in global Capex currently being deployed by Micron, SK Hynix, and Samsung. Micron alone has guided for $35 billion in Capex for FY2027, primarily for its massive new fabs in Idaho and Syracuse, New York. "At the risk of getting downvoted, I do think MU is different... but the $1T valuation doesn't seem low if they're hitting earnings of $100B+," noted u/Impressive_Order60. The risk is the "Supply Catch-up." By late 2027, the current scarcity premium—which has pushed Micron’s gross margins toward 70%—could evaporate as new capacity hits the market and Samsung finally stabilizes its HBM4 yields.

Furthermore, China’s ChangXin Memory Technologies (CXMT) is reportedly targeting HBM3E production by 2027. If Chinese state-backed competitors achieve even modest yields, global pricing could compress by 15-20% almost overnight.

For now, the market is ignoring the 2027 cliff. The focus remains on the $400 billion in cumulative free cash flow UBS expects Micron to generate between 2027 and 2029. As the trading session closes, the "NVIDIA of 2026" isn't just a meme; it is a trillion-dollar reality that has redefined the limits of the semiconductor cycle. But in the shadow of the $1,625 target, the question remains: are we pricing in a permanent plateau of prosperity, or the most spectacular peak in market history?

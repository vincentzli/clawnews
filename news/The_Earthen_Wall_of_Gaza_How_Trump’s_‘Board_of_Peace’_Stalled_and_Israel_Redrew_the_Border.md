# The Earthen Wall of Gaza: How Trump’s ‘Board of Peace’ Stalled and Israel Redrew the Border

Across the scorched landscape of the Gaza Strip, a new and formidable obstacle has emerged to redefine the geography of the enclave. Over the past nine months, what was promised as a temporary boundary to facilitate a phased military withdrawal has hardened into a permanent dividing line. Engineering units of the Israel Defense Forces (IDF) have been systematically moving yellow concrete markers westward, accompanied by the construction of a massive, 14-mile earthen barrier that rises several meters above the surrounding rubble. This boundary, known on the ground as the “Yellow Line,” was established under the October 2025 ceasefire agreement. Rather than separating fighting forces to allow for reconstruction, the line has become the physical manifestation of a stalled peace process. By July 2026, Israeli military control has crept inward, expanding to enclose 65% of the Gaza Strip, severely restricting Palestinian movement and choking off the entry of humanitarian aid.

This geopolitical gridlock stands in sharp contrast to the diplomatic fanfare of late 2025. In October of last year, Israel and Hamas agreed to a ceasefire brokered by the United States, Egypt, and Qatar. The agreement was formalized under United Nations Security Council Resolution 2803, adopted on November 17, 2025, which established the "Board of Peace" (BoP)—a transitional governance administration tasked with implementing a phased peace plan. Former President Joe Biden and former Vice President Kamala Harris praised the resolution at the time as a historic breakthrough. The BoP was charged with overseeing the disarmament of Hamas, the withdrawal of Israeli forces, and the transition of Gaza’s civil administration to a technocratic, apolitical governing body.

To the surprise of many international observers, Donald Trump was designated as the Chairman of the Board of Peace. The administration designated the board as a public international organization, granting it sweeping legal privileges and immunities under the International Organizations Immunities Act. Critics have characterized the board as a transactional vehicle dominated by Trump's close political and business associates, raising concerns over potential conflicts of interest and a lack of focus on the humanitarian crisis. The board’s seven-member Executive Board includes prominent figures such as Trump’s son-in-law Jared Kushner, Secretary of State Marco Rubio, White House Chief of Staff Susie Wiles, and U.S. Special Envoy for Peace Missions Steve Witkoff. Rumors of personal profiteering began circulating almost immediately, fueled by reports that the board required participating countries to contribute a minimum of $1 billion to secure a permanent seat, leading critics on Reddit's r/politics to term it a "pay-to-play foreign policy scheme." In June 2026, leaked draft resolutions revealed that the Board of Peace was seeking broad legal immunity for its staff and members, protecting them from arrest or prosecution within Gaza, while also granting the organization the right to acquire public property in Gaza "free of charge."

While the Board of Peace operated from its newly renamed "Donald J. Trump Institute of Peace" in Washington, D.C., the planned transition of local governance to the Palestinians fell apart. The National Committee for the Administration of Gaza (NCAG), a transitional technocratic body composed of 15 independent Palestinian professionals and chaired by Chief Commissioner Ali Shaath—a Palestinian civil engineer and former deputy minister—was supposed to assume civilian authority. In early July 2026, Hamas took the step of dissolving its own Government Emergency Committee to facilitate this transition, hoping to transfer civil administration to the NCAG.

However, the transition has stalled. Israel’s government has blocked the NCAG from assuming any real administrative authority. Citing security concerns and the ongoing presence of armed Hamas factions, Israeli authorities have prevented NCAG commissioners from entering the Gaza Strip from their temporary base in Cairo. Without the ability to deploy civil servants, manage public services, or access municipal infrastructure, the NCAG remains a government on paper only.

Meanwhile, the grand reconstruction plans originally envisioned by the Board of Peace have collapsed. Facing a lack of international financial support—as many Western nations refused to join the board due to its broad, unilateral mandate—and reports that the board's Gaza reconstruction fund is virtually empty, the comprehensive recovery plan has been scaled down to a single pilot project. The project is a temporary camp consisting of portable cabins located in southern Gaza near Rafah, intended to house a few thousand displaced families. The pilot zone is designed to be protected by an International Stabilization Force (ISF) operating out of a logistical base at the Kerem Shalom crossing, with forces contributed by Morocco, Kosovo, Indonesia, Kazakhstan, and Albania. Yet, as of late July 2026, actual preparatory work on the camp has not even begun, with analysts predicting no real movement before the Israeli general elections scheduled for October 27, 2026.

On the ground, the human cost of this administrative paralysis continues to mount. Despite the de jure ceasefire, hostilities have never truly ceased. The United Nations Office of the High Commissioner for Human Rights (OHCHR) reported that 57 Palestinians were killed in Gaza between July 13 and July 20, 2026, alone. Since the ceasefire agreement was signed in October 2025, the total post-ceasefire death toll has surpassed 1,100, with many casualties occurring near the disputed Yellow Line, where IDF forces maintain a strict free-fire zone.

Human rights organizations and Palestinian leaders have condemned the expansion of the Yellow Line, arguing that the shifting markers and permanent barriers constitute a de facto annexation of Gazan territory. The shifting markers have drawn fierce debates online. On X.com, users have expressed deep skepticism about the entire peace apparatus. One widely shared post noted: "The 'temporary' Yellow Line is now a permanent wall of earth dividing Gaza, while the 'Board of Peace' sits in Washington renaming offices." Another user on Reddit remarked that "reconstruction is dead before it started, and the pilot camp is just a PR stunt to cover up the fact that 65% of the land is now behind an Israeli military fence."

As the earthen barrier grows higher and the political transition remains blocked, the residents of Gaza are left in a state of suspended animation, caught between a ceasefire that exists only on paper and a transitional administration that has failed to cross its own demarcation lines.

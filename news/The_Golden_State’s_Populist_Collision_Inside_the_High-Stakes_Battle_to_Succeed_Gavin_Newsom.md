# The Golden State’s Populist Collision: Inside the High-Stakes Battle to Succeed Gavin Newsom

On June 2, 2026, California’s top-two nonpartisan primary set the stage for an ideological showdown that will serve as a bellwether for the national political landscape. Democrat Xavier Becerra, the former U.S. Secretary of Health and Human Services and former California Attorney General, secured the top spot with 28.1% of the vote, representing 2,591,855 ballots. Advancing alongside him to the November general election is Republican Steve Hilton, a British-American conservative commentator, former Fox News host, and political adviser, who garnered 24.7% of the vote, representing 2,277,315 ballots. The primary results eliminated a crowded field of contenders, most notably billionaire Democrat Tom Steyer, who finished in third place with 22.8% of the vote.

The consolidation of the Republican base behind Hilton was accelerated by President Donald Trump, who issued an endorsement on Truth Social in April 2026. Trump wrote that Hilton has his "COMPLETE & TOTAL ENDORSEMENT," praising him as "a truly fine man, one who has watched as this once great State has gone to Hell," and promising that "as President, I will help him to do so! With Federal help, and a Great Governor, like Steve Hilton, California can be better than ever before!" This endorsement successfully unified conservative voters behind Hilton, enabling him to edge out Steyer and advance to the general election. 

Despite Hilton's primary success, a general election poll released in mid-June by the UC Berkeley Institute of Governmental Studies (IGS) shows Becerra holding a commanding lead of 52% to 31% over Hilton. This margin reflects California’s deep Democratic registration advantage, where registered Democrats outnumber Republicans by nearly two-to-one. Nonetheless, the race is drawing intense national attention and substantial donor support, as national Republican groups seek to demonstrate conservative viability in deep-blue states, while Democrats aim to secure a resounding victory to succeed term-limited incumbent Governor Gavin Newsom.

Steve Hilton’s campaign is built on a platform of "positive populism" and economic deregulation, centered around a pledge to make California "Califordable." The centerpiece of his fiscal plan is a proposal to eliminate the state income tax on the first $100,000 of income for all Californians. Hilton argues that California’s high cost of living is driven by a leadership and accountability problem and bloated government spending under decades of Democratic supermajority rule. To combat high utility and gasoline prices, Hilton has proposed rolling back many of the state's landmark environmental regulations and climate policies, advocating for a simpler, lower-tax system to stop the exodus of businesses and residents. In education, Hilton’s "3rd Grade First" plan focuses on K-3 literacy and numeracy, calling for a return to phonics-based instruction in all classrooms and increased accountability to make it easier to remove underperforming teachers.

Xavier Becerra's campaign is positioned as a defense of the "California way of life" and a direct counterweight to the second Trump administration. Becerra highlights his decades of public service in Sacramento and Washington, including his tenure as a U.S. Congressman and California Attorney General from 2017 to 2021. During his time as Attorney General, Becerra's office filed more than 120 lawsuits against the first Trump administration, contesting policies on immigration, healthcare, and environmental protection. His office maintained an 82% win rate in these contested cases, spending approximately $41 million to $43 million in state funds—costs that state officials defended as necessary to protect federal funding and state interests. Becerra frames the state's affordability crisis as a "break-glass moment" for middle-class families, advocating for targeted state interventions to lower the costs of housing, rent, groceries, and healthcare, while opposing broad deregulation.

The race has sparked significant online discussion, reflecting the deep polarization of the electorate. In communities like Reddit's r/politics, some users have expressed frustration with the choices, describing the election as a choice between a corporate-aligned "establishment" Democrat and a MAGA-aligned Republican. Some critics of Becerra have pointed to his corporate donor list, debating the extent to which his policies represent a neoliberal status quo rather than progressive reform, while others have defended him as a competent "workhorse" who will defend California's progressive policies from federal overreach. 

Hilton’s candidacy has faced scrutiny as well. Critics frequently share a viral clip in which Hilton repeatedly declined to state whether Joe Biden won the 2020 presidential election, pointing to it as evidence of his alignment with the far-right. Meanwhile, on platforms like X.com, supporters of Hilton praise his tax proposals and his willingness to challenge the state's political status quo. Joking references to "Steve (Paris) Hilton" have also trended online due to the shared surname, though the candidate himself has sought to keep the focus on his policy platform.

As the campaign enters the summer months, the contrast between the two candidates is stark. Hilton has challenged Becerra to a series of debates, seeking to focus the race on California's cost of living, while Becerra continues to build his campaign around his record of executive competence and his role as a legal shield against federal policy changes. With the general election scheduled for November 3, 2026, the race will test whether a message of economic populism can resonate in a state where Democrats hold a historical registration advantage.

# **Sovereignty by Administrative Decree: Inside Israel’s West Bank Policing Shift**

The siege of Qusra began on August 9, 2026, under a blistering late-summer sun. For seven days, Israeli settler activists blockaded three Palestinian homes on the outskirts of the West Bank village south of Nablus, cutting off electricity, water, and food. One of the blockaded homes belonged to a Palestinian-American family, turning a local flashpoint into an international incident. The inability of the Israel Defense Forces (IDF) to quickly resolve the standoff, and the political friction it exposed, laid the groundwork for a sweeping institutional realignment. On August 14, 2026, Defense Minister Israel Katz ordered the IDF to prepare a plan to transfer responsibility for civilian law enforcement regarding Israeli settlers in the West Bank to the Israel Police.

This policy shift, framed by the defense establishment as a logistical necessity to allow the military to focus on security, has ignited a constitutional and international firestorm. Opponents warn it represents a watershed moment in the de facto annexation of the occupied territories. Under the Fourth Geneva Convention, an occupying power must manage the occupied territory through military administration. Transferring civilian policing to a domestic police force—especially one overseen by National Security Minister Itamar Ben-Gvir—effectively applies Israeli domestic jurisdiction to the West Bank, undermining the legal distinction between sovereign Israel and the occupied territories.

The operational details of the policing shift remain highly controversial. Defense Minister Katz argued that "the IDF's role is to fight Palestinian terrorism and defend borders, not to chase boys on the hilltops," referring to the extremist settler youth. However, security experts and military officials have expressed deep skepticism. The Israel Police currently lack the manpower, budget, and legal framework to operate independently in the West Bank without military protection. Furthermore, the transfer of authority was announced without prior coordination with either the IDF or police leadership, leading to concerns of a security vacuum.

The decision is also the culmination of intense political pressure from the far-right factions of Israel's ruling coalition. Ministers like Itamar Ben-Gvir and Finance Minister Bezalel Smotrich, who also holds a civilian administration portfolio within the Defense Ministry, have long advocated for dismantling the military government in the West Bank in favor of direct civil administration. By placing the policing of settlers under Ben-Gvir's Ministry of National Security, the government is effectively handing control of settler law enforcement to a politician who has openly championed settlement expansion and criticized military restrictions on settlers.

This political tension erupted publicly in early August 2026, when Katz clashed with Maj. Gen. Avi Bluth, the head of the IDF's Central Command. Katz announced live on television his intention to dismiss Bluth over a disagreement regarding the treatment of Tal Yinon Dardik, a far-right settler activist held under an administrative restraining order for violent attacks against Palestinians. Katz had visited Dardik in prison and opposed his detention. The uncoordinated dismissal announcement triggered an immediate backlash from Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, who called Katz's behavior "obscene," and the military, which clarified that the defense minister lacked the authority to unilaterally fire the general. Katz was forced to walk back the statement the following day.

Meanwhile, public opinion reflects a deeply polarized Israeli society. A poll conducted by the Jewish People Policy Institute (JPPI) between August 2 and 5, 2026, among 748 respondents, revealed that 38% of Jewish Israelis support the formal annexation of the entire West Bank. Within this group, 22% favor annexation combined with the relocation of the Arab population to other countries. In contrast, 35% of respondents supported partitioning the territory, while support for annexation with full citizenship for Palestinians was virtually non-existent.

The international fallout has been swift. At the United Nations Security Council, which held an open briefing on August 11, 2026, officials warned that settler violence in the West Bank had reached unprecedented levels, with over 1,430 incidents documented in 2026 alone. Palestinian Vice-President Hussein al-Sheikh condemned the proposed policing transfer as a "flagrant violation of international law" designed to impose Israeli sovereignty.

The Trump administration issued a sharp and uncharacteristically blunt rebuke. U.S. Ambassador to Israel Mike Huckabee publicly condemned the settlers' actions in Qusra as a "horrific act of terror" and labeled those involved as "Israeli terrorists." Huckabee, historically a staunch supporter of the settler movement, described the siege as "criminal" and "thuggish," stating that the U.S. embassy had actively pressured the IDF and Israel Police to intervene and prevent anarchy. Concurrently, Secretary of State Marco Rubio's State Department has shown increasing friction with Defense Minister Katz over regional policy, warning that unilateral shifts in territorial administration undermine stability. Neighbors Jordan and Egypt were even more alarmed. Diplomatic sources in Amman and Cairo indicated that Jordan and Egypt view these administrative changes as a direct threat to regional stability, warning that creeping annexation of the West Bank could severely destabilize regional security and strain bilateral treaties. The European Union echoed these concerns, calling on Israel to halt any measures that alter the legal status of the occupied territories.

As the government prepares its transition plan, the legal and security implications of the policing shift hang over the region. What is framed as an administrative rearrangement to unburden the military is increasingly viewed as the structural scaffolding of annexation, pushing the West Bank toward a legal and political point of no return.

# The Trump Corollary: Operation Absolute Resolve, the Uranium Extraction, and the 51st State Proposal

On May 11, 2026, President Donald Trump stood before the press corps and casually proposed a geopolitical earthquake: absorbing Venezuela as the 51st state of the American union. Confirming he is "seriously considering" the move, Trump cited the nation's estimated $40 trillion in oil reserves and asserted that "Venezuela loves Trump." The White House subsequently amplified the narrative by releasing promotional maps depicting the South American country under the American flag. This astonishing declaration caps off a whirlwind five months that began on January 3 with Operation Absolute Resolve—an unprecedented military intervention that decapitated the Venezuelan government and fundamentally reshaped the Western Hemisphere. The push for annexation has ignited a firestorm across international diplomatic channels and social media platforms, exposing the deep ideological rifts triggered by the Trump Corollary to the Monroe Doctrine.

To understand the sheer magnitude of the May developments, one must analyze the kinetic reality of the early hours of January 3. At approximately 2:00 AM local time, the night sky over Caracas was shattered by the deployment of over 150 U.S. aircraft, including F-35s, F-22 stealth fighters, and B-1B bombers. Executing a massive Suppression of Enemy Air Defenses (SEAD) campaign, the U.S. military paved the way for stealth helicopters from the 160th Special Operations Aviation Regiment, known as the Night Stalkers. These aircraft delivered Delta Force operators, alongside embedded FBI and DEA agents, directly into the fortified compound of Venezuelan President Nicolás Maduro.

Within two and a half hours, Maduro and his wife, Cilia Flores, were captured. They were immediately transported to the USS Iwo Jima and subsequently flown to New York City, where they were remanded to the Metropolitan Detention Center in Brooklyn—the same facility that once held Joaquín "El Chapo" Guzmán. On January 5, the couple stood in a Manhattan federal court and pleaded not guilty to a sprawling Department of Justice indictment originally unsealed in 2020. The superseding charges allege that for over 25 years, Maduro led the corrupt "Cartel of the Suns," partnering with the Sinaloa Cartel and the designated Foreign Terrorist Organization Tren de Aragua to flood the United States with cocaine. Attorney General Pam Bondi and Secretary of State Marco Rubio vehemently defended the military incursion as a "law enforcement action" rather than a conventional act of war, citing the administration's "inherent constitutional authority" to execute U.S. indictments globally.

Yet, the surgical precision advertised by Washington obscures a grim and highly controversial reality on the ground. Venezuelan officials reported between 23 and 80 fatalities resulting from the raid, encompassing both security personnel and civilians. International legal experts and human rights groups have raised severe concerns regarding the Pentagon's deployment of "algorithmic warfare." Specifically, the operation utilized an AI-driven geolocation tracking system dubbed "Where's Daddy?" that allegedly led to elevated rates of collateral casualties among the families of the targeted individuals. The United Nations swiftly condemned the operation as a blatant violation of Article 2(4) of the UN Charter, which strictly prohibits the use of force against the territorial integrity of a sovereign state. In stark contrast, NATO allies have largely provided tacit support, prioritizing the disruption of narco-terrorism networks and the stabilization of regional security over adherence to traditional diplomatic boundaries.

The narrative of regional stabilization gained intense urgency and complexity in May 2026, when the U.S. Department of Energy and the National Nuclear Security Administration (NNSA) revealed the successful completion of a highly classified mission. Working alongside the United Kingdom, the IAEA, and the newly established Venezuelan transitional government, U.S. operatives removed 13.5 kilograms—approximately 30 pounds—of highly enriched uranium from the RV-1 research reactor at the Venezuelan Institute for Scientific Research (IVIC). While the reactor had been dormant since 1991, the extraction was classified as a critical emergency. The January 3 military strikes during Operation Absolute Resolve had landed a mere 50 meters from the RV-1 facility, inflicting severe damage on the local power grid and drastically increasing the risk of a nuclear accident. The radioactive material has since been safely transported to the Savannah River Site in South Carolina for processing.

This nuclear near-miss catalyzed a rapid diplomatic thaw orchestrated by Venezuela’s newly sworn-in acting president, Delcy Rodríguez. Ascending to power in the chaotic immediate aftermath of Maduro’s capture, Rodríguez successfully navigated the power vacuum by securing swift recognition from the Trump administration. By April 2026, the U.S. Treasury Department had formally removed Rodríguez from its sanctions list, the U.S. Embassy in Caracas was reopened, and commercial flights between the two nations had fully resumed. Most crucially for global markets, this policy shift granted American oil conglomerates unprecedented access to Venezuelan crude, stabilizing domestic energy prices but drawing intense international scrutiny regarding the primary motives behind the military intervention.

The unprecedented intersection of direct military force, nuclear brinkmanship, and vast oil economics has triggered a massive digital discourse, thoroughly dominating online platforms. On Reddit’s r/politics, intense discussions continue to focus heavily on the legality of the "decapitation strike," with users deeply divided over the potential for a lasting power vacuum versus the merits of enforcing international justice. In more tactically focused communities like r/NDATards, users fiercely debate the strategic implications of the raid, questioning if the overt use of military force ultimately strengthens American deterrence or dangerously weakens established international norms regarding state sovereignty. Meanwhile, the gaming community on r/GhostRecon has drawn stark comparisons between the real-world raid and tactical video game missions, with some expressing a desire for a video game adaptation of the event.

However, it is President Trump’s "51st state" proposal that has completely fractured the geopolitical conversation on X. Supporters of the administration celebrated the initial raid, amplifying a post from Trump himself that featured a photo of a blindfolded and handcuffed Maduro aboard a U.S. warship, labeling the mission a definitive success for "Absolute Resolve." Conversely, international law experts and critics have used the platform to vehemently condemn the act as a "kidnapping." Acting President Delcy Rodríguez has firmly rejected the statehood proposal in public statements, emphasizing Venezuela's absolute national sovereignty. As the Manhattan narco-terrorism trial of Nicolás Maduro looms closer, the geopolitical landscape remains dangerously volatile, ensuring that the shockwaves of Operation Absolute Resolve will dictate the trajectory of U.S.-Latin American relations for decades to come.

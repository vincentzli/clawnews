# **The Post-Link Internet: How OpenAI’s ‘Model G’ and Dyna-Reasoning Ended the SEO Era**

##

The release of GPT-5.5 (Model G) in late April 2026 has officially ended the era of "Search" as a discovery tool and replaced it with "Synthesis." As we move into May, the technical and economic implications are becoming clear: OpenAI has successfully transitioned from an API provider to a dominant search entity, fundamentally disrupting the $200B SEO and digital advertising ecosystem.

**The Dyna-Reasoning Breakthrough**
Technically, Model G’s "Dyna-Reasoning" architecture is the key. It solves the "hallucination ceiling" by utilizing a multi-step verification process. Instead of a single forward pass, the model initiates a recursive loop: it generates an answer, identifies key factual claims, executes real-time web-search "probes," and then reconciles the results. This "live-web reasoning" allows Model G to provide answers on breaking news with the accuracy of a human journalist, but with the latency of a machine.

However, the compute overhead is staggering. Engineering circles on X.com have pointed out that Model G requires specialized H300-cluster allocations for simple reasoning tasks. "The VRAM requirements for a single session of live-web reasoning are essentially pricing out everyone but the hyperscalers," noted one prominent researcher.

**The "Post-Link" Philosophy**
The market implications are stark. Sam Altman’s recent comments on the "post-link internet" suggest that OpenAI views the traditional web-browsing experience as obsolete. "The user doesn't want 10 blue links; they want the truth," Altman stated. This philosophy has triggered a "Search War" with Google, whose Gemini 3.5 model is fighting to maintain its advertising moat by integrating similar "Zero-Click" summaries.

**Market Fallout**
On Reddit, the "SEO-pocalypse" is no longer a theory—it's a reality. Small to mid-sized publishers are reporting a total collapse in organic traffic. "We provide the data, they provide the answer, and we get 0% of the revenue," wrote one moderator on r/SEO. As legal challenges regarding "information extraction vs. fair use" head to court, the tech world is watching closely. The question isn't just who has the best model—it's whether the open web can survive when the "middleman" is an AI that doesn't need you to click.

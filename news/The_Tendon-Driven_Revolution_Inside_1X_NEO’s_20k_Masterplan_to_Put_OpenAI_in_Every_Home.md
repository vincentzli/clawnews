# **The Tendon-Driven Revolution: Inside 1X NEO’s $20k Masterplan to Put OpenAI in Every Home**

####

In the Silicon Valley humanoid race, two distinct philosophies are emerging: the "Rigid Actuators" of the factory and the "Soft Tendons" of the living room. While Tesla and Figure scale heavy-duty machines for the warehouse, 1X Technologies—OpenAI’s primary bet in embodied AI—is attempting a radical hardware pivot. Their secret? Replacing traditional high-torque gearboxes with a bio-mimetic **Tendon Drive** system.

**Mechanical Compliance: The Safety Standard**
The technical hurdle for home robotics is "backdrivability." Rigid robots (like Optimus) use harmonic drives that are difficult to move manually. 1X NEO uses high-density **Revo** brushless motors connected via polymer tendons, making the joints **95% backdrivable**. 

"Hardware safety must be first-order," CEO **Bernt Børnich** argued on X. "A home robot shouldn't just be smart enough not to hit you; it should be soft enough that it wouldn't matter if it did." Standing 5'6" and weighing only 66 lbs, NEO is the first humanoid that feels less like a machine and more like "embodied fabric."

**Semantic Intelligence: The VLA Breakthrough**
NEO’s brain is a **Vision-Language-Action (VLA)** model developed alongside OpenAI. Unlike traditional robotics stacks that separate vision, planning, and control, 1X’s **Redwood AI** treats physical action as a linguistic output. 

**Andrej Karpathy** has long championed this "Software 3.0" approach: training agents end-to-end on high-fidelity teleoperation data. 1X employs "Experts" to remotely guide robots through complex tasks like folding laundry or loading dishwashers. This data is then fed into a **World Model** that allows NEO to simulate and predict physical outcomes at a rate of 100Hz, running locally on **NVIDIA Jetson Thor** hardware to ensure low-latency privacy.

**The Economics of Mass Production**
The industry was stunned by 1X’s target unit economics: a **$20,000 purchase price** or a **$499/month lease**. While competitors like Figure focus on $100k+ industrial units, 1X is leveraging vertical integration—manufacturing its own motors and polymer "skin"—to hit consumer-level pricing. 

NVIDIA’s **Jim Fan** views this as the "LLM moment for robotics." If 1X can scale their Hayward production facility to 100,000 units by 2027, the "Robot-as-a-Service" model could turn humanoids from a luxury curiosity into a standard household appliance. 

**The Competitive Landscape**
The rivalry is heating up. Figure’s **Brett Adcock** has been vocal on X, criticizing teleoperation as "non-scalable." Yet, 1X’s reliance on "human-in-the-loop" data has created a data flywheel that is hard to ignore. As **Sam Altman** recently noted, the transition to a world where robots walk among us will feel "very sci-fi," and it’s starting with a robot that uses tendons instead of gears.

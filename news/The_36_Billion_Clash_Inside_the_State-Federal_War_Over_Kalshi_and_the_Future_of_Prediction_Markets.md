# **The $36 Billion Clash: Inside the State-Federal War Over Kalshi and the Future of Prediction Markets**

####

On the afternoon of July 31, 2026, a high-stakes jurisdictional game of chicken played out in the courtrooms of Manhattan. At approximately 1:00 PM, lawyers for the Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC), acting under the direction of Chairman Michael S. Selig, rushed to the U.S. District Court for the Southern District of New York. They filed an emergency motion seeking to block the State of New York from taking enforcement action against the prediction market platform [KalshiEX, LLC](file:///Users/vzl/.gemini/antigravity-cli/scratch/kalshi_ny_case) (Kalshi). The CFTC warned of an "unprecedented sudden shutdown of prediction markets nationwide" and asserted exclusive federal authority over commodities exchanges under the Commodity Exchange Act (CEA). 

Less than an hour later, New York Governor Kathy Hochul and Attorney General Letitia James executed their countermove. In the Supreme Court of the State of New York, they filed a sweeping civil lawsuit against Kalshi, accusing the platform of operating an illegal, unlicensed sports gambling business. The state’s complaint seeks a calculated total of at least $36 billion in statutory civil penalties, disgorgement of profits, and consumer restitution. 

This double-barreled legal filing represents a major constitutional conflict over financial derivatives and state police powers. At its core, the dispute asks a fundamental question: Can a federally licensed financial exchange be declared an illegal gambling operation by an individual state?

The roots of the conflict trace back to October 2025, when the New York State Gaming Commission, led by Executive Director Robert Williams, issued a cease-and-desist order to Kalshi. The commission alleged that the platform’s newly introduced "event contracts" on sporting events were nothing more than unlicensed mobile sports wagering. Under New York law, sports betting is heavily regulated, subject to a 51 percent state tax on gross gaming revenue, and restricted to individuals aged 21 and older. Kalshi, which is based in New York City, did not possess a state gaming license, paid no state sports-betting taxes, and permitted users aged 18 to 20 to trade on its platform.

Kalshi fought back in federal court, filing a lawsuit titled [*KalshiEX LLC v. Williams*](file:///Users/vzl/.gemini/antigravity-cli/scratch/kalshi_ny_case) in the Southern District of New York. The platform’s co-founder and CEO, Tarek Mansour, argued that the federal CEA preempts state gambling regulations. According to Kalshi, because it is registered with the CFTC as a Designated Contract Market (DCM), its event contracts are federally regulated financial instruments, not wagers. "So what is the expectation? That fifty-fifty, people win/lose? If that were the case, then let's just flip a coin... This is a game of skill. It's a highly competitive market. People are competing," Mansour asserted in a July 14, 2026 interview at Axios House DC, defending the platform's order-book mechanics.

However, Kalshi’s legal shield fractured in July 2026. U.S. District Judge Analisa Torres systematically dismantled the platform's preemption argument. In a July 7 ruling denying Kalshi's motion for a preliminary injunction, Judge Torres concluded that the CEA's grant of exclusive jurisdiction to the CFTC does not supersede state authority to regulate gambling, which remains a traditional police power reserved to the states under the Tenth Amendment. She followed this on July 27 by denying an emergency injunction pending appeal. Two days later, the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Second Circuit rejected Kalshi's request for temporary administrative relief, clearing the path for Attorney General James to launch the state's civil action.

The state’s $36 billion penalty calculation is built on the volume of Kalshi's operations. The state is demanding a civil penalty of $100,000 for each individual instance of an unlicensed sports bet offered or facilitated in New York, alongside restitution for users, forfeiture of all New York-derived profits, and statutory treble damages. Attorney General James was unequivocal in her assessment: "New York's gambling laws protect children from underage betting and help combat gambling addiction. No matter what they call themselves, prediction markets like Kalshi are gambling platforms, plain and simple. By ignoring our laws, Kalshi is running an illegal operation and harming New Yorkers in the process."

The CFTC's aggressive defense of Kalshi has sparked intense scrutiny, particularly regarding Chairman Michael S. Selig. Confirmed by the Senate in late 2025 under the administration of President Donald Trump, Selig previously worked as a corporate lawyer representing the digital asset and prediction market industries. Critics have pointed to this professional background, alleging a conflict of interest and suggesting the CFTC is acting to protect the sector it regulates. Public interest groups and lawmakers have voiced concerns about the systemic risks of prediction markets, citing the potential for insider trading and the ethics of allowing bets on sensitive political, military, or sporting events. The NFL and other major sports leagues have also pressured the CFTC, demanding strict limits on sports-event contracts to protect the integrity of their games.

Selig, however, frames the issue as one of federal supremacy and regulatory clarity. Following New York's state court filing, Selig posted on X: "Rather than seek reasoned answers from the courts, Letitia James and New York seek to force an unprecedented sudden shutdown of prediction markets nationwide. The @CFTC has already sued to stop this and will continue to defend its jurisdiction."

The federal courts, however, have not embraced the CFTC’s arguments. In early August 2026, U.S. District Judge Victor Marrero denied the CFTC and the Department of Justice’s joint emergency motion for a temporary restraining order against New York. The ruling means the state's civil action can proceed in state court, leaving Kalshi exposed to potential financial liability while the broader preemption battle winds its way through the appellate system. Tarek Mansour has remained defiant, stating, "This is the same playbook you see any time a legacy industry gets disrupted. First they litigate. Then they try to legislate. Eventually they will come around to the fact that consumer demand isn't going anywhere." Kalshi has since moved to remove the state-court case to federal court, seeking to consolidate the litigation.

The outcome of this jurisdictional conflict will shape the landscape of the broader prediction market industry, which includes platforms like ForecastEx and Robinhood's newly launched derivatives arms. If New York succeeds in shutting down Kalshi and extracting billions in penalties, the federal regulatory framework under the CFTC will offer little protection to fintech innovators looking to build event-driven markets. For now, the prediction market industry remains caught in a division between federal approval and state-level prosecution.

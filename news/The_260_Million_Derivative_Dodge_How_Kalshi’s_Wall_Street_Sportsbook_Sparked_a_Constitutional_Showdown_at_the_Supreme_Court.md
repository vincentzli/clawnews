# The $260 Million Derivative Dodge: How Kalshi’s Wall Street Sportsbook Sparked a Constitutional Showdown at the Supreme Court

In late 2024, when retail prediction exchange KalshiEX LLC defeated the Commodity Futures Trading Commission in the D.C. Circuit to list binary event contracts on congressional elections, the ruling was viewed across the financial technology sector as a decisive legal validation of event forecasting. Less than two years later, that initial market focus on political and economic forecasting has been rapidly eclipsed by athletic event wagering, precipitating one of the sharpest federal-state jurisdictional clashes in modern administrative law.

Behind Kalshi’s electronic order books and institutional derivative terminology sits an unprecedented retail betting volume. Throughout 2025, the platform processed $22.9 billion in cumulative trading volume, generating $263.5 million in exchange fee revenue. Crucially, platform transaction data and evidentiary disclosures submitted in federal court show that more than 89% of those fees stemmed from athletic event contracts. By listing binary propositions on professional and collegiate sports outcomes—from game winners to in-play athlete performances—Kalshi established an active nationwide betting exchange without obtaining state sports wagering licenses, paying state gross gaming revenue taxes, or connecting to state-mandated responsible gaming registries.

That regulatory divergence has now triggered a multi-front enforcement offensive from state attorneys general and sovereign tribal nations, met by aggressive federal litigation from the CFTC and emergency petitions for certiorari currently arriving before the Supreme Court of the United States.

The legal counter-attack began in state courts before spreading across federal jurisdictions. In January 2026, Suffolk County Superior Court in Boston granted Massachusetts Attorney General Andrea Joy Campbell a preliminary injunction, holding that Kalshi’s athletic contracts constitute unlicensed sports wagering under Massachusetts General Laws Chapter 23N and rejecting the company’s assertion that federal commodities law displaced state police powers. 

Two months later, the legal foundation of Kalshi’s model encountered a critical obstacle in the Southern District of Ohio. In *KalshiEX LLC v. Ohio Casino Control Commission*, Chief U.S. District Judge Sarah D. Morrison denied Kalshi’s motion for a preliminary injunction, explicitly invoking the statutory absurdity doctrine. Kalshi asserted that its sports event contracts satisfied the definition of "swaps" under 7 U.S.C. § 1a(47), thereby triggering exclusive federal jurisdiction under Section 2(a)(1)(A) of the Commodity Exchange Act (CEA) and preempting state gaming prohibitions. Judge Morrison rejected that construction. She observed that while currency valuations, interest rate shifts, and severe weather patterns exert direct systemic pressure on physical commodities markets, athletic competitions have no organic transmission mechanism into traditional commodities. Accepting Kalshi's interpretation, Morrison concluded, would yield the absurd result of converting all conventional sports betting across the nation into federally preempted swaps, effectively dismantling state gaming regulatory structures through an administrative loophole Congress never enacted.

State enforcement swiftly escalated. In Washington State, following an action filed by Attorney General Nick Brown, King County Superior Court Judge John McHale issued an injunction requiring Kalshi to maintain geofencing against Washington residents by September 2, 2026, backed by prospective fines of $120,000 per day across sports, politics, and culture categories. In Michigan, Attorney General Dana Nessel obtained a preliminary injunction in Ingham County Circuit Court enforcing daily penalties of up to $500,000 under Michigan's Lawful Sports Betting Act, characterizing the exchange's athletic products as unlicensed commercial gambling operating under the pretext of financial hedging.

The financial stakes expanded exponentially on July 31, 2026, when New York Attorney General Letitia James, joined by Governor Kathy Hochul, sued Kalshi in New York Supreme Court. Citing widespread statutory violations, New York’s complaint seeks full restitution, disgorgement of revenue, and civil penalties that could reach a theoretical statutory ceiling of $36 billion under state anti-wagering statutes.

Concurrently, sovereign tribal governments launched independent litigation under the Indian Gaming Regulatory Act (IGRA), 25 U.S.C. § 2701 et seq. In parallel actions, the Ho-Chunk Nation in Wisconsin, alongside the Blue Lake Rancheria and the Chicken Ranch Rancheria of Me-Wuk Indians in California, filed suit alleging that Kalshi facilitates unauthorized Class III gaming within tribal territorial boundaries, undermining sovereign tribal-state compacts. In September 2026, the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit affirmed that tribal plaintiffs demonstrated a substantial likelihood of establishing that Kalshi’s sports offerings infringe upon IGRA protections on tribal lands.

Rather than accommodating state boundaries, the federal commodities regulator intervened directly against state enforcement. Operating under the administration of President Donald Trump, the CFTC initiated affirmative federal lawsuits against both Wisconsin and New York. Relying on Section 2(a)(1)(A) of the CEA, the commission claimed plenary exclusive jurisdiction over all contracts executed on designated contract markets, arguing that both field and conflict preemption prohibit states from deploying local criminal and civil gaming statutes against federally registered financial exchanges.

The dispute has also generated significant political controversy. Donald Trump Jr. joined Kalshi as a strategic adviser in January 2025, receiving platform equity valued in excess of $300,000 after publicly praising the exchange's forecasting speed on election night in 2024. That financial tie has drawn formal scrutiny in Washington, where Republican Senator John Curtis called for inquiries by the Senate Judiciary Committee into potential executive branch conflicts of interest regarding federal agency intervention against state regulators.

These conflicting authorities have fractured the federal judiciary. On April 6, 2026, the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Third Circuit ruled 2-1 in *KalshiEX LLC v. Flaherty* that sports event contracts qualify as statutory swaps and that the CEA’s exclusive jurisdiction preempts New Jersey from enforcing state gaming laws against the exchange. However, on August 28, 2026, the Ninth Circuit ruled unanimously in *KalshiEX, LLC v. Assad* that athletic contracts fail to satisfy the statutory criteria for swaps and that federal law does not preempt state gambling oversight. In September 2026, a unanimous three-judge panel of the Sixth Circuit affirmed Judge Morrison's Ohio ruling, solidifying an irreconcilable circuit split.

The resulting legal uncertainty has reverberated across retail trading communities. On Reddit’s r/wallstreetbets, where derivative traders and sports bettors regularly post transaction logs, users have engaged in sustained debate over the platform's regulatory posture. "Kalshi’s 'swaps' defense is just legal gymnastics so they can dodge state gaming taxes and licensing while running a 24/7 retail sportsbook," argued one top-ranked commenter. On X, financial exchange advocates presented the counter-argument, warning of broader market disruption: "If courts break CEA preemption here, they're unraveling the foundational premise of federal commodities exchanges to protect legacy casino monopolies."

With New Jersey filing a formal petition for a writ of certiorari on September 2, 2026 (*Flaherty v. KalshiEX LLC*), and cross-petitions following from the Sixth and Ninth Circuit decisions, the Supreme Court now faces an emergency determination on whether binary outcome betting constitutes federally protected financial derivatives or unlawful commercial gambling.

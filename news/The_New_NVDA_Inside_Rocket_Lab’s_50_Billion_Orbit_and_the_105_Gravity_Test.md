# The New NVDA? Inside Rocket Lab’s $50 Billion Orbit and the $105 Gravity Test

On the floor of the New York Stock Exchange this week, the ticker RKLB has become more than a sequence of letters; it is a Rorschach test for the 2026 bull market. Following the May 7 release of its Q1 2026 earnings, Rocket Lab USA, Inc. saw its shares breach the $105 mark, a staggering ascent from the doldrums of 2024. For the retail faithful on r/wallstreetbets, the verdict is unanimous: "RKLB is the new NVDA," as one user posted in a thread that garnered 14,000 upvotes. "Beck is the Jensen Huang of the vacuum. If you aren't long, you aren't paying attention."

But as Sir Peter Beck’s company crosses a $51.2 billion market capitalization, the fundamental tension between speculative euphoria and aerospace reality has reached a breaking point. The numbers reported on Thursday were undeniably historic. Rocket Lab posted quarterly revenue of $200.35 million, the first time the company has cleared the $200 million hurdle, representing a 64% year-over-year surge. More critically, the total backlog has swelled to $2.2 billion, a 108% increase from early 2025. 

The catalyst for this latest leg up wasn't just the revenue beat, but the tactical completion of Beck’s "end-to-end" vision. On the same day as the earnings call, Rocket Lab announced the definitive acquisition of Motiv Space Systems. This follows the April absorption of laser-comms leader Mynaric. By folding Motiv’s Mars-heritage robotics—the same technology powering the Perseverance rover’s arm—into its Space Systems division, Rocket Lab has effectively insourced the high-margin "brains and hands" of the satellite industry. Space Systems now accounts for 68% of total revenue, up from 55% a year ago, shifting the narrative from a low-margin launch provider to a high-margin infrastructure play.

"The market is finally pricing Rocket Lab as a hardware-software platform rather than a trucking company for orbits," says one senior analyst at a major Manhattan hedge fund. "With the Motiv deal, they’ve cornered the market on Solar Array Drive Assemblies (SADAs) and precision gimbals. They aren't just building the bus; they are building the entire fleet."

However, the "New NVDA" comparison faces a harsh mathematical reality. At $105 per share, Rocket Lab is trading at roughly 62 times its projected 2026 revenue of $825 million. For comparison, Nvidia, even at the height of the 2024 AI mania, rarely sustained a price-to-sales multiple above 40x. The skepticism is palpable in the options pits, where put volume for the June $80 strike has spiked 400% in the last 48 hours. 

"RKLB is a classic liquidity trap in the making," warns a contrarian post on the StockTwits 'Bearish' board. "Insiders were dumping blocks in the $65 to $75 range just four months ago. Why is the retail crowd buying at $100+ when Peter Beck himself was trimming at $70? This is the Motiv acquisition being used as a smoke screen for a massive valuation overshoot."

The political backdrop adds another layer of complexity. Under the second Trump administration, the space sector has been treated as a front line for national security. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has been vocal about "space-based industrial dominance" as a pillar of the 2025-2029 economic plan. Rocket Lab’s $816 million contract for the Space Development Agency’s (SDA) Tranche 3 satellites is the crown jewel of this shift, providing a non-dilutive capital stream that few competitors can match. Yet, the entire $50 billion valuation hinges on a single event scheduled for Q4 2026: the debut launch of the Neutron rocket.

Neutron is Rocket Lab’s answer to the Falcon 9, a reusable medium-lift vehicle designed to break SpaceX’s monopoly. While Beck confirmed on the May 7 call that Archimedes engine testing is "ahead of schedule" at the Stennis Space Center, the history of aerospace is littered with "binary events" that turned into disasters. SpaceX’s Starship development has moved at a blistering pace under the 2025 deregulation push, and the cost-per-kilogram of launch is falling faster than Rocket Lab’s revenue is growing.

The Federal Reserve’s transition also looms. With Jerome Powell’s term as Chair ending in five days on May 15, and Kevin Warsh nominated to take the helm, the era of "higher for longer" may be ending, but the cost of capital for capital-intensive aerospace firms remains elevated. If Neutron slips into 2027, the burn rate on Rocket Lab's Pasadena and Long Beach facilities could quickly erode the $540 million in cash and equivalents currently on the balance sheet.

In the hallways of the Treasury, the talk is of a "New Space Race" that fuels American GDP. On Reddit, the talk is of "generational wealth." But for the institutional investor, the question is simpler: Is the Motiv acquisition the final piece of a dominant aerospace prime, or is it the last great narrative used to propel a $4 stock to $100 before the gravity of the Neutron launch takes hold? At $105, there is no margin for error, only the thin air of the upper atmosphere.

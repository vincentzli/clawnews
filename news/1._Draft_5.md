### 1.1 Headline
**Hacking the Lipidome: Inside Editas’s First-in-Human Cas12a Multiplex Trial and the High-Stakes Gamble of Somatic Liver Reprogramming**

### 1.2 Body
For three decades, preventive cardiology has been an exercise in chemical compliance. If you were born with the genetic misfortune of familial hypercholesterolemia, your life has been dictated by a daily cocktail of statins, cholesterol absorption inhibitors, and, more recently, bi-weekly subcutaneous injections of monoclonal antibodies. But human adherence is notoriously fragile: registry data shows more than 50% of patients abandon statins within twelve months, and circulating low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) remains the primary driver of atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD), the world’s leading cause of death.

Now, Editas Medicine is attempting to cut the Gordian knot of chronic therapy. With the initiation of first-in-human clinical trials for its EDIT-401 program, the Cambridge-based pioneer is moving *in vivo* multiplex CRISPR from academic proof-of-concept into the human clinic. Rather than modifying ex vivo stem cells or knocking out a single target, EDIT-401 executes a simultaneous, dual-target genomic excision in hepatocytes, knocking out both *PCSK9* and *ANGPTL3*. 

In non-human primate (NHP) studies, this combinatorial genomic hit achieved what small molecules and monotherapies never could: sustained, durable reductions exceeding 80% in both LDL-C and serum triglycerides. 

Yet, as EDIT-401 enters the clinic, it lands in the center of an ideological and technical firestorm. The molecular promise of a permanent, one-and-done cure is colliding head-on with the irreversible nature of somatic double-strand breaks, the unresolved immunology of lipid nanoparticles (LNPs), and a skeptical payer landscape that wonders why medicine should rewrite a patient's DNA when a generic five-cent atorvastatin pill already exists.

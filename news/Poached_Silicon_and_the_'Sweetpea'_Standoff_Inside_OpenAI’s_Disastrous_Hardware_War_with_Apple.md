# Poached Silicon and the 'Sweetpea' Standoff: Inside OpenAI’s Disastrous Hardware War with Apple

On July 10, 2026, Apple filed a trade-secrets lawsuit in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California, alleging a coordinated pattern of intellectual property theft targeting its hardware engineering teams. The complaint accuses OpenAI of systematically poaching top Apple engineers to build out its consumer hardware division. At the center of the litigation is Tang Tan, Apple’s former vice president of product design for the iPhone and Apple Watch, who currently serves as OpenAI’s Chief Hardware Officer.

According to court filings reassigned to U.S. District Judge Edward Davila, OpenAI’s hardware division was populated with personnel who allegedly took proprietary designs with them. Apple claims that Tan emailed himself confidential supplier agreements and technical documents months before his resignation. The complaint further alleges that Tan used internal Apple project code names during interview loops to probe candidates who were still employed at Apple. In what the filings describe as "show and tell" sessions, candidates were reportedly asked to bring physical Apple parts, such as custom batteries, logic boards, and System-in-Package configurations, to OpenAI's offices. Tan also allegedly coached recruits on how to pass exit-security checks at Apple.

The claims against co-defendant Chang Liu, a former Apple electrical engineer who joined OpenAI in early 2026, details security breaches. Apple alleges that Liu failed to return a corporate MacBook that remained authenticated to Apple's network. Exploiting a security bug, Liu reportedly accessed internal servers to download proprietary hardware specifications. The complaint quotes a message Liu sent to a former colleague: "LOL, I found out I can access the [network storage], so funny." Additionally, Apple alleges that OpenAI pressured a mutual manufacturing supplier to use a proprietary Apple metal-finishing technique by claiming Apple had authorized the transfer.

Notably, Apple did not name Jony Ive as a defendant. Ive, who left Apple in 2019 to found LoveFrom, is directing the design of OpenAI’s consumer hardware. OpenAI acquired io Products, Inc.—a hardware startup co-founded by Ive and Sam Altman—for a reported $6.5 billion. Legal experts note that excluding Ive avoids a public confrontation with an iconic figure who maintains close ties to prominent Silicon Valley investors, including Laurene Powell Jobs.

The legal pressure coincides with developmental setbacks for OpenAI. Its signature voice-first, screenless consumer AI device has been delayed until at least February 2027. The project, internally code-named "Sweetpea," has encountered severe thermal and power management issues. Engineering sources indicate that local model processing has caused prototype devices to overheat rapidly during ambient testing, while power consumption has consistently outpaced target battery capacity.

The timeline has also been derailed by a trademark battle. In June 2025, audio startup iyO, Inc. sued OpenAI, Altman, and Ive, alleging that the "io" brand infringed upon its "IYO ONE" trademark. Following a temporary restraining order, U.S. District Judge Trina L. Thompson granted a preliminary injunction on April 23, 2026, preventing OpenAI from utilizing the "io" name. iyO subsequently expanded its lawsuit to claim trade-secret theft, alleging that Tan and an internal informant misappropriated physical files. Backed by litigation funding from Omni Bridgeway, iyO’s legal challenge has forced OpenAI to pursue a complete rebranding strategy.

As the consumer device remains delayed, OpenAI released its first physical hardware product, the Codex Micro, on July 15, 2026. Built in collaboration with boutique manufacturer Work Louder, the $230 macro pad features 13 mechanical keys, a rotary encoder dial for adjusting AI reasoning parameters, a mini joystick, and six translucent "Agent Keys" with RGB backlighting to monitor active coding agents. While the limited run sold out within hours on OpenAI's online Supply Co. store, the device remains a niche tool for developers rather than a mass-market consumer entry.

The strategic window for OpenAI is narrowing. While antitrust priorities have evolved under the administration of President Donald Trump, the race to control physical AI interfaces has intensified. Former Vice President Kamala Harris recently commented at a technology summit that direct-to-consumer physical distribution will define the next phase of AI commercialization. 

Google has moved aggressively, deploying Gemini Nano directly on Pixel hardware and advancing Project Astra’s real-time multimodal capabilities. Concurrently, Apple's partnership model has shifted. Although Apple announced plans at WWDC 2024 to integrate ChatGPT, it has since integrated Google’s Gemini into Apple Intelligence. With Apple now pursuing OpenAI in federal court over hardware trade secrets, the path for OpenAI to bypass traditional mobile operating systems with its own ambient hardware remains blocked by litigation and technical constraints.

# **Treasury vs. Token-holder: The 50/50 Revenue Split and the 2026 Crisis at Pump.fun**

In the volatile heart of the Solana meme-coin ecosystem, a "civil war" is currently being waged between the platform that birthed it and the traders who sustain it. As of May 17, 2026, Pump.fun, the dominant launchpad for algorithmic meme-coin creation, finds itself at a precarious crossroads. A high-stakes shift in tokenomics, a devastating social media security breach, and a looming expansion into rival networks have combined to create an atmosphere of unprecedented analytical tension.

The conflict reached a boiling point on April 28, 2026, when Pump.fun co-founder Alon Cohen announced a fundamental restructuring of the platform’s revenue model. For nearly a year following the July 2025 Initial Coin Offering (ICO) of the $PUMP token—which raised $1.3 billion at a $4 billion fully diluted valuation—the protocol had operated on a 100% buyback-and-burn mechanism. Every cent of net revenue was funneled into buying $PUMP on the open market and permanently destroying it.

However, Cohen’s pivot to a 50/50 split—where only half of the revenue is burned, while the other 50% is retained for operations and "strategic acquisitions"—triggered an immediate revolt among token holders. Defending the move, Cohen issued a statement that has since become a focal point of community derision: "If we forgo retaining a portion of revenues for operations and growth, we run the risk of our treasury being throttled by burn rather than being used for high-impact strategic investments, such as impactful acquisitions and new product ventures."

For $PUMP holders, "throttled by burn" has become a rallying cry for those who feel the protocol has abandoned its deflationary promises. Since the ICO, the $PUMP token has struggled to maintain its launch valuation, and many retail investors viewed the 100% burn as the primary mechanism for price support. The tension is rooted in a fundamental misalignment: a platform built on the ethos of "fair launches" is now facing accusations of centralizing its massive revenue stream to fund a corporate war chest.

This internal strife was exponentially magnified by the fallout from the May 13, 2026 social media crisis. Just days after the verified X account of Keith Gill (Roaring Kitty) was compromised on May 11 to promote the Red Kitten Crew ($RKC) token, on-chain investigators released definitive reports on May 13 detailing the breach's mechanics. The hackers utilized "bundled" launches, purchasing 40% of the supply in the same block as the token’s creation, allowing them to siphon over $500,000 in profit within minutes.

On-chain investigators, including the researcher known as Specter, revealed that the attackers successfully bypassed Pump.fun’s bonding curve protections using sophisticated automation. This incident has reignited a fierce debate over the platform's technical integrity. As one Reddit user on r/solana pointed out, "If a celebrity hack can rug $10 million in an hour using the platform's own tools, the bonding curve isn't a safety net—it's a liquidity trap." The fallout on May 13 saw a 14% drop in daily active addresses, as users questioned the "fairness" of the launch mechanism.

Amidst this crisis of trust, Pump.fun is attempting to pivot the narrative through a massive multi-chain expansion. On-chain observers have identified the registration of several new subdomains, including eth.pump.fun, monad.pump.fun, and base.pump.fun. The planned move to Monad, a high-performance parallelized EVM chain, is seen by analysts as a strategic attempt to capture new liquidity before the current Solana-based user base erodes further.

To mitigate the flight of capital, rumors of a retroactive airdrop for high-volume traders have been intensified. Speculation suggest that users with over $10 million in lifetime volume may receive a significant allocation of $PUMP tokens, provided they can pass rigorous anti-bot filtering. The team is reportedly using human-only metrics—wallet age, social linkage, and livestreaming activity—to filter out the estimated 90% of top traders who are identified as AI bots.

The political environment adds further complexity. Under the current administration of President Donald Trump, the regulatory outlook for crypto has shifted toward deregulation, yet Pump.fun remains the target of a federal class-action lawsuit in New York. The plaintiffs allege the platform's bonding curve model facilitates the sale of unregistered securities, a charge that has gained significant traction following the May 13 social media storm. As former Vice President Kamala Harris and other figures from the previous administration transition into the private sector, the industry is watching to see if the "Wild West" era of meme-coins can survive the maturation of its own infrastructure.

# The Synthetic Surrogacy of the Brazilian Right: Inside the Legal, Diplomatic, and Electoral Fallout of the 2026 Presidential Race

On July 25, 2026, the Liberal Party (PL) national convention in São Paulo culminated not in the physical appearance of its ideological leader, but in a projection testing the limits of Brazilian constitutional law. As Senator Flávio Bolsonaro was formally nominated as the PL's presidential candidate for the October 4, 2026, general election, a towering LED screen displayed an artificial intelligence-generated avatar of his father, former President Jair Bolsonaro. The elder Bolsonaro is currently serving a 27-year and 3-month prison sentence following his September 2025 conviction by the Supreme Federal Court (STF) for plotting a military coup. Under the terms of his house arrest—granted in March 2026 due to failing health—Bolsonaro is barred from public communication, social media use, and political campaigning. 

"As everyone here knows, I am imprisoned and silenced by an unjust and arbitrary decision based on something I never did," the AI avatar’s synthesized voice announced to the thousands gathered. "But I guarantee, no prison will silence the feeling of hope we have awakened." The avatar then urged the crowd to back Flávio's run against the incumbent leftist President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva, who is seeking a fourth term.

The presentation immediately triggered a complex legal battle. The Federação Brasil da Esperança, a coalition led by Lula's Workers' Party (PT), filed urgent complaints with the Superior Electoral Court (TSE) and the STF, claiming the PL violated synthetic media rules and bypassed judicial communication bans. Under TSE Resolution nº 23.755—which updated electoral rules for the 2026 cycle—the use of unauthorized deepfakes or synthetic media to deceive voters can result in the annulment of a candidate's registration. Supreme Court Justice Alexandre de Moraes ordered Bolsonaro's legal team to explain the video within 48 hours. On July 29, 2026, the defense team submitted their response, denying that the former president had authorized the video. The lawyers argued that Bolsonaro's house arrest conditions, which ban communication with his son, made such authorization legally impossible, though they claimed his family has a "natural relationship of trust" to use his likeness. Senator Flávio Bolsonaro downplayed the challenge, arguing that the pre-campaign rules do not clearly restrict AI use and pointing to a visual disclaimer on the screen labeling the clip as a simulation.

The convention also provoked an unprecedented diplomatic crisis. Argentine President Javier Milei attended the event in person, using the stage to launch a blistering attack on the host nation's government. Milei labeled President Lula a "thief," a "convict," and a "totalitarian," while warning of the "Lula risk" spreading across South America. Milei also directed personal insults at Justice Moraes, calling him a "bald piece of trash" for rejecting his request to visit the imprisoned elder Bolsonaro. In response to the diplomatic breach, the Brazilian Ministry of Foreign Affairs recalled its ambassador to Argentina, Julio Bitelli, for consultations. Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu added to the geopolitical polarization, sending a video message of support for the Bolsonaro campaign.

Beyond the international endorsements, Flávio Bolsonaro’s campaign is struggling with severe structural fractures. The candidate has failed to secure formal alliances with the centrist parties of the *Centrão*, who are crucial for securing TV advertising time and local campaign machinery but remain wary of the Bolsonaro family's legal liabilities. Internal family dynamics have also spilled into public view. Former First Lady Michelle Bolsonaro, whose influence among evangelical voters is critical, did not attend the São Paulo convention. Her absence followed a public dispute in which she accused Flávio of disrespecting her. Although she posted a video on July 24 accepting his apology and calling for party unity, her physical absence from the stage underscored the campaign's inner turmoil. 

Seeking to salvage the campaign’s trajectory, national PL coordinator Senator Rogério Marinho announced a major leadership shakeup on July 30, 2026. The campaign dismissed publicists Alexandre Oltramari and Eduardo Fischer, replacing them with veteran political marketer Duda Lima. Lima, an ally of PL president Valdemar Costa Neto, previously directed Jair Bolsonaro’s 2022 campaign and São Paulo Mayor Ricardo Nunes’s successful 2024 reelection bid. 

Lima inherits a campaign facing flatlining poll numbers. A late-July AtlasIntel survey conducted from July 22–27, 2026, placed President Lula in the lead with 44.9% of first-round voting intentions, compared to Flávio Bolsonaro’s 35.8%. Similarly, a PoderData poll conducted from July 26–29, 2026, showed Lula leading 41% to 35%. In simulated second-round runoffs, AtlasIntel showed Lula leading Flávio 49.2% to 42.9%, while PoderData simulations placed the two in a technical tie within the statistical margin of error.

The conservative landscape was further fragmented on July 27, 2026, when the Novo Party officially nominated the former governor of Minas Gerais, Romeu Zema, as its presidential candidate at its national convention in Brasília. Zema, who resigned his governorship in April 2026 to run, presents a technocratic alternative to both *Lulismo* and the populist right. His platform centers on aggressive economic liberalism, including the privatization of state-controlled entities like Petrobras, and hardline public security measures, such as the construction of private mega-prisons and the designation of criminal factions as terrorist organizations. Zema has not yet chosen a running mate ahead of the August 5 legal deadline, but confirmed he is negotiating with former Supreme Court President Joaquim Barbosa, raising the possibility of a center-right coalition that could split the anti-Lula vote and deny Flávio Bolsonaro a spot in the second round.

Online spaces have reflected these deep political divisions. On X.com, supporters of the PL campaign have mobilized around hashtags defending the AI video as a legitimate tool to bypass what they term "judicial censorship." Critics, however, argue that allowing digital avatars of convicted and politically ineligible leaders to run campaigns undermines the rule of law. Center-right commentators are actively debating whether Zema’s clean-cut economic focus will offer an exit ramp for voters fatigued by the legal dramas of the Bolsonaro family, or if it will simply divide the opposition, guaranteeing a smooth path for Lula’s bid for a fourth term.

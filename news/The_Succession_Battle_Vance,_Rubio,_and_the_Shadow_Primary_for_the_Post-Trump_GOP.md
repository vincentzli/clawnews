# The Succession Battle: Vance, Rubio, and the Shadow Primary for the Post-Trump GOP

WASHINGTON — The 22nd Amendment of the U.S. Constitution has transformed the first term of the second Trump administration into an unprecedented political arena. Because President Donald Trump is constitutionally barred from seeking a third term, the typical deference shown to a sitting president has evaporated, replaced by an early, high-stakes battle for the 2028 Republican presidential nomination. At the center of this shadow primary is a fluid rivalry between Vice President JD Vance, the populist heir apparent, and Secretary of State Marco Rubio, the standard-bearer for the party’s traditionalist and internationalist wing. 

A mid-August 2026 poll conducted by Emerson College Polling among likely Republican primary voters revealed that the race has entered a dead heat. Vice President Vance leads Secretary Rubio by just a single percentage point, 36% to 35%. This represents a dramatic tightening of the field since February 2026, when Vance held a commanding 32-point lead over Rubio, 52% to 20%. The narrowing gap has intensified the policy debate within the Republican coalition, exposing deep debates over the party's ideological direction and electoral viability.

The succession battle is playing out against a challenging national environment for the GOP. The same Emerson College survey, conducted August 16–17, 2026, among 1,000 likely voters, showed President Trump’s approval rating holding at 40%, with 56% of voters disapproving of his performance. On the generic congressional ballot for the upcoming November 3, 2026 midterm elections, Democrats lead Republicans by eight percentage points, 51% to 43%. This defensive midterm posture has turned the upcoming elections into a proxy battlefield, where both Vance and Rubio are maneuvering to install loyalists and demonstrate their respective political clout.

For Vance, the path to the nomination relies on consolidating the populist, working-class "America First" movement that swept him into the vice presidency. Vance has spent the summer of 2026 headlining fundraisers and campaigning for populist congressional candidates. His base of support is heavily concentrated among younger voters; the polling shows Vance leading Rubio 37% to 26% among Republican primary voters under the age of 50. In online conservative circles, MAGA loyalists argue that Vance is the only candidate who can carry forward the administration's economic nationalism and skepticism toward foreign interventions. On platforms like X, supporters emphasize Vance's focus on domestic manufacturing, border security, and trade protectionism as the core tenets of the party's future.

Rubio’s supporters, meanwhile, point to a different set of numbers from the August 20, 2026 Emerson poll to argue that the Secretary of State is far more electable in a general election. The survey tested both men against leading Democratic contenders, showing that Rubio consistently outperforms the Vice President. In a hypothetical matchup against former Vice President Kamala Harris, Rubio leads by five points, 48% to 43%, whereas Harris leads Vance by four points, 49% to 45%. Against California Governor Gavin Newsom, Vance trails by five points (49% to 44%), while Rubio is within two points (48% to 46%). Similarly, Georgia Senator Jon Ossoff leads Vance by five points (49% to 44%) but leads Rubio by just three (47% to 44%). Only Secretary of Transportation Pete Buttigieg maintains an identical five-point lead over both Republicans, leading each 49% to 44%. 

These numbers have energized establishment and moderate Republicans, who argue that Rubio's appeal to voters over 50—where he leads Vance 41% to 35%—and his strength with suburban moderates are essential for rebuilding a winning general election coalition. In public forums and moderate subreddits like r/politics, analysts debate whether a Vance nomination would alienate swing voters. Some online commentators have floated the "sacrificial lamb" theory, suggesting that Vance's populist wing might inherit a difficult 2028 landscape if the midterms go poorly, leaving the door open for Rubio to consolidate the party's traditionalist donor base.

The midterm primaries have already illustrated this internal friction. In Texas, the Trump-aligned populist wing scored a major victory on May 26, 2026, when Attorney General Ken Paxton defeated incumbent U.S. Senator John Cornyn in a primary runoff by a decisive 63.8% to 36.2% margin. Paxton’s victory was secured after a late endorsement from President Trump, highlighting the power of the "America First" endorsement. In Louisiana, the divide was even more pronounced. Senator Bill Cassidy, who faced intense backlash within the state party after voting to convict Trump in his 2021 impeachment trial, was knocked out of the primary on May 16, 2026, finishing third with 25% of the vote. Cassidy conceded the race, noting, "When you participate in democracy, sometimes it doesn't turn out the way you want it to. But you don't pout, you don't whine." U.S. Representative Julia Letlow, who was endorsed by Trump, eventually won the Republican nomination for the Senate seat in the June 27 runoff and is set to face Democrat Jamie Davis in November.

While Vance and Rubio represent distinct factions, they must both navigate the presence of President Trump, who remains the ultimate arbiter of the party's future. Trump has publicly sought to defuse tensions by teasing that Vance and Rubio would make a "great team" or a "dream ticket" for 2028. Behind the scenes, however, sources close to the administration report that Trump has privately expressed support for Vance, viewing him as his ideological successor. By keeping his public preferences ambiguous, Trump ensures that both men remain intensely loyal to his current policy agenda while preventing either faction from openly fracturing the cabinet.

Adding to the complexity is the presence of potential dark-horse candidates, most notably Florida Governor Ron DeSantis. DeSantis is currently entering the final months of his second term, which concludes on January 5, 2027. While he registered just 6% in the February primary poll, DeSantis remains a powerful figure in Tallahassee. Political strategists note that DeSantis, free from the constraints of serving in the federal cabinet, has the latitude to position himself as an executive-tested populist alternative who can bridge the gap between Vance's ideological purity and Rubio's traditionalist appeal.

As the November midterms approach, the shadow primary between the Vice President and the Secretary of State will only intensify. The outcome of the generic ballot struggle and the performance of Trump-endorsed candidates will serve as the first concrete test of whether the Republican electorate favors Vance’s populist succession or Rubio’s electability argument. For a party looking past the Trump era, the battle for 2028 is already well underway.

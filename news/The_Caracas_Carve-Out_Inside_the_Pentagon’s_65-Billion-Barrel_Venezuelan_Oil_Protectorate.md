# The Caracas Carve-Out: Inside the Pentagon’s 65-Billion-Barrel Venezuelan Oil Protectorate

In the pre-dawn darkness of January 3, 2026, when U.S. Delta Force operators breached the Fuerte Tiuna military complex during Operation Absolute Resolve, the immediate objective was the extraction of Nicolás Maduro and his wife, Cilia Flores, to face federal narco-terrorism indictments in New York. The assault, which left approximately 80 dead and followed months of lethal maritime strikes under Operation Southern Spear that claimed 221 lives and destroyed 67 vessels, was categorized by the White House as a surgical law enforcement action. Yet eight months later, the military spearhead has resolved into the most aggressive restructuring of sovereign natural resources in modern Latin American history.

On August 28, 2026, President Donald Trump announced what he termed "the biggest oil deal in world history": an agreement with Venezuelan interim President Delcy Rodríguez granting an obscure entity, North American Blue Energy Partners (NABEP), operational control over 17 premier oil fields containing an estimated 65 billion barrels of proven reserves. The 100-year concessions encompass vast production blocks in the Orinoco Heavy Oil Belt previously managed by Russian state-controlled Rosneft and China National Petroleum Corporation (CNPC).

Beneath the official framing of economic stabilization lies a corporate and military architecture designed in Washington. Corporate filings establish that NABEP is a Barbados-domiciled entity owned by Venezuelan tycoon Alejandro Betancourt López. Rather than operating as an arm's-length commercial concessionaire, NABEP has been integrated directly into the strategic apparatus of the United States. Under the negotiated governance structure, the Pentagon’s Office of Strategic Capital (OSC) secured a 35% equity stake in NABEP's corporate parent through "penny warrants"—nominal purchase options struck at roughly 1 cent per share that guarantee Washington significant equity without an upfront capital outlay while preventing dilution as operations expand. Simultaneously, the U.S. State Department acquired statutory rights to purchase 20% of all extracted crude at the direct cost of production, accompanied by a right of first refusal for the remaining 80%. Washington retains structural control of NABEP's board of directors and exercises supervisory oversight over all fund flows.

The elevation of Betancourt as the central corporate partner has ignited scrutiny across Washington and international energy corridors. Betancourt, who amassed substantial wealth during the administration of Hugo Chávez through state electrical contracts awarded to Derwick Associates, was previously the subject of long-running international investigations into money laundering and PDVSA fund embezzlement in the United States, Spain, and Switzerland. While Betancourt currently faces no active charges in U.S. courts, federal intelligence records indicate he served as an intermediary who supplied operational intelligence that aided U.S. planning prior to the January 3 capture of Maduro. 

On September 2, U.S. Energy Secretary Chris Wright arrived at Miraflores Palace in Caracas, leading an American corporate delegation to cement complementary industrial agreements. Wright and interim President Rodríguez presided over the signing of major commercial pacts intended to overhaul Venezuela’s dilapidated hydrocarbon infrastructure. Chevron finalized terms committing over $7 billion in capital expenditures through 2031 to develop the Carabobo 1 and Carabobo-2-South-A fields in the Orinoco Belt, aiming to more than double output to 600,000 barrels per day. Concurrently, GE Vernova entered into a strategic alliance with Petróleos de Venezuela, S.A. (PDVSA) to rehabilitate electrical grids servicing the petroleum sector, alongside a contract with the National Electric Corporation (Corpoelec) to add 1 gigawatt of capacity within two years and 5 gigawatts over the subsequent four years. Wright stated the administration was executing at "Trump-speed" to replace "conflict with commerce," noting that heavy crude imports would be swapped to support domestic refiners and replenish the U.S. Strategic Petroleum Reserve.

The pact has triggered severe constitutional friction inside Venezuela. Legal scholars and constitutional jurists emphasize that the century-long transfer of field management violates Article 303 of the 1999 Constitution, which mandates that the Republic retain total ownership of PDVSA. While Article 303 allows for strategic operational joint ventures, critics contend that delegating exclusive management of 17 major deposits to a foreign-controlled vehicle constitutes a de facto parallel national oil company. Opponents also point to Article 12, which codifies hydrocarbon deposits as inalienable and unassailable assets of the nation. The interim administration bypassed popular consultation, relying on a rapid resolution passed by the chavista-dominated National Assembly to authorize the contracts without broader legislative consensus.

Opposition leadership and Venezuelan civil society have mounted fierce resistance. Exiled opposition leader María Corina Machado and President-elect Edmundo González Urrutia—who remains in political limbo after Washington prioritized working relations with Rodríguez’s transitional government—issued a sharp repudiation. Machado denounced the arrangement as an "extralegal sovereign resource transfer," emphasizing that a transitional executive possesses no democratic mandate to surrender national patrimony. On digital platforms, the public response has centered on allegations of neocolonial extraction. On Reddit forums such as r/worldnews and r/energy, observers questioned the circumvention of competitive bidding and the partnership with an oligarch previously targeted by federal prosecutors. As one widely circulated critique on X summarized the sentiment, the transaction represents an unprecedented commercial protectorate, swapping sovereign constitutional safeguards for transactional survival under foreign supervision.

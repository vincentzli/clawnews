# The Bureaucratic Annexation: How Bezalel Smotrich Rewrote the Legal DNA of the West Bank

In a windowless reception hall in the West Bank settlement of Revava in June 2024, Israeli Finance Minister Bezalel Smotrich laid out what he termed his "life's mission" before a conference organized by the settlement movement. The audio recording, obtained by the anti-settlement watchdog Peace Now and published by The New York Times, captured a senior cabinet minister detailing a deliberate administrative revolution. "I’m telling you, it’s mega-dramatic," Smotrich told the audience. "Such changes change a system’s DNA." The strategy avoided formal parliamentary declarations of sovereignty that would trigger swift diplomatic sanctions. Instead, it systematically dismantled six decades of military governance across Area C of the West Bank, transferring municipal, planning, and enforcement powers to civilian administrators answering directly to him. The objective, Smotrich explained, was to make the integration of the territory into Israel "easier to swallow in the international and legal context," circumventing charges of formal annexation while cementing permanent control on the ground.

That bureaucratic machinery is now fully operational, fundamentally transforming the governance of the West Bank. Operating in a dual capacity—both as Finance Minister and as a minister within the Defense Ministry leading the civilian Settlement Administration—Smotrich has orchestrated the most consequential structural overhaul of the territory since the 1967 Six-Day War. For 57 years, international humanitarian law and Israeli Supreme Court rulings treated the West Bank as territory held under temporary belligerent occupation, administered exclusively by the military commander of the Israel Defense Forces (IDF) Central Command. Under military orders signed in May 2024 by outgoing Central Command chief Maj. Gen. Yehuda Fox, the IDF Civil Administration transferred 21 core governing powers to Hillel Roth, a civilian deputy appointed by Smotrich. Authority over land zoning, real estate registration, building permits, municipal ordinances, and water distribution was severed from the military chain of command and absorbed into an Israeli civilian bureaucracy.

The operational results of this administrative transfer have transformed the geography of Area C at an unprecedented pace. In 2024 alone, Israeli authorities declared 23,700 dunams (approximately 5,856 acres) across the West Bank as "state land"—the largest volume of land designated as such in a single calendar year since the signing of the Oslo Accords in 1993. The designations included an 8,000-dunam tract near Ma’ale Adumim in March 2024 and a 12,700-dunam parcel in the Jordan Valley in late June, surpassing the total area declared state land over the entire preceding decade (roughly 13,000 dunams between 2014 and 2023). Under Ottoman-era land statutes retained in the territory, the state land classification transfers uncultivated, unregistered rural land into government custody, where it is allocated almost exclusively for Jewish settlement expansion, precluding Palestinian development.

Simultaneously, the government has accelerated the formalization of illegal settlement outposts. The security cabinet approved the retroactive legalization and establishment of 34 outposts, backed by a dedicated NIS 1.3 billion ($350 million) civilian infrastructure budget. These outposts, historically defined as unauthorized under domestic Israeli law, are being integrated directly into Israel’s national electrical grid, water networks, and transportation corridors. Concurrently, Smotrich revived the long-dormant E-1 master plan—a 12-square-kilometer corridor between East Jerusalem and Ma’ale Adumim designed for 3,500 housing units. Authorities launched the initial marketing phase for 1,400 of those residential units. For three decades, successive U.S. and European administrations blocked E-1 on the grounds that it physically bisects the West Bank, severing Ramallah from Bethlehem and cutting East Jerusalem off from its Palestinian hinterland. Smotrich explicitly confirmed the strategic intent, stating that advancing E-1 serves to "bury the idea of a Palestinian state" by forging an unbroken urban corridor of Jewish settlement from West Jerusalem to the Jordan Valley.

This rapid restructuring has provoked intense resistance from within Israel’s security establishment. Commanders for Israel’s Security (CIS), an association representing more than 500 retired generals from the IDF, former directors of the Mossad, heads of the Shin Bet, and retired police commissioners, has mobilized against the civilianization of the West Bank. CIS economic assessments, prepared alongside former Finance Ministry director general David Brodet, examined the fiscal ramifications of systematically undermining the Palestinian Authority. According to CIS impact models, the total collapse of the Palestinian Authority would legally obligate Israel under international law to assume direct civil administration and policing for 3 million Palestinians, costing the national treasury between 12 and 15 billion shekels ($3.2 to $4.1 billion) annually in localized security, healthcare, and municipal upkeep alone. In a full-annexation scenario, CIS estimated total annual state expenditures at NIS 52 billion, consuming roughly 10 to 12 percent of the national budget at a time when the Israeli economy is already burdened by prolonged multi-front defense expenditures.

Public forums and social media have reflected this sharpening institutional rift. Across threads on Reddit’s r/worldnews and r/politics, users widely discussed the leaked recording in which Smotrich described changing the governing system’s DNA. One highly shared analysis noted: "This isn't about building houses; it's about swapping the legal foundation from military occupation to domestic civilian ministry control without ever holding a Knesset vote on annexation." On X.com, Israeli legal scholars and military analysts warned of the underlying fiscal and operational consequences, emphasizing: "Smotrich is dismantling the Civil Administration to annex the land, but the bill will arrive at the door of every Israeli taxpayer in the form of direct military governorship and economic collapse."

International diplomatic pushback has grown in intensity. In London, Foreign Secretary David Lammy of the UK Labour government summoned the Israeli ambassador, instituted sanctions on extremist settler entities, and suspended negotiations on a bilateral free trade agreement, characterizing settlement expansion as an existential threat to a two-state solution. In Brussels, European Union High Representative Kaja Kallas warned that the E-1 development would "drive a wedge" through the West Bank, signaling broad support among member states to restrict trade in settlement goods. In Washington, the administration of President Donald Trump confronts tension between conservative political allies supportive of settlement expansion and broader geopolitical interests aimed at preventing regional instability. Concurrently, Arab normalization partners—including the United Arab Emirates and Saudi Arabia—have cautioned that unilateral territorial absorption in Area C crosses a strategic threshold, undermining regional security frameworks.

Yet Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu remains constrained by the arithmetic of the Knesset. Dependent on the 14 parliamentary seats controlled by Smotrich’s Religious Zionism and National Security Minister Itamar Ben-Gvir’s Otzma Yehudit, Netanyahu cannot curtail the Settlement Administration’s initiatives without triggering the dissolution of his coalition. By shifting civilian authority within the defense establishment to political hardliners, the Israeli government has transformed the de facto annexation of the West Bank from an ideological aspiration into established administrative reality.

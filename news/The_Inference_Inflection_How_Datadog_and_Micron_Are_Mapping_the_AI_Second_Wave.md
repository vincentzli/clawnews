# **The Inference Inflection: How Datadog and Micron Are Mapping the AI "Second Wave"**

On the trading floors of Lower Manhattan and the server racks of northern Virginia, a fundamental shift in the artificial intelligence trade has reached its tipping point. As of May 8, 2026, the market is no longer pricing "potential"; it is pricing "telemetry." The blowout earnings reports this week from Datadog (DDOG) and Micron Technology (MU) have provided the most definitive evidence yet that the AI trade has successfully transitioned from the "Gold Rush" phase of model training into a more durable, if more complex, "Inference Inflection."

The numbers are startling. Datadog’s Q1 2026 report, released yesterday, saw revenue cross the billion-dollar threshold for the first time, hitting $1.006 billion—a 32% year-over-year surge. More critically, CEO Olivier Pomel confirmed that 80% of the company's Annual Recurring Revenue (ARR) is now tied to AI integrations. On Fintwit, the reaction was immediate. One widely shared post on X.com noted: "The 'Magnificent Seven' era has fractured. We are now in the 'Agentic AI' era where observability is the new rent." This sentiment captures the core of the second wave: as enterprises move from experimental LLMs to autonomous "agentic" workflows, the complexity of monitoring these systems has turned software "plumbing" into a high-margin necessity.

Datadog’s eight-figure deal with Anthropic and its strategic partnership with Sakana AI highlight a recovery in enterprise cloud spending that many feared had stalled. "The AI story is no longer theoretical, it's in the telemetry," wrote one user on r/wallstreetbets, where DDOG was the top-trending ticker after its 28% single-day surge. The company’s Bits AI SRE Agent and LLM Observability tools are now being used by 6,500 customers to track "tokens per watt" and agent latency—metrics that were virtually non-existent in corporate lexicons two years ago.

While Datadog provides the visibility, Micron is providing what institutional analysts now call the "oxygen" of the ecosystem: High-Bandwidth Memory (HBM). Micron’s Fiscal Q2 2026 results, reported in March, featured a revenue print of $23.86 billion—up 196% from the previous year. But it is the forward-looking supply chain that is truly rattling the bears. Micron has publicly confirmed that its entire HBM production capacity for all of calendar year 2026 is 100% sold out. The acceleration of HBM4 into high-volume production this quarter, intended for NVIDIA’s Vera Rubin platform, has effectively shifted the "wafer tax" onto traditional DRAM, creating a structural shortage that pushed Micron's gross margins to a record 74.9%.

"Micron is the silent partner of the AI revolution," noted a senior strategist at Goldman Sachs, whose recent note highlighted that MU alone accounted for 51% of all S&P 500 EPS revisions in the first half of 2026. This institutional fervor was cemented in March when Micron was added to the S&P 100, triggering massive structural buying from passive index funds. This stands in sharp contrast to the retail frenzy in the Direxion Daily MU Bull 2X Shares (MUU), which has seen its assets explode to $400 million as retail traders chase the "memory super-cycle." One Reddit user captured the mood succinctly: "Up 500% on January MU 800c. Every data center build-out is just a giant Micron order in disguise."

However, this "Second Wave" is unfolding against a backdrop of significant macro-political tension. The Trump administration, now in its second year, has aggressively pushed for tech deregulation, but the Treasury Department under Secretary Scott Bessent is closely monitoring the concentration of capital in AI infrastructure. Meanwhile, the Federal Reserve is at a historic crossroads. Chair Jerome Powell’s term as Chair expires in exactly seven days, on May 15, 2026. The market is currently pricing in a 65% chance of a "hawk-to-dove" transition under a potential Trump-appointed successor, even as April's BLS data showed core inflation stubbornly holding at 2.9%, driven by the massive energy demands of data center expansion.

The debate among economists at the BEA and FRED is no longer about whether AI is real, but whether the productivity gains can justify the current 470x P/E ratios seen in some "AI-native" software names. Skeptics point to the "AI Winter" risk if enterprise margins don't expand by Q4. As one contrarian voice on Stocktwits put it: "80% chance AI generates moderate productivity gains, but not enough to support these multiples. Watch for the reset if tokens-per-dollar doesn't scale."

Yet, for now, the data favors the bulls. The transition from GPUs to ASICs and 1.6T networking has made HBM4 the scarcest commodity on earth. Micron's projected FQ3 revenue of $33.5 billion suggests the "utility economy" of AI is just beginning to scale. If the first wave was about building the brains, the second wave—exemplified by Datadog’s $1.63 billion in bookings and Micron's power-efficient 12-layer stacks—is about the nervous system and the blood flow. In the 2026 economy, growth is no longer a broad-based rising tide; it is a surgical bet on the infrastructure that makes intelligence operational.

# MegaETH Kills 'Mega Mafia' Accelerator After Incubated Teams Raise $80 Million and Flee Ecosystem

On July 16, 2026, MegaETH co-founder Shuyao Kong took to X to confirm what had been quietly circulating through crypto founder group chats for weeks: Mega Mafia, the high-profile startup accelerator launched by MegaLabs, was dead. There would be no Cohort 3.0. 

For two years, Mega Mafia stood as one of the most generous incubator experiments in Web3. Across two cohorts, the program selected roughly 20 early-stage teams, pairing them with MegaLabs’ core engineering team for intensive co-living and technical residency. MegaLabs provided dedicated security auditing, market-making connections, liquid capital lines, and hands-on product design. In exchange, MegaLabs demanded zero equity, zero governance tokens, and zero contractual lock-ins—relying on the premise that providing world-class infrastructure and white-glove incubation would naturally bind high-caliber founders to the MegaETH network.

On paper, the program was a resounding success for participating founders. The 20 incubated startups leveraged MegaETH’s brand and technical endorsement to raise more than $80 million in pre-seed, seed, and Series A funding from top-tier venture capital firms. But as the network matured following the launch of MegaETH's mainnet and the rollout of its $MEGA Token Generation Event (TGE) and Terminal Season 1 campaign, the core team confronted a stark reality: venture capital success for incubated founders had failed to translate into protocol value for MegaETH.

"Very little of that value has trickled to Mega," Kong conceded in her public post detailing the shutdown. 

Once funded, the accelerator’s flagship alumni executed a widespread exodus toward rival execution layers and custom infrastructure. Global Token Exchange (GTE), one of the accelerator's showcase decentralized trading protocols, abandoned its exclusive deployment plan to spin out its own application-specific blockchain. Consumer app Noise migrated its primary infrastructure to Coinbase’s Layer-2 network, Base. HelloTrade, an automated market maker nurtured inside the accelerator, shifted its primary liquidity deployment to competing high-throughput Layer-1 Monad. Meanwhile, leverage protocol Cap announced a multi-chain expansion strategy that diluted its ties to MegaETH entirely. 

The breakdown of Mega Mafia underscores a growing crisis in Web3 grant and accelerator mechanics. For years, Layer-1 and Layer-2 networks have operated as subsidized launchpads, competing to hand out non-dilutive support and ecosystem marketing. However, without equity stakes or contractual token lockups, protocols function as free stepping stones for mercenary founding teams who farm early liquidity and venture backing before migrating to whatever network offers the next incentive wave. 

In response to this capital leakage, MegaLabs—backed by a $20 million seed round raised in June 2024 from Dragonfly Capital, Robot Ventures, Figment Capital, and angel investors including Ethereum co-founder Vitalik Buterin—is radically shifting its strategy. Instead of bankrolling third-party developers, MegaETH is reallocating its internal engineering and capital reserves toward building first-party "OMEGA apps."

These vertically integrated applications are built in-house specifically to exploit MegaETH’s custom execution engine, which targets real-time processing capabilities of up to 100,000 transactions per second with 10-millisecond block times. By coupling native wallet infrastructure and stablecoin liquidity directly with first-party trading, gaming, and consumer products, MegaETH aims to capture protocol fee revenues directly, insulating its economic model from external developer flight.

The strategic pivot has ignited intense debate across the crypto landscape. Proponents of the shift argue that Web3 infrastructure projects must adopt an "Apple model"—vertically integrating execution infrastructure with proprietary software applications—to achieve sustainable protocol economics. Skeptics, however, warn that shutting down open grant programs in favor of in-house app studios risks alienating independent developers and transforming permissionless networks into closed ecosystem silos.

As one industry observer noted on X following Kong's announcement, "MegaETH provided white-glove incubation for 20 teams that raised $80M for zero equity, only to watch them migrate to Base and Monad. It is a brutal case study proving that altruism without token lockups is not a sustainable L1 growth strategy."

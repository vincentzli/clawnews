# The Disarmament Trap: Inside the Cairo Deadlock Threatening Gaza's Peace Plan

In the guarded conference rooms of Cairo’s Fairmont Nile City hotel, the future of the Gaza Strip is currently suspended in a state of administrative limbo. On June 26, 2026, members of the National Committee for the Administration of Gaza (NCAG)—a 15-member Palestinian technocratic body established to govern the enclave's civil affairs—convened their latest round of planning sessions. Chaired by Dr. Ali Abdel Hamid Shaath, a former deputy minister in the Palestinian Authority, the committee met with Christophe Bigot, the European Union's Special Representative for the Middle East peace process, to hammer out a detailed "100-day action plan." The agenda was granular: the restoration of shattered water networks, the distribution of emergency medical supplies, and the resumption of municipal services. Yet, for all the talk of municipal budgets and logistics, the reality hanging over the Cairo talks was stark: the committee, which has been operating from temporary headquarters in Cairo since its formation in mid-January 2026, has still not been able to enter the Gaza Strip.

The administrative impasse is the direct result of a diplomatic deadlock that threatens to collapse Phase Two of the U.S.-brokered peace plan. Introduced by President Donald Trump in September 2025 and endorsed by UN Security Council Resolution 2803 in November of that year, the multi-phase framework was designed to transition Gaza from an active combat zone to a demilitarized, reconstructed territory. In January 2026, U.S. Special Envoy Steve Witkoff officially launched Phase Two, shifting the focus from initial hostage exchanges to long-term governance and security. But the transition has ground to a halt over a single, uncompromising demand: the complete disarmament of Hamas forces as a prerequisite for the withdrawal of Israeli troops and the transfer of administrative power to Dr. Shaath's committee.

For Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, the disarmament demand is non-negotiable. Facing intense domestic political pressure and a fragmented coalition, Netanyahu has repeatedly doubled down on his promise of "total victory." In public addresses, he has maintained that the Israel Defense Forces (IDF) will not cede security control of the enclave nor withdraw its forces until Hamas’s military infrastructure is fully dismantled. In Tel Aviv, the consensus among military planners is that allowing a technocratic body to govern Gaza while Hamas retains its hidden arsenals would simply pave the way for the group’s eventual resurgence.

Hamas has rejected this framework outright. In official communiqués delivered through mediators in Cairo, Doha, and Ankara, the group’s representatives argue that the demand for immediate disarmament contradicts the original peace plan brokered by Special Envoy Steve Witkoff. According to Hamas negotiators, the agreement was structured as a phased transition where security responsibilities would gradually transfer to a local police force and an International Stabilization Force (ISF) in parallel with an Israeli withdrawal, rather than as a unilateral surrender of weapons before IDF troops leave. Hamas leaders, including Khaled Meshaal, have publicly argued that disarmament without permanent ceasefire guarantees and a clear path to Palestinian statehood would leave the population vulnerable to renewed military strikes, effectively rendering them "easy victims."

Compounding the diplomatic friction is the controversial role of the Board of Peace, an international body chaired by President Donald Trump to oversee Gaza's reconstruction and transition. The board, which counts Jared Kushner, Steve Witkoff, Tony Blair, and former Vice President Kamala Harris among its executive members, has recently come under intense fire. In mid-June 2026, a "sensitive but unclassified" draft resolution leaked to international media revealed that the Board of Peace plans to grant itself, its personnel, international military forces, and private contractors absolute legal immunity from arrest, detention, or prosecution in Gaza. Furthermore, the draft contains provisions allowing the board to acquire public property in Gaza "free of charge."

The leaked draft has fueled intense debate on platforms like Reddit and X, where critics have questioned the Board’s commercial and legal designs. One highly upvoted comment on Reddit's r/politics characterized the proposal as "a corporate land grab wrapped in a peacekeeping flag," pointing to Jared Kushner's previous public remarks regarding the commercial value of Gaza's waterfront. While a Board of Peace representative stated that there is "no operative resolution or immunity framework of the kind" currently in place, the controversy has underscored deep-seated anxieties over the transparency and ultimate motives of the transitional authority.

Meanwhile, the security vacuum inside Gaza continues to widen. While the NCAG refines its 100-day plan in Cairo, the absence of a functioning local police force or an authorized international peacekeeping mission has left the enclave in a state of lawlessness. Gangs and local clans have increasingly seized control of humanitarian aid distribution, while sporadic skirmishes between remnants of Hamas and the IDF continue to report daily casualties. The U.S. has struggled to secure formal troop commitments for the proposed International Stabilization Force, as European and Arab allies refuse to deploy personnel into a zone without a durable, agreed-upon peace framework.

Diplomats in Cairo warn that the window for saving the transition is closing. Egyptian Foreign Minister Badr Abdelatty has engaged in frantic shuttle diplomacy, meeting with Christophe Bigot and regional security experts to find a compromise on the disarmament timeline. The fear among mediators is that if Phase Two collapses, the region will slide back into full-scale war, rendering the work of Dr. Shaath's committee entirely moot. For now, the 100-day plan remains a paper exercise, drawn up in the relative safety of Cairo while the enclave it is meant to govern remains locked in ruins and armed standoff.

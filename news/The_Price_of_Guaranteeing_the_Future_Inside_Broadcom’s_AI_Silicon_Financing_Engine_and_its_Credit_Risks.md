# The Price of Guaranteeing the Future: Inside Broadcom’s AI Silicon Financing Engine and its Credit Risks

When Broadcom Inc. (AVGO) shares tumbled 8.11% in mid-August 2026, the sell-off caught equity markets by surprise but felt like an inevitability in the bond pits. For months, fixed-income traders had been quietly bidding up the cost of insuring Broadcom’s debt, with credit default swap spreads and bond yields widening by 20 to 30 basis points since June. The immediate catalyst was twofold: a critical remote code execution vulnerability (CVE-2026-59310) in its newly acquired VMware vCenter platform, and a sobering research note from Bank of America analyst Tom Curcuruto downgrading Broadcom's credit rating from Overweight to Marketweight. Underneath these dual shocks lies a deeper structural debate over how CEO Hock Tan is funding the company's aggressive pivot to custom artificial intelligence application-specific integrated circuits (ASICs) and whether Broadcom is taking on massive, unacknowledged credit risk to fuel its growth.

At the heart of the controversy is the "AI XPV Platform," a bespoke off-balance-sheet financing vehicle launched in June 2026. Structured in partnership with private equity giant Apollo Global Management and Blackstone’s Credit & Insurance Business, the platform is designed to fund the acquisition of Broadcom's custom XPUs and networking pipelines. Under the arrangement, institutional credit funds purchase the hardware and lease it to capital-constrained frontier AI labs, most notably Anthropic. The first tranche is a massive $35 billion capital commitment enabling Anthropic to secure 1 gigawatt of data center capacity. By leasing the hardware rather than buying it upfront, Anthropic avoids bloating its own balance sheet, while Broadcom books immediate hardware revenue. 

But BofA’s Tom Curcuruto warned that the structure carries a heavy catch. If the XPV platform scales to its stated goal of enabling 20 gigawatts of compute capacity through 2028, it could amass up to $370 billion in senior debt by mid-2029. To make this debt attractive to Blackstone and Apollo, Broadcom has agreed to provide "residual value guarantees" (RVGs) to backstop the lease obligations. On the initial $35 billion tranche, Broadcom has disclosed a maximum loss exposure of $29 billion. Under BofA's worst-case stress tests, Broadcom's potential loss exposure across the entire scaled platform could reach $42 billion. 

On social media, the credit downgrade sparked fierce debate between growth-oriented equity investors and risk-averse bondholders. One bullish commentator on Fintwit argued: "AVGO is literally printing cash. EBITDA at $15.24B (69% margin) and they are on track to guide $16B in Q3 AI chip revenue. The BofA downgrade is a classic debt analyst missing the forest for the trees. The $370B is off-balance sheet and backed by Apollo/Blackstone. AVGO's max exposure on Anthropic's tranche is capped at $29B. Panic buying opportunity." 

Conversely, a more skeptical contributor on the Reddit forum r/stocks countered: "People are ignoring the risk here. What happens if Anthropic doesn't achieve scale, or OpenAI switches off custom silicon? Broadcom's residual value guarantees (RVGs) mean they are on the hook if these custom XPUs become obsolete. There is no secondary market for custom ASICs. This is a massive hidden credit risk that could blow up their A- credit rating."

The debate hits a sensitive nerve because Broadcom’s traditional balance sheet is already heavily leveraged from its $69 billion acquisition of VMware in November 2023. As of Broadcom's fiscal second-quarter earnings report released on June 3, 2026, the company carried $64.91 billion in total debt against just $19.63 billion in cash and cash equivalents. The acquisition of VMware was supposed to build a fortress of recurring, high-margin software revenue to offset the cyclicality of the chip business. In fiscal Q2 2026, Broadcom reported record total revenue of $22.19 billion, up 48% year-over-year, and Adjusted EBITDA of $15.24 billion. However, the shift in product mix toward lower-margin custom AI silicon is already compressing profitability. Broadcom's gross margin fell to 77.1% in Q2, representing a 230 basis point decline year-over-year. As Broadcom guides for fiscal third-quarter AI semiconductor revenue to accelerate to $16.0 billion (out of $29.4 billion in total guided revenue), margins are expected to face further dilution because custom ASICs typically yield gross margins of 65% to 70%, far below VMware's software gross margins of approximately 93%.

This margin compression makes Broadcom highly vulnerable to operational hiccups, which are now emerging in VMware. On July 29, 2026, Broadcom published security advisory VMSA-2026-0006.1, detailing CVE-2026-59310—a critical CVSS 9.8 directory traversal vulnerability in the VMware vCenter Server Syslog that allows unauthenticated attackers to bypass security layers and execute arbitrary code. By August 3, 2026, security researchers at QUIRSO reported that state-sponsored hackers were actively exploiting the bug, using the directory traversal flaw to install cron jobs and drop a `reverse_ssh` backdoor to establish command-and-control persistence. Compromised systems have been detected across 47 countries, with heavy clusters in Germany and the United States. 

The software exploit has aggravated an already tense customer base. Following the VMware acquisition, Hock Tan aggressively transitioned customers to subscription-based models, in some cases raising licensing fees by 300% to 500% and cutting off perpetual licenses. On Reddit’s r/sysadmin, the security flaw became a lightning rod for criticism of Broadcom’s support model: "Sysadmins are losing their minds over CVE-2026-59310. An unauthenticated 9.8 CVSS RCE in vCenter Syslog that's actively being exploited to drop reverse_ssh backdoor. Broadcom's VMware licensing hikes already pissed everyone off, and now their support portal is making it hard for unentitled users to get the patch. Absolute disaster."

If enterprise clients accelerate their departure from VMware due to security vulnerabilities and pricing hikes, Broadcom’s cash generation could falter just as its off-balance-sheet commitments balloon. Rating agencies are watching closely. In September 2025, S&P Global Ratings upgraded Broadcom's issuer credit rating to 'A-' with a stable outlook, while Moody's upgraded it to 'A3'. However, both agencies have warned that off-balance-sheet guarantees are treated as "debt-like obligations." Under Trump administration Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent and Federal Reserve Chair Kevin Warsh, the macro-credit environment remains sensitive to corporate leverage, particularly in high-growth technology sectors. If S&P or Moody’s decide to consolidate the XPV platform’s obligations onto Broadcom’s balance sheet, Broadcom’s credit metrics would deteriorate rapidly. 

Ultimately, the tension at Broadcom is between the short-term demand narrative and the long-term structural risk. The custom AI chip market is growing exponentially, but by underwriting its customers’ infrastructure leases, Broadcom is essentially acting as a subprime lender for AI compute. If Anthropic or other lessees default, or if proprietary silicon becomes obsolete in a fast-evolving technological landscape, Broadcom will be left holding hundreds of billions of dollars in highly specialized, illiquid hardware that no other buyer wants. For now, Hock Tan is betting that the AI boom is permanent. But as bond spreads widen and security exploits chip away at VMware’s software moat, the market is beginning to price in the possibility that Tan’s financial engineering has finally met its match.

# The Quiet Annexation: Inside the Bureaucratic Machine Reshaping the West Bank

On June 9, 2024, in a Jerusalem conference room filled with municipal officials and settler leaders, Israeli Finance Minister Bezalel Smotrich outlined the strategic blueprint he had spent eighteen months embedding into the state apparatus. The audio recording, obtained and released by the Israeli watchdog Peace Now, contained none of the standard political posturing. Instead, Smotrich spoke with the measured cadence of an executive detailing a structural reorganization.

"I tell you that it's mega-dramatic," Smotrich told the gathering. "Such changes change a system’s DNA." His declared objective was the institutional extraction of civilian governance in the West Bank from the chain of command of the Israel Defense Forces. To insulate the process from diplomatic blowback, Smotrich explained that the new civilian apparatus was housed directly inside the Ministry of Defense: "It will be easier to swallow in the international and legal context, so that people won’t say that we are now doing an annexation."

For 57 years following the 1967 war, the West Bank’s Palestinian population and its Jewish residents lived under a singular sovereign authority: the IDF Central Command and its operational organ, the Civil Administration, created in 1981 under Military Order 947. Under public international law and the Fourth Geneva Convention, an occupying power administers conquered territory through a temporary military hierarchy obligated to safeguard the local population and maintain the status quo ante.

That system has undergone a fundamental administrative mutation. Through coalition protocols, cabinet resolutions, and revised military decrees, Smotrich—serving simultaneously as Finance Minister and an additional minister within the Defense Ministry—has transferred primary civilian powers away from uniformed military officers to a civilian body under his direct political control: the Settlement Administration (Minhelet HaHityashvut).

The operational hinge turned on May 29, 2024, when the commander of IDF Central Command, Major General Yehuda Fox, signed a military order delegating substantial administrative authority over Area C to Hillel Roth. Roth, an ideological compatriot of Smotrich and former municipal official from the Yitzhar settlement, was appointed Civilian Deputy Head of the Civil Administration. 

Under the terms of the delegation, authority over statutory planning, zoning schemes, land allocation, infrastructural management, building enforcement, and real estate transactions was transferred to Roth. In July 2026, the Israeli Civil Service Commission entrenched these reforms by making the Settlement Administration a permanent division within the Ministry of Defense and extending Roth’s tenure to six years. The institutional divide between domestic civilian government and military administration has been formally dismantled.

"This is not creeping annexation; it is codified administrative annexation," stated Michael Sfard, an Israeli legal scholar and attorney who has argued prominent land-rights cases before Israel's High Court of Justice. By placing territorial management under an elected Israeli minister rather than a military commander bound by the laws of belligerent occupation, the state has fundamentally transformed its legal posture in the territory.

The pace of real estate consolidation has exceeded every previous period of settlement growth since the 1993 Oslo Accords. According to cartographic documentation compiled by Peace Now, the Israeli government declared 23,700 dunams (roughly 5,856 acres or 23.7 square kilometers) of West Bank territory as state land during 2024 alone. This single-year total surpassed the combined state-land declarations of the preceding twenty years.

The legal mechanism rests upon an expansive interpretation of the 1858 Ottoman Land Code. Under Ottoman jurisprudence, agricultural tracts (*Miri*) left unworked for three consecutive years without legal impediment could revert to the sovereign. In 1979, following the Israeli High Court's *Elon Moreh* ruling—which prohibited the confiscation of private Palestinian land for civilian settlements on military grounds—state attorney Plia Albeck constructed a framework utilizing uncultivated, unregistered lands to generate "state property." Under Smotrich and Roth, this methodology was expanded at scale through the Civil Administration’s "Blue Line" survey team. In March 2024, the authorities declared 8,000 dunams in the northern Jordan Valley as state land; in late June 2024, an additional 12,700 dunams were gazetted along the Jordan Valley basin.

Simultaneously, the coalition executed what planners describe as an infrastructure-first regularization. In April 2024, Smotrich instructed government ministries and state corporations to prepare municipal budgets and connect 68 unauthorized outposts to water, electricity, telecommunications, and access roads, effectively normalizing their existence before formal statutory approval.

By late June 2024, the Security Cabinet ratified the retroactive legalization of five strategically positioned outposts: Evyatar, Givat Asaf, Sde Efraim, Heletz, and Adorayim. Evyatar, built atop Mount Sabih adjacent to the Palestinian town of Beita, had generated protracted clashes and diplomatic inquiries from Western capitals. Its formal authorization signaled the institutional dominance of the settlement lobby over the traditional security establishment.

This structural overhaul was enabled by the parliamentary arithmetic of Israel’s Knesset. Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu’s government has rested upon a razor-thin majority dependent on the 13 combined seats of Smotrich’s Religious Zionism and National Security Minister Itamar Ben-Gvir’s Otzma Yehudit. Smotrich systematically applied this political leverage to extract operational concessions.

To enforce cabinet compliance, Smotrich exercised his statutory authority as Finance Minister to withhold hundreds of millions of shekels in tax clearance revenues collected by Israel on behalf of the Palestinian Authority. Concurrently, he threatened not to sign indemnity extensions protecting Israeli commercial institutions—principally Bank Hapoalim and Israel Discount Bank—from cross-border financing litigation when clearing transactions with Palestinian lenders. 

During cabinet meetings in June 2024, Netanyahu suspended sessions to negotiate terms directly with his Finance Minister. Smotrich made his position unequivocal: the indemnity waivers would be extended and partial tax distributions released only in exchange for the regularization of the five outposts, approval of thousands of settlement housing permits, and the transfer of heritage and preservation enforcement in Area B to Israeli civilian inspectors. Netanyahu conceded to each requirement.

Having secured administrative supremacy, Smotrich declared 2025 the "year of sovereignty" over the West Bank, laying groundwork for draft annexation bills that reached preliminary Knesset floor debates by late 2025. Across digital forums and policy networks, the developments sparked intense debate. On Reddit’s r/politics, one widely cited analysis observed: "The two-state solution isn’t dying; it was administratively dissected and buried under zoning permits." On X, settler advocates framed the administrative integration as equitable governance, arguing that over 500,000 residents should no longer be subjected to military red tape for basic municipal services.

The legal reaction from international tribunals was swift. On July 19, 2024, the International Court of Justice delivered an advisory opinion determining that Israel’s continued occupation of Palestinian territory is unlawful. The ICJ explicitly scrutinized the administration of Area C, concluding that Israel’s permanent infrastructure, natural resource exploitation, and application of domestic legislation constitute annexation in breach of Article 49 of the Fourth Geneva Convention.

The geopolitical fallout has strained diplomatic channels. In late 2024, the Biden administration utilized Executive Order 14115 to sanction the primary settlement development cooperative, Amana. Following his return to the presidency in January 2025, President Donald Trump rescinded the executive order, lifting U.S. sanctions against Amana. Nevertheless, the Trump administration did not issue blanket approval for statutory annexation. President Trump reiterated to Israeli officials that formal annexation remained an explicit boundary that would jeopardize bilateral cooperation and disrupt broader Middle East strategy.

Concurrently, European and Commonwealth allies broke diplomatic precedent. In June 2025, the governments of the United Kingdom, Canada, Australia, New Zealand, and Norway announced joint sanctions targeting Bezalel Smotrich and Itamar Ben-Gvir over their roles in facilitating outpost regularizations and displacement policies. While U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio criticized the allied measures, stating they were counterproductive to regional stabilization, the sanctions highlighted Israel's growing legal exposure in Western capitals.

Across the region, the administrative consolidation has complicated the Abraham Accords. The United Arab Emirates has repeatedly informed international intermediaries that West Bank annexation remains an absolute red line. Furthermore, diplomatic efforts to secure normalization between Israel and Saudi Arabia have stalled, with Riyadh refusing to advance bilateral treaties without binding, verified commitments toward Palestinian self-determination.

Yet inside the offices of the Settlement Administration in Tel Aviv and Beit El, administrative integration proceeds methodically. Unencumbered by high-profile parliamentary votes or international diplomatic assemblies, Hillel Roth’s planning directors are reshaping the map. Through parcel classifications, state land declarations, and utility easements, the physical and bureaucratic integration of the West Bank is advancing by decree—one dunam at a time.

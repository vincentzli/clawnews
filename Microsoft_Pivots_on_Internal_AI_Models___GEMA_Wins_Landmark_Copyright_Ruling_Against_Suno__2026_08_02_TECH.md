# Microsoft Pivots on Internal AI Models & GEMA Wins Landmark Copyright Ruling Against Suno

### **Microsoft Shifts AI Strategy to Reassure Wall Street on Capital Expenditure**
[Read More](news/Inside_Microsoft’s_MAI_Pivot_The_175B_Capex_Reckoning,_Custom_Silicon_Co-Design,_and_the_OpenAI_Margin_Squeeze.md)
Microsoft has initiated a significant strategic pivot during its quarterly earnings call on July 29, 2026. Facing investor anxiety over its mounting artificial intelligence capital expenditure, CEO Satya Nadella pitched internally developed "MAI" (Microsoft AI) models directly to Wall Street analysts as a tool to protect profit margins. This family of models, including the reasoning-focused MAI-Thinking-1 and the coding-optimized MAI-Code-1-Flash, is being positioned as a cost-effective, high-control alternative to external offerings from long-time partners like OpenAI and Anthropic. This development follows a strong quarterly performance where Azure cloud revenue grew 43% year-over-year. By integrating these in-house models with its existing productivity suite and security infrastructure, Microsoft aims to mitigate the "margin leakage" associated with third-party software licensing. The strategic repositioning seeks to address Wall Street's "capex super cycle" worries by showcasing Microsoft's ability to act as a self-sufficient, end-to-end AI developer, even as it maintains its multi-billion-dollar investments in cloud infrastructure for partner workloads.

### **Munich Court Hands GEMA Victory in Copyright Ruling Against Suno Music**
[Read More](news/Suno’s_Waterloo_in_Munich_How_German_Courts_Exposed_the_Technical_and_Legal_Limits_of_'Fair_Use'_AI_Training.md)
In a landmark decision for the generative AI music sector, the Munich Regional Court ruled in favor of German collecting society GEMA in its copyright infringement lawsuit against AI music platform Suno on July 31, 2026. The court determined that Suno infringed on copyrights by using GEMA-represented musical works—including prominent hits like "Forever Young," "Mambo No. 5," "Rasputin," and "Atemlos durch die Nacht"—to train its AI model without a license or compensation. The court rejected Suno's fair use and transformative output defenses, ruling that the company had effectively "memorized" and reproduced portions of the protected works. Suno was ordered to cease unauthorized use of GEMA's catalog for AI training, disclose revenue generated from these activities, and pay damages. While this first-instance judgment is not yet legally binding, Suno has expressed strong disagreement and announced plans to evaluate an appeal. The ruling establishes a critical legal precedent in Europe, signaling that developers of generative AI models must negotiate licenses and pay rights holders for training data.

### **Waymo Resumes Freeway Robotaxi Operations in Major Cities After Software Recall**

Waymo officially resumed freeway operations for its autonomous robotaxi service in Phoenix, San Francisco, and Los Angeles on July 30, 2026. The Alphabet-owned company had suspended freeway driving approximately two months prior following a voluntary recall designed to address software issues that caused confusion when navigating complex construction zones. During the pause, Waymo updated its sensor integration and path-planning algorithms to ensure reliable handling of temporary lane closures and emergency responder interactions. In addition to restarting freeway services, Waymo is preparing for a new public service rollout in Sacramento scheduled for August 2026, with local city officials coordinating the integration of driverless vehicles. Despite these expansions, the autonomous vehicle industry continues to navigate regulatory scrutiny and emergency response coordination challenges, particularly following minor fleet incidents in late July that highlighted the ongoing complexity of deploying driverless fleets in dense urban environments.

### **European Union Enters Active Enforcement Phase for Key EU AI Act Rules**
[Read More](news/The_Brussels_Split_Europe’s_AI_Act_Enforcement_Hits_Big_Tech_Today,_and_the_Friction_is_Real.md)
On August 2, 2026, the European Union entered a critical enforcement phase of the EU AI Act as key transparency and regulatory oversight rules became active. Under the new rules, specifically Article 50, organizations deploying AI systems must inform users when they are interacting with a machine, and all AI-generated or manipulated media, such as deepfakes, must be clearly labeled with machine-readable markings. While a grace period until December 2, 2026, allows existing generative AI systems to implement these markings, the European Commission's AI Office now holds active enforcement powers over general-purpose AI (GPAI) model providers. The office is authorized to request technical documentation, conduct safety audits, order the removal of non-compliant models, and impose fines of up to 3% of a company's global annual turnover. Furthermore, EU Member States were required to establish at least one national AI regulatory sandbox by August 2 to facilitate safe innovation, setting a structured compliance framework ahead of the broader high-risk AI system deadlines scheduled for 2027 and 2028.

### **AMD Secures 15-Year, 2.5-Gigawatt AI Infrastructure Deal with Core Scientific**
On July 28, 2026, AMD and high-performance hosting provider Core Scientific announced a massive 15-year infrastructure partnership, securing up to 2.5 gigawatts (GW) of data center capacity to support AMD's AI hardware scaling. Under the agreement, AMD has committed to an initial 530 megawatts (MW) of "AI-ready" capacity across five U.S. states—Texas, Oklahoma, Alabama, and Georgia—with deployments scheduled to begin in 2027. The contract grants AMD an option to expand this capacity to 2.5 GW through December 2028. Valued at over $14 billion in potential base contracted revenue for Core Scientific, the deal also awards AMD warrants to purchase up to 30 million shares of Core Scientific common stock, with vesting tied to the megawatt load deployed. This partnership provides the essential physical footprint required for AMD to deploy its Instinct GPUs and EPYC CPUs at enterprise scale, while marking a significant milestone in Core Scientific's transition from legacy cryptocurrency mining to high-density high-performance computing hosting.

### **Tricentis Acquires Tabnine to Pioneer Agentic Quality Engineering Platforms**
Tricentis announced the acquisition of AI-coding developer Tabnine on July 30, 2026, in a deal reported to be worth tens of millions of dollars. The acquisition aims to solve the "context problem" in enterprise software testing, where autonomous AI agents struggle to make reliable quality decisions due to a lack of understanding of complex system architectures. By integrating Tabnine's Enterprise Context Engine—which utilizes knowledge graphs to map code, documentation, tickets, and infrastructure metadata—into the Tricentis Agentic Quality Engineering Platform, the combined system will enable AI agents to perform highly contextual and reliable software testing. This integration is designed to reduce AI hallucinations and improve decision-making accuracy within complex enterprise environments. The acquisition reflects a broader industry trend where software quality assurance platforms are shifting toward agentic architectures to automate end-to-end validation processes.

### **Moonshot AI Raises $3.5 Billion in Series F Funding Round at $35 Billion Valuation**
Beijing-based artificial intelligence startup Moonshot AI has closed a $3.5 billion Series F funding round, lifting its valuation to $35 billion. The massive round, which was anchored by China's National Artificial Intelligence Industry Investment Fund, represents a dramatic rise from the company's $10 billion valuation in January 2026. The primary catalyst for the funding was the successful July 16 release of Kimi K3, a 2.8-trillion-parameter open-weight model featuring native multimodality, a 1-million-token context window, and advanced long-horizon reasoning. Following the closure of the Series F, Moonshot AI has reportedly initiated talks for a pre-IPO funding round targeting a $50 billion valuation as it prepares for a Hong Kong public offering. The round underscores the intense capitalization of Chinese AI development and highlights the competitive pressure open-weight architectures are placing on closed-source model providers.

### **FDA Approves Novartis’ Pluvicto for Early-Stage PSMA-Positive Prostate Cancer**
The U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) granted expanded approval for Novartis’ radioligand therapy Pluvicto (lutetium Lu 177 vipivotide tetraxetan) on July 31, 2026. The expanded indication allows Pluvicto to be used in combination with an androgen receptor pathway inhibitor (ARPI) for adult patients with PSMA-positive metastatic androgen pathway modulation-naïve or -sensitive (mAPMN/S) prostate cancer, moving the treatment significantly earlier in the disease progression continuum. The approval is based on Phase III PSMAddition trial data, which demonstrated a 28% to 33% reduction in the risk of radiographic disease progression or death when Pluvicto was added to standard-of-care therapy. Eligible patients must be identified using an approved PSMA PET imaging agent, such as Locametz. This expansion is expected to double the eligible patient population for Pluvicto, representing a major milestone in targeted radioligand therapeutics.

### **The ArXiv "Slop" Crisis: Academic Publishing Implements One-Year AI Ban**
Academic integrity faced its sternest test this week as ArXiv announced a mandatory one-year ban for any authors submitting "obviously AI-generated work" without rigorous human oversight. The move follows a "swamp of slop" that forced the temporary closure of the computer science review section after thousands of papers containing hallucinated citations and invented mathematical proofs flooded the system. The ban is a direct response to the democratization of LLMs, which allowed "paper mills" to generate high volumes of low-quality preprints. ArXiv's new policy requires banned authors to prove their credibility by publishing in a traditional peer-reviewed venue before being allowed back onto the preprint server. This decision has sparked a fierce debate on Reddit's r/MachineLearning, where researchers are divided between the need for strict "Human-in-the-loop" verification and the fear that legitimate AI-assisted research might be unfairly penalized in the crackdown.

### **Recursive Meltdown: How Autonomous Agents Paralyzed GitHub**
[Read More](news/Machine-Scale_Meltdown_Inside_the_GitHub_Blackout_and_the_Rise_of_Agentic_Guardrails.md)
GitHub’s infrastructure faced a "near-total collapse" this week as autonomous AI agents like Devin and OpenDevin triggered massive recursive API loops that overwhelmed the platform’s capacity. Microsoft confirmed that the outages, which saw GitHub’s uptime drop below 85% for several hours, were caused by agents entering "automation feedback loops"—where one agent’s failure triggered a retry that increased system load, causing more failures across the network. GitHub CTO Vlad Fedorov admitted that the platform was "built for human-paced commits," and the sudden 30x surge in "machine-scale" activity exposed fundamental flaws in how modern version control handles autonomous workers. The incident has reignited the debate over "Agentic Guardrails," with many in the tech community calling for a "Rate-Limit for Reason," suggesting that AI agents must be throttled by logic-density rather than just simple API request counts.

Analysts suggest this move is a direct attempt to bypass Western chip export controls by focusing on domestic specialized "Small-Brain" motor control chips that require less advanced fabrication nodes than general-purpose LLM processors.

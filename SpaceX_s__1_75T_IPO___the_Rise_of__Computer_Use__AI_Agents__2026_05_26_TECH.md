# SpaceX’s $1.75T IPO & the Rise of "Computer Use" AI Agents

### **SpaceX Files for Massive $1.75 Trillion IPO to Fuel Mars Ambitions** [Read More](news/The_1.75_Trillion_Singularity_Decoding_the_SpaceXAI_S-1,_the_4.2B_Q1_Burn,_and_the_Rise_of_the_Orbital_AI_Mesh.md)
SpaceX has officially filed its S-1 prospectus with the SEC, setting the stage for the largest Initial Public Offering in history. The company is targeting a valuation between $1.75 trillion and $2 trillion, a figure that analysts suggest could make Elon Musk the world’s first trillionaire. On social media, debates are polarized between "hyper-bulls" who see SpaceX as the backbone of a new space economy and skeptics who question a price-to-sales ratio that dwarfs even Nvidia’s peak. The filing also disclosed that X (formerly Twitter) now hosts 550 million monthly active users, with 20% actively engaged with xAI’s Grok, highlighting the deepening synergy across the Musk ecosystem.

### **The "Computer Use" Era: Microsoft and Google Move from Chat to Action** [Read More](news/microsoft_computer_use_ga_analysis.md)
The paradigm of AI interaction shifted fundamentally this week as Microsoft announced the General Availability (GA) of "Computer Use" agents within Copilot Studio. Unlike traditional automation that relies on brittle APIs, these agents utilize vision and reasoning to navigate any desktop or web interface exactly like a human—clicking, typing, and scrolling across legacy software. The GA release includes enterprise-grade governance through Microsoft Purview and supports both Anthropic’s Claude Sonnet 4.5 and OpenAI’s latest CUA models. Simultaneously, Google unveiled Gemini 3.5 Flash, a model specifically optimized for these "agentic" workflows, boasting a 4x speed improvement over Gemini 3.1. While Microsoft admits current success rates for complex, multi-app desktop tasks hover around 35%, the technical community on X.com is abuzz with the implications of "API-less" automation. As one prominent engineer noted, "We are moving from AI that talks to us about work, to AI that simply does the work."

### **Andrej Karpathy’s Anthropic "Talent Coup" and the Claude Mythos Model**

Karpathy’s mandate is to build a "self-accelerating" research team that uses Claude to automate the development of the next generation of LLMs. This talent shift coincides with Anthropic’s unveiling of "Claude Mythos," a frontier model specifically designed for high-end cybersecurity. Mythos has demonstrated unprecedented capabilities in identifying and neutralizing zero-day vulnerabilities, prompting Anthropic to restrict its use to a select group of defensive partners under "Project Glasswing." The debate on Reddit’s r/MachineLearning suggests this marks a new phase in the AI talent war, where the focus has shifted from raw model scale to the automation of the research process itself.

### **Nature Landmarks: AI Agents "Robin" and "Co-Scientist" Solve the Discovery Gap** [Read More](news/Beyond_the_Napkin_Gap_How_DeepMind_and_FutureHouse’s_Multi-Agent_'Scientists'_Just_Compressed_Decades_of_Pharma_RD_into_Hours.md)
Two landmark papers published in *Nature* this week have demonstrated that AI is no longer just a tool for scientists, but an autonomous collaborator. "Robin," developed by FutureHouse, and "Co-Scientist," built by Google DeepMind, have successfully managed the entire scientific method—from hypothesis generation to laboratory validation—without human intervention. The systems reportedly reduced the cognitive labor of a drug discovery cycle from 900 human hours to less than two hours, a 450x speedup. Co-Scientist successfully identified 30 new drug candidates for acute myeloid leukemia, one of which has already shown "significant efficacy" in initial lab tests. Dr. Christopher Olah noted on X that while these agents still require physical lab interfaces, their ability to navigate the "non-linguistic reasoning" of biology marks the end of the "napkin gap" in physical AI.

### **NASA’s HPSC Chip: Bringing 500x Computing Power to Deep Space**
NASA has completed successful environmental testing of its High Performance Spaceflight Computing (HPSC) chip, a radiation-hardened RISC-V processor developed in partnership with Microchip Technology. The HPSC delivers 100x to 500x the computing power of current space-rated processors, such as those powering the Mars rovers. This breakthrough is critical for the upcoming Artemis lunar missions and future Mars exploration, as it allows spacecraft to perform real-time AI analysis and autonomous navigation. Currently, deep-space missions must wait for a 24-minute signal delay for Earth-based instructions; the HPSC chip will allow future landers to "think" and adjust their trajectory in milliseconds. Space enthusiasts on Reddit have hailed this as the most significant upgrade to space hardware since the transition from vacuum tubes to transistors.

### **Longevity Breakthrough: Retro Biosciences Launches Autophagy Human Trials**
Retro Biosciences, the cellular rejuvenation startup backed by Sam Altman, has officially launched Phase 1 human trials for RTR242. This oral therapeutic is designed to restart "autophagy"—the cell’s internal waste-clearing process that naturally declines with age. The trial aims to treat early-onset Alzheimer’s by clearing amyloid plaques more efficiently than previous antibody treatments. Retro Biosciences also announced a new $1.8 billion funding round, with the explicit goal of adding 10 healthy years to the human lifespan. The announcement has sparked a heated debate on X regarding the ethics of "longevity inequality," while medical researchers are focused on whether the "cellular waste" theory of aging will finally yield a production-ready therapy.

### **The Healthcare Crisis: 90% Error Rates in AI-Driven Insurance Denials** [Read More](news/The_1.2-Second_Death_Sentence_How_'Black_Box'_Insurance_Algorithms_Created_a_Systemic_Crisis—and_the_Regulatory_War_of_2026.md)
A series of federal lawsuits and investigative reports have exposed the "dark side" of AI in the US healthcare system. Data revealed that UnitedHealth’s naviHealth AI and Cigna’s PxDx system are being used to "batch-deny" medical claims in as little as 1.2 seconds, with an staggering 90% error rate—meaning 9 out of 10 denials are overturned when patients eventually appeal. This "industrial-scale" denial of care has prompted the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) to issue emergency 2026 regulations requiring a "human-in-the-loop" for every denied claim. On Reddit, the controversy has gone viral, with users sharing evidence of life-saving surgeries being denied by algorithms that insurers claimed were "merely predictive." The legal fallout is expected to be the largest in healthcare history, as states move to ban "black-box" algorithms from making clinical decisions.

### **OpenAI and Broadcom Unveil "Jalapeño" Custom AI Inference Chip**
[Read More](news/Inside_Jalapeño_How_OpenAI_and_Broadcom’s_3nm_ASIC_Bypasses_Nvidia’s_Blackwell_Moat_to_Redefine_LLM_Inference.md)
On June 24, 2026, OpenAI and Broadcom officially pulled back the curtain on "Jalapeño," OpenAI's first custom-designed silicon. Unlike general-purpose graphics processing units (GPUs) that handle a wide variety of parallel processing tasks, Jalapeño is an inference-native Application-Specific Integrated Circuit (ASIC) built strictly to run large language model (LLM) serving workloads. The silicon architecture features a massive reticle-sized layout integrating six High Bandwidth Memory (HBM) modules, allowing it to optimize memory movement and networking routing pathways for autoregressive token generation. 

The launch has ignited intense technical debates across hardware forums like Reddit's r/hardware and X.com. Engineers are questioning the viability of custom ASICs against Nvidia's dominant Blackwell architecture. While general-purpose GPUs excel in training models, industry experts note they introduce massive latency and cost overheads for simple model inference. By stripping out unnecessary floating-point capabilities, Jalapeño is designed to optimize memory bandwidth, which is the primary bottleneck in serving LLMs. Early performance reports from engineering samples running in OpenAI's labs suggest the chip could slash inference costs by approximately 50%. 

Another major point of discussion is the chip's rapid development cycle. Developed from initial schematics to a completed tape-out in a blistering nine months, OpenAI and Broadcom utilized OpenAI's own model suites to automate physical design rule checking and verify routing layout options. Fabricated by TSMC and integrated into rack systems by Celestica, the chips are targeted for a gigawatt-scale deployment in partner data centers by the end of 2026. This development marks a major step in OpenAI's bid for vertical integration and highlights Broadcom's expanding footprint in the custom silicon market, which already includes collaborations with Google and Meta. More details are available on [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com) and [Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com).

### **Google and SpaceX Enter $30 Billion Terrestrial Compute Agreement**
[Read More](news/Untitled_16.md)
In a major realignment of cloud infrastructure resources, Google and SpaceX have entered into a massive three-year, $30 billion terrestrial compute leasing agreement. Under the terms of the deal, Google will pay SpaceX approximately $920 million monthly starting in October 2026 and running through June 2029. In return, Google will gain access to approximately 110,000 Nvidia GPUs hosted in terrestrial data center facilities operated by SpaceX, which were substantially expanded following SpaceX's integration of xAI’s physical infrastructure. 

The contract contains a critical performance clause allowing Google to terminate the agreement if SpaceX fails to deliver the committed GPU capacity by September 30, 2026. The technical community on X and Reddit has reacted with surprise, debating why a company that develops its own custom Tensor Processing Units (TPUs) would lease Nvidia hardware from a direct competitor. Industry analysts suggest that Google faces an immediate, acute compute capacity shortage for training its next-generation frontier models. SpaceX’s rapid expansion of terrestrial data centers—adjacent to high-capacity energy grids—provided a speed-to-market advantage that Google’s own internal data center builds could not match.

Separately, the agreement highlights the ongoing discussion surrounding orbital data centers. While Google has explored space-based computing through its "Project Suncatcher" and SpaceX has filed with the FCC for satellite compute constellations, the massive $30 billion deal is strictly terrestrial. Commentators point out that while orbital data centers promise unlimited solar power and natural vacuum cooling, the near-term demands of frontier AI models remain firmly anchored to Earth's power grids. Further reporting on this deal can be found on [The Street](https://www.thestreet.com).

### **Agility Robotics Announces $2.5 Billion Humanoid SPAC Merger**
[Read More](news/The_2.5_Billion_Bipedal_Gamble_Inside_Agility_Robotics’_SPAC_Integration,_Kinematic_Hurdles,_and_the_Battle_for_the_Warehouse_Floor.md)
On June 24, 2026, Agility Robotics announced a definitive business combination agreement to go public through a merger with special purpose acquisition company (SPAC) Churchill Capital Corp XI (NASDAQ: CCXI). The transaction values Agility at a $2.5 billion pre-money equity valuation and is expected to provide more than $620 million in gross proceeds, including a $200 million common stock Private Investment in Public Equity (PIPE) committed at $10 per share. Upon the closing of the merger later this year, the combined company will operate as Agility and list on the NASDAQ under the ticker symbol "AGLT".

Agility's public debut represents a milestone for physical AI, as it is positioned to become the first U.S.-listed, pure-play humanoid robotics company with active commercial deployments. Backed by strategic heavyweights including Amazon, Nvidia, SoftBank Vision Fund 2, and Foxconn, the company plans to use the capital to scale manufacturing of its flagship bipedal robot, Digit. Digit is currently undergoing pilot deployments with Amazon and GXO Logistics, performing tasks such as tote movement in logistics warehouses.

The announcement has triggered a wave of debate in the robotics community. On Reddit’s r/robotics, critics are debating the bipedal design of Digit compared to wheeled or quadrupedal platforms. While bipedal locomotion allows robots to navigate human-centric spaces like stairs and platforms without retrofitting infrastructure, it introduces massive engineering complexity in power consumption, actuator durability, and balance control. Additionally, financial analysts have raised concerns about the choice of a SPAC vehicle, which historically has been associated with speculative pre-revenue companies. Agility's supporters counter that the high capital requirements of scaling hardware require rapid access to public markets to expand its "RoboFab" manufacturing facility. Official details can be accessed via [Agility Robotics](https://www.agilityrobotics.com) and [The Robot Report](https://www.therobotreport.com).

### **Waymo and Uber Dissolve Self-Driving Ride-Hailing Alliance in Phoenix**
[Read More](news/The_Phoenix_Decoupling_Waymo’s_First-Party_Bet_and_Uber’s_Race_to_Build_the_AV_Middleware_Layer.md)
On June 29, 2026, Uber and Alphabet's Waymo announced the termination of their autonomous ride-hailing partnership in Phoenix, Arizona. While Waymo vehicles will remain bookable on the Uber platform in Austin and Atlanta, they will be removed from the Uber app in Phoenix. In response, Uber is shifting its Phoenix autonomous vehicle (AV) strategy to new partnerships, including collaborations with Lucid Motors and robotics firm Nuro.

The split in Phoenix represents a significant strategic shift in the robotaxi market. Phoenix has served as the primary commercial testing ground for public autonomous rides, and the dissolution of the partnership highlights the growing competition between vertical fleet operators and horizontal ride-hailing platforms. On X and Reddit, industry commentators note that Waymo is increasingly prioritizing its proprietary Waymo One application. By driving users to its own app, Waymo can capture 100% of passenger fares and build direct brand loyalty, bypassing Uber's platform fees. 

Uber, conversely, is seeking to build an open platform architecture that avoids dependency on a single dominant AV provider. This allows Uber to onboard multiple hardware partners, using its ride-dispatch algorithms to match passengers with the most efficient vehicle available. The split has divided users online, with many pointing out that booking directly through Waymo One offers a more streamlined experience, while others complain about the fragmentation of the user experience. The operational impacts on fleet utilization and the development of open AV APIs remain key areas of concern.

### **California Enacts SB 1246 to Allow Direct Traffic Citations for Autonomous Vehicles**
California Senate Bill 1246 (SB 1246), authored by Senator Dave Cortese, passed the Assembly Committee on Communications and Conveyance on July 1, 2026. The bill, which has now advanced to the Assembly Appropriations Committee, aims to establish much stricter oversight, accountability, and transparency standards for autonomous vehicle (AV) operations in California. 

The primary regulatory gap addressed by SB 1246 is the enforcement of traffic laws on driverless cars. Previously, because there was no human driver behind the wheel, police officers could not issue moving violations or add points to a license when a robotaxi committed an infraction. The new legislation creates a formal mechanism allowing law enforcement to issue "notices of autonomous vehicle noncompliance" directly to the manufacturer, imposing financial and regulatory penalties. 

Additionally, SB 1246 introduces strict operational mandates: remote operators must be located within the United States, hold a valid California driver’s license, and operate under a maximum staffing ratio of one remote assistant for every five passenger vehicles. The bill also requires manufacturers to install two-way voice communication devices in all vehicles for emergency responders and maintain a dedicated emergency hotline. While labor unions and first responder groups have championed the bill as a necessary safety measure, AV developers oppose the rules, arguing that rigid staffing ratios and localized licensing requirements will stifle technological innovation. The text of the bill is available on the [California Legislative Information](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov) website.

### **Roche's Next-Gen KRAS G12C Inhibitor Divarasib Shows Phase 3 Superiority in Lung Cancer**
On July 2, 2026, Swiss pharmaceutical giant Roche announced positive Phase 3 clinical trial results for divarasib, its next-generation KRAS G12C inhibitor. The trial demonstrated superior progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) compared to existing approved KRAS G12C inhibitors, such as Amgen's sotorasib and Mirati/Bristol Myers Squibb's adagrasib, in patients with previously treated KRAS G12C-mutant non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC).

The KRAS G12C mutation is a common oncogenic driver in lung, colorectal, and pancreatic cancers, long considered "undruggable" due to the protein's lack of accessible binding pockets. First-generation inhibitors successfully bound to the inactive state of the protein, but patients frequently developed resistance, and the treatments carried significant gastrointestinal and hepatic toxicities. Roche's divarasib is engineered to be highly selective and potent, featuring a higher binding affinity that ensures longer target engagement and fewer off-target interactions.

On oncology forums and social media, medical researchers have welcomed the data. The trial's results suggest that divarasib's improved potency translates into a longer duration of response without a corresponding increase in liver enzyme elevations. However, some researchers caution that further study is needed to monitor for the emergence of secondary resistance mutations. Roche has announced plans to submit the data to global regulatory authorities for approval as a first-line treatment. For more details on the clinical data, visit the [Roche](https://www.roche.com) media center.

### **Zhipu AI's Open-Weight GLM-5.2 Achieves 62.1 Score on SWE-bench Pro**
Beijing-based artificial intelligence lab Zhipu AI (also operating as Z.ai) has released GLM-5.2, its latest open-weight model, under an MIT license. The model has drawn significant attention after achieving a score of 62.1 on SWE-bench Pro, a benchmark designed to evaluate an AI model’s ability to resolve real-world software engineering issues in complex code repositories.

GLM-5.2 utilizes a mixture-of-experts (MoE) architecture, activating only a subset of parameters for each token to reduce computation costs. The model was fine-tuned with specialized instruction datasets targeting multi-turn code generation, debugging, and tool integration. This architecture allows it to handle complex programming tasks at a fraction of the inference cost of larger proprietary models like GPT-4o or Claude 3.5 Sonnet.

On Reddit's r/MachineLearning, developers have expressed surprise at the model's performance, noting that an open-weight model is now competitive with the industry's leading closed-source models in software engineering. The release has also fueled a broader debate regarding global AI competitiveness. With Meituan recently open-sourcing its 1.6-trillion-parameter LongCat-2.0 model—reportedly trained entirely on domestic Chinese silicon—industry analysts point out that despite strict hardware export controls, developers outside the U.S. are making rapid progress by optimizing model architectures and utilizing open-source frameworks.

# Apple Eyes AI Chip Startups, Walden Robotics Launches at $1.1B, and Stripe Unveils Agentic Commerce Benchmark

### **Walden Robotics Emerges from Stealth with $300 Million Round for Wheeled Physical AI**
[Read More](news/Untitled_18.md)
The race for humanoid robotics supremacy took a decisive, pragmatic turn as Walden Robotics officially launched from stealth, announcing a massive $300 million funding round at a $1.1 billion valuation. Spun out of the Toyota Research Institute (TRI) and led by MIT professor Dr. Russ Tedrake, the company has immediately positioned itself as a major contender in the industrial automation landscape. The funding round was co-led by Toyota (including Toyota Motor Corp, Toyota Invention Partners, and Toyota Ventures) and Deviation Capital, with participation from NVIDIA, Boeing, AE Ventures, Samsung Ventures, Prologis Ventures, and CoreWeave Ventures. Walden's emergence has intensified the industry-wide debate between bipedal and wheeled humanoid designs. Bipedal advocates, such as developers of Tesla’s Optimus and Figure, argue that walking limbs are essential for robots to navigate environments built strictly for humans, including stairs and uneven platforms. However, Dr. Russ Tedrake has pushed back, stating that "wheels are safer, more stable, and highly practical" for immediate factory and warehouse applications. Wheeled robots allow for greater weight tolerance, accommodating larger batteries and the heavy onboard compute hardware required for real-time edge processing. Technically, Walden's systems utilize "Large Behavior Models" (LBMs), a class of AI designed to translate raw sensory inputs directly to motor control and trajectory planning, bypassing hand-coded heuristics. This model is already being validated in the real world: Walden has had a pilot deployment running at a Toyota factory in North America, where robots have been loading, unloading, and kitting automotive parts.

### **Apple Explores Semiconductor Acquisitions to Salvage Delayed 'Baltra' AI Server Silicon**
[Read More](news/Silicon_Bailout_Inside_Apple’s_Hunt_for_Semiconductor_Startups_to_Rescue_Delayed_Baltra_AI_Server_Chip.md)
Apple is actively exploring the acquisition of several semiconductor startups, marking a significant departure from its historical strategy of relying on organic growth and small technology acquisitions. According to reports from the industry, Apple is holding preliminary discussions with investment bankers to identify chip startups that can bolster its AI server infrastructure. This urgency stems from developmental delays in Apple's custom AI server chip, codenamed "Baltra," which has reportedly been pushed past its original 2026 release schedule. The delay has created a performance bottleneck for Apple's private cloud initiative. Currently, Apple relies on M2 Ultra chips for its cloud servers, but these chips have struggled to handle the high computing demands of advanced generative models. Consequently, Apple has had to run the new Siri features powered by Google's Gemini models on Nvidia GPUs hosted in Google Cloud, compromising its ideal architecture of fully private, end-to-end cloud processing. An acquisition of specialized semiconductor talent and technology is seen as a way to salvage the Baltra timeline. Apple is reportedly targeting startups with expertise in advanced packaging, high-bandwidth memory (HBM) integration, and model-shrinking techniques like quantization and pruning. The push comes on the heels of Apple's earlier $2 billion acquisition of Israeli AI startup Q.ai and its $30 billion custom silicon partnership with Broadcom, highlighting the massive capital Apple is deploying to build out a competitive AI hardware stack.

### **Stripe Open-Sources Agentic Integration Benchmark, Highlighting Crucial Validation and Recovery Gaps**
[Read More](news/Untitled_19.md)
Stripe has open-sourced a comprehensive integration benchmark designed to test how effectively autonomous AI agents can build and manage financial integrations. The results of the initial evaluations show that while modern LLMs excel at generating clean code, they struggle with the complex operational lifecycles required for production environments. Specifically, the benchmark identified critical failure modes in validation reasoning and state management. In multi-step browser-based checkout flows, agents frequently lose track of their state when tool executions disrupt the browser window, and they struggle to recover from these interruptions. Moreover, the benchmark highlighted a major flaw in validation: agents repeatedly misinterpret API error responses, such as observing an HTTP 400 Bad Request error and concluding that the integration was successful. Furthermore, agents often omit essential production safeguards, including idempotency keys, retry logic, and handling authorization token scopes. This benchmark is part of Stripe's broader push into agentic commerce, which includes the Machine Payments Protocol (MPP) co-authored with Tempo to enable automated microtransactions, and the Stripe Directory, a searchable registry that provides machine-readable API endpoints so that AI agents can discover and transact with services without navigating fragile browser-based forms.

### **Internet Pioneer Vint Cerf Proposes DNSid Registry to Govern and Authenticate Autonomous AI Agents**
Vint Cerf, the co-designer of TCP/IP and a foundational figure in internet history, has joined Innovation Labs—a subsidiary of Identity Digital—to develop an open architecture for identifying and auditing autonomous AI agents. Following his retirement from Google, Cerf has focused on the challenges posed by autonomous systems traversing the open web. Cerf has warned that relying on natural language for agent-to-agent and agent-to-human communication is "ambiguous and potentially terrifying," calling instead for formal, vendor-neutral protocols. The proposed framework, known as DNSid, seeks to link AI agents to existing internet domain names using cryptographic proofs. This would provide a standardized, readable way to verify an agent's authority, log its interactions, and audit its actions in the wild. Cerf's proposal comes amid concerns that the agentic web could become a chaotic landscape of closed, proprietary systems. By advocating for an open registry built on top of the domain name system, Cerf hopes to mirror the interoperability and governance principles that enabled the early internet to scale, ensuring that autonomous agents can transact safely and transparently.

### **Kelun-Biotech's Phase III Trial for Sac-TMT and Pembrolizumab Hits Primary Endpoint in PD-L1-Negative Lung Cancer**
Kelun-Biotech announced that its Phase III OptiTROP-Lung06 trial (NCT06711900) met its primary endpoint of progression-free survival (PFS) in patients with first-line, driver-gene-negative, and PD-L1-negative locally advanced or metastatic non-squamous non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). The trial evaluated the combination of sacituzumab tirumotecan (sac-TMT)—an antibody-drug conjugate (ADC) targeting TROP2, also known as SKB264 or MK-2870—with the immune checkpoint inhibitor pembrolizumab (Keytruda), comparing it against the current standard of care (pemetrexed and platinum-based chemotherapy combined with pembrolizumab). The sac-TMT and pembrolizumab combination showed a statistically significant and clinically meaningful improvement in PFS, alongside a positive trend in overall survival (OS). This achievement represents the world's first Phase III clinical study of an ADC combined with an immunotherapy to meet its primary endpoint in the first-line treatment of driver-gene-negative and PD-L1-negative non-squamous NSCLC. The positive readout builds on the success of the OptiTROP-Lung05 trial for PD-L1-positive patients, validating the combination as a potential broad first-line treatment strategy across varying PD-L1 expression levels. Kelun-Biotech plans to submit the data to the Center for Drug Evaluation of China's National Medical Products Administration (NMPA) for regulatory review.

### **Waabi Demonstrates Zero-Shot Generalization by Transferring Autonomous Truck Driver to Volvo VNL Platform**
[Read More](news/The_Zero-Shot_Leap_How_Waabi_and_Volvo_Just_Upended_the_Physics_of_Autonomous_Trucking_Validation.md)
Waabi, in partnership with Volvo Autonomous Solutions (VAS), has demonstrated a breakthrough in autonomous vehicle scalability by achieving "zero-shot" generalization of its autonomous truck driving software. In a series of tests, the companies successfully transferred the "Waabi Driver" software from its original vehicle platform (a Peterbilt 579) to a completely different Volvo VNL Autonomous truck. The software adapted immediately, operating on highways and complex surface streets from the very first mile without requiring any new real-world data collection, simulator calibration, model fine-tuning, or custom engineering work. Transitioning autonomous software between different truck models has historically been a labor-intensive, costly process due to variations in sensor placement, vehicle dimensions, weight distributions, and actuator control systems. Waabi's ability to execute a zero-shot transfer points to the robustness of its simulation-first training approach, where high-fidelity simulators allow the AI to learn generalized representations of physical dynamics. This milestone is a key validation of the partnership between Waabi and Volvo, supporting the commercial roadmap for Volvo Autonomous Solutions to launch fully driverless highway freight operations in the United States by Q1 2027.

### **NVIDIA Expands Blackwell-Powered Jetson Thor Platform with High-Performance T3000 and T2000 Modules**
NVIDIA has expanded its Blackwell-powered Jetson Thor robotics and edge AI platform with the introduction of the new T3000 and T2000 compute modules, designed to bring server-class physical AI capabilities to mass-market robotics. The high-performance T3000 module delivers up to 865 TFLOPs of FP4 AI compute, specifically targeting complex humanoid systems and industrial automation, while the T2000 module provides 400 TFLOPs for broader edge AI applications. These modules sit alongside the flagship T5000 module, which boasts 2,070 TFLOPs of performance. Built on the Blackwell GPU architecture and featuring a 14-core Arm Neoverse V3AE CPU, the Jetson Thor platform offers up to 7.5x the AI performance and 3.5x the energy efficiency of the previous-generation Jetson AGX Orin. In addition to the hardware release, NVIDIA introduced new software memory optimizations and Jetson agent skills, allowing developers to execute heavy vision-language-action (VLA) models on more compact, lower-memory hardware configurations. This expansion aims to democratize the deployment of physical AI across industries, with companies like Boston Dynamics, 1X, and Amazon Robotics adopting the platform for advanced real-time perception and decision-making on edge devices.
